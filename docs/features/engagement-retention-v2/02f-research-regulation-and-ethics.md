# Regulation, Platform Policy and Dark-Pattern Evidence (status 2026-09)

As of September 2026 the engagement-design space is constrained by four different kinds of instrument, and the plugin currently conflates them. Binding statute exists in Korea, Brazil, Japan, China and (contingently) Texas. Rating consequences — the newest and most actionable layer — arrive through PEGI's June 2026 interactive risk categories and Australia's classification guidelines. Everything the EU has published on addictive design is still guidance or preliminary findings. And a large part of what the skills forbid is defensible only as evidence-of-harm or as a stated preference. Verification corrected five load-bearing legal claims in the research set, including the contents of Korea's Art. 33-2, the status of three US state app-store laws, and Japan's surcharge threshold; the corrected text is used below and marked.

### 1.1 European Union — guidance with enforcement momentum, not statute

The Digital Fairness Act is **not law and has no text**. Consultation ran 17 Jul–24 Oct 2025; the Commission's indicative proposal timing is Q4 2026; the European Parliamentary Research Service still describes it as "upcoming" and says it "may introduce stricter rules, potentially including obligations to switch off manipulative features" (EPRS, 2026, https://epthinktank.eu/2026/05/06/addictive-design-on-online-platforms/). Loot boxes and games are not singled out in either the EPRS brief or the DFA tracker. Any skill text that treats DFA obligations as imminent compliance requirements is wrong.

The Art. 28(1) DSA guidelines (European Commission, 14 Jul 2025, https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors) are the operative EU document for engagement design, and they name mechanics directly. Verbatim: "Disabling by default features that contribute to excessive use, like communication 'streaks,' ephemeral content, 'read receipts,' autoplay, or push notifications, as well as removing persuasive design features aimed predominantly at engagement." They also require that "children's lack of commercial literacy is not exploited," naming "certain virtual currencies or loot-boxes," and that recommenders "prioritise explicit signals from children over behavioural signals." Legal status is explicit both ways: "Following these guidelines is voluntary and does not automatically guarantee compliance," but the Commission uses them "to assess compliance with Article 28(1)" and they "may inform national regulators." Scope is all online platforms accessible to minors, not only VLOPs.

The TikTok preliminary findings (Commission, 6 Feb 2026, https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/news/commission-preliminarily-finds-tiktoks-addictive-design-breach-digital-services-act) matter because they articulate a regulator's harm model for variable reward: "by constantly 'rewarding' users with new content, certain design features of TikTok fuel the urge to keep scrolling," which "may lead to compulsive behaviour and reduce users' self-control." EPRS frames it as the first enforcement action aimed at "the harmful architecture of the platform itself" rather than content, and the DSA Observatory reports the Commission's Arts. 34–35 reasoning that "generic or user-side tools… are insufficient where the risk is embedded in the very configuration of the service." **[contested]** — findings are preliminary and do not prejudge the outcome; TikTok called them "categorically false"; the parallel Meta investigation opened in 2024 is unresolved.

The CPC Network's "Key Principles on In-Game Virtual Currencies" (21 Mar 2025, https://commission.europa.eu/live-work-travel-eu/consumer-rights-and-complaints/enforcement-consumer-protection/coordinated-actions/social-media-online-games-and-search-engines_en) demand that in-game purchases be expressed in real-world money, that bundle denominations not force purchase of unused currency, that withdrawal rights be respected, and that children's vulnerabilities be protected. The coordinated action against Star Stable Entertainment AB alleges direct exhortation to children in advertising, "pressuring techniques" including time-limited offers, and unclear virtual-currency information. It followed BEUC's 12 Sept 2024 complaint by 22 consumer groups against seven companies, citing average child spend of €39/month (https://www.beuc.eu/press-releases/consumer-groups-denounce-video-games-manipulative-spending-tactics). **[contested]**: Video Games Europe and EGDF call the principles "misinterpretations of EU consumer law," and no public outcome of the Star Stable action was found as of 5 Sept 2026.

*Skill implication:* cite the Art. 28 guidelines by date as the EU rule of thumb, cite TikTok as a harm model with the "preliminary" caveat attached, and never present the DFA as a compliance obligation.

### 1.2 Korea — the only jurisdiction with statutory odds liability

**Art. 33(2) 게임산업진흥에 관한 법률** has required per-item probability disclosure since 22 March 2024 — in-game, on the website and in advertising, including ceiling/pity counts and advance notice of changes; ignoring an MCST corrective order carries up to two years' imprisonment or a KRW 20m criminal fine under Art. 45(11) (Kim & Chang, 2024, https://www.kimchang.com/en/insights/detail.kc?idx=29487&sch_section=4). Compliance is partial and monitored. A peer-reviewed content analysis of the **100 highest-grossing iPhone games in Korea** found 90 contained paid loot boxes and only **84.4% of those disclosed probabilities**, with accessibility and visual prominence often poor (Xiao & Park, *Acta Psychologica*, 2025, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0001691825008030). Regulator tallies reported in the Korean business press: **338 companies and 2,181 violations detected March 2024–September 2025**, rising 1,022 (2024) → 1,159 (2025), with roughly 51% being non-display of probabilities or of individual-item probabilities, against an administrative fine capped at KRW 10m (Dealsite, https://dealsite.co.kr/articles/156564). GRAC's first-period monitoring covered 1,255 games between 22 Mar and 8 Oct 2024 and logged 544 violation cases, 65.4% from foreign companies.

**Art. 33-2 (litigation special provision), in force 1 Aug 2025 — [corrected].** The statute has exactly five paragraphs and applies only to display-duty violations occurring on or after 1 Aug 2025. (1) The operator is liable unless it proves absence of both intent and negligence — a reversed burden. (2) Where damage is established but the amount is very hard to prove, the court may fix a reasonable amount from the whole record — a damages-estimation rule mirroring 민사소송법 제202조의2, **not** a discovery or document-production power; the original research claim's "court document-production orders" element does not exist and must be deleted. (3) Where the violation is found intentional, the court **may** set an award **not exceeding 3× the amount recognised under paragraph 2** — a discretionary cap on the total award, not a punitive add-on on top of compensatory damages. (4) Six mandatory factors: degree of intent or awareness of risk; scale of user harm; economic benefit obtained; duration and number of violations; fines and surcharges already imposed; and the operator's remedial efforts. (5) A statutory basis for a report-and-relief centre (법무법인 화우 via 법률신문, 2025, https://www.lawtimes.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=210245; 김·장, https://www.kimchang.com/ko/insights/detail.kc?idx=31242; 율촌 via Lexology, https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d7cea681-75a1-4996-8af2-7120ff61f45f). No reported case has yet cited Art. 33-2, so the 3× exposure is untested.

That relief centre is now real and is the channel a Korean-market designer will actually meet. GRAC opened the **확률형 아이템 피해구제센터** in Busan on 27 Feb 2026 under a new 이용자보호본부 (~20 staff at launch, ~40 by June 2026); by 22 May 2026 it had taken 609 consultations and 11 formal relief filings, with a 피해구제분과위원회 first convened 28 Apr 2026 and referral linkage to the Content Dispute Mediation Committee. Enforcement is mediation-led, not punitive.

**KFTC under 전자상거래법 is the live deterrent.** Nexon's **KRW 11.642bn** fine (Jan 2024, the largest ever under that Act) covered lowering Cube option probabilities — including to 0% from 2011 — and cutting Black Cube tier-up odds from 1.8% to 1.0% without notice across 2010–2021, against KRW 547bn of related sales. On 2 Sept 2026 the Seoul High Court cancelled the scheduled judgment and reopened arguments for the fourth time, with a new hearing at 16:00 on **28 Oct 2026** (매일경제 via Daum, https://v.daum.net/v/Zrq1oxpXvd). The decisive question is whether non-disclosure *before* the 2024 statutory duty is "deception" under Art. 21. **[contested]**: Nexon argues omission is not deception and points out that Cube sales *rose 46%* after it voluntarily published odds on 5 Mar 2021 — evidence that disclosure alone is a weak spending brake. Webzen was fined **KRW 158m** on 28 Nov 2025 for a hidden floor where rare-item odds were 0% until 51–150 purchases while 0.25–1.16% was displayed, over 2020-06-27 to 2024-03-21; only 860 of 20,226 affected consumers (4.3%) were compensated (게임메카, https://www.gamemeca.com/view.php?gid=1768689). Gravity, Wemade, Krafton and Com2uS took corrective orders plus KRW 2.5m fines in Apr–Jun 2025.

**Complete-gacha ban — [corrected] PENDING, NOT LAW.** Bill 2212569, 「게임산업진흥에 관한 법률 일부개정법률안」, was proposed 1 Sept 2025 by Democratic Party floor leader **김병기 등 13인** (13 signatories in total, i.e. Kim plus 12 — not 14). It adds Art. 28(1) 2-3 (undisclosed odds manipulation) and 2-4 (컴플리트 가챠: collecting loot-box items to complete a set and thereby obtain a new item), with a criminal penalty under Art. 45; new Art. 33(4)–(5) giving MCST power to demand server logs and inspect premises; a revised Art. 2(11) definition; and Art. 34-2 disclosure and refund duties for paid content. It was referred to committee 2 Sept 2025, tabled 17 Nov 2025, and remains at 위원회 심사 with no subcommittee approval, no Legislation & Judiciary passage and no plenary vote as of 5 Sept 2026 (National Assembly record, https://likms.assembly.go.kr/bill/billDetail.do?billId=PRC_G2E5E0M4L2L2J1K6I2J3R0R0Q3O4P6; 게임메카, https://www.gamemeca.com/view.php?gid=1765621). The "widely expected to pass" framing was a September-2025 industry read based on a presidential campaign pledge and floor-leader sponsorship; a year later Korean game legislation has broadly stalled, and 21st-Assembly precedent shows the 문체위 expert opinion required 실태조사 before an outright ban and MCST gave a 신중 검토 opinion.

**Adjacent Korean rules the skills keep missing.** 전자상거래법 has banned six dark-pattern types since **14 Feb 2025** — 숨은갱신 (explicit consent within the 30 days before a free→paid conversion or price rise), 순차공개 가격책정, pre-selected options, false visual hierarchy, cancellation/withdrawal obstruction, and **반복간섭** (re-asking a settled decision two or more times is unlawful unless the prompt offers a ≥7-day "do not ask again"); the KFTC interpretive guideline took effect 24 Oct 2025 and the first fines landed 15 Oct 2025 against Coupang, Wavve, NHN Bugs and Spotify (~KRW 9.5m total, against a KRW 1–5m per-case cap) (공정거래위원회, https://www.ftc.go.kr/www/selectBbsNttView.do?key=12&bordCd=3&nttSn=46527; policy briefing https://www.korea.kr/news/reporterView.do?newsId=148967630). KISA's 7th-edition 정보통신망법 anti-spam guide (posted 4 Mar 2026) bars vague consent wording such as "혜택 알림," bars login or complex steps for opting out of app-push ads, and requires explicit prior consent before sending issuance-or-expiry notices for unilaterally granted coupons, mileage or points; standing §50 rules require separate consent for 21:00–08:00 sends, a "(광고)" prefix, biennial consent re-confirmation and fines up to KRW 30m (https://www.kisa.or.kr/401/form?postSeq=3608). Art. 12-3 requires online game providers to display elapsed play time and the warning "과도한 게임이용은 정상적인 일상생활에 지장을 줄 수 있습니다." on screen every hour for at least three seconds (https://easylaw.go.kr/CSP/CnpClsMainBtr.laf?popMenu=ov&csmSeq=718&ccfNo=2&cciNo=2&cnpClsNo=5). The forced shutdown curfew was **repealed effective 1 Jan 2022** — no skill may cite it, and there is no "Prevent Game Addiction Act."

**AI Basic Act — [corrected].** The 인공지능 발전과 신뢰 기반 조성 등에 관한 기본법 and its decree took effect 22 Jan 2026 (now cite the amended version, 시행 2026. 7. 21., 법률 제21311호). Art. 31(1) requires advance notice that a product runs on high-impact or generative AI. Art. 31(2)–(3) require labelling of generative outputs, with human-perceptible labelling the **only** permissible method for deepfakes. Two corrections matter: the **KRW 30m fine attaches to the advance-notice duty, the domestic-representative duty and non-compliance with a corrective order — not to the labelling duty**, which draws a corrective order first; and **machine-readable watermarking alone is insufficient** — where it is used, the operator must additionally provide at least one text or audio notice that the output was AI-generated (Cooley, 2026, https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways; MSIT via korea.kr, https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148958380; Kim & Chang, https://www.kimchang.com/en/insights/detail.kc?idx=34018&sch_section=4). A guidance period of at least one year from 22 Jan 2026 limits investigations to exceptional serious-harm cases. Pending, not law: the 우리아이 AI 안심 패키지법 (조인철, introduced 23 Jun 2026) would add guardian consent, usage-time restriction on guardian request and mandatory self-harm detection for conversational AI (ZDNet Korea, https://zdnet.co.kr/view/?no=20260623095806), and separate 정보통신망법 bills would default-off personalised recommendation and "이용유도" features on minors' accounts.

*Skill implication:* Korea's regime is **disclosure-plus-liability, not prohibition**. The design rules that follow are: per-item percentages at the purchase, inquiry and use surfaces; floors/pity disclosed as conditions, never averaged away; **announce every odds change before it takes effect and keep the change log** (silent nerfs are the fact pattern in every KFTC case); no dynamic or personalised drop rates; and a separate Korean checklist for push, subscription UI and hourly play-time notices.

### 1.3 United States — orders, not statutes, define the gacha standard

**FTC v. Cognosphere/HoYoverse** (17 Jan 2025, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/genshin-impact-game-developer-will-be-banned-selling-lootboxes-teens-under-16-without-parental) is the closest thing to a US design standard for random items: $20m plus an order banning loot-box purchases by under-16s without a parent's affirmative express consent; prohibiting sale of loot boxes for virtual currency **unless players can also buy them directly with real money**; requiring disclosure of odds and of exchange rates for multi-tiered virtual currency; and requiring deletion of under-13 data collected without parental consent. The complaint theory targeted currencies in unusual denominations requiring repeated re-exchange, and promotions implying better five-star odds than actual.

**FTC v. Epic** remains the price tag on interface dark patterns: $245m total, with **$72m paid to 629,344 consumers (Dec 2024)** and **$126m to 969,173 consumers (June 2025)**, and a further round expected in 2026 after claim validation (https://www.ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds/fortnite-refunds). The conduct was "counterintuitive, inconsistent and confusing button configuration," children's charges without parental consent (Jan 2017–Nov 2018) and locking accounts of users who disputed charges. The order requires explicit consent before charging and bars dispute lockouts.

The **FTC 6(b) companion-chatbot inquiry** (11 Sept 2025, 3-0 vote, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions) issued orders to Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and X.AI asking how they "monetize user engagement," "develop and approve characters," and "measure, test, and monitor for negative impacts before and after deployment." No report has been published.

**California SB 243** (Ch. 677, signed 13 Oct 2025; core duties 1 Jan 2026; reporting from 1 Jul 2027, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billNavClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB243) requires companion-chatbot operators to disclose AI status, provide break reminders **at least every three hours** for minors in continuing interaction, block sexually explicit content for minors, publish a suicide/self-harm protocol, and creates a private right of action at the greater of actual damages or **$1,000 per violation**. Critically for games, it **excludes video-game characters whose dialogue is limited to the game and cannot discuss mental health, self-harm or sexual content** — that exclusion is a design boundary, not a general safe harbour. **New York GBL Art. 47** (in force 5 Nov 2025, https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/GBS/1700) defines an "AI companion" by three prongs: retaining prior-session information "to personalize the interaction and facilitate ongoing engagement"; asking "unprompted or unsolicited emotion-based questions"; and "sustaining an ongoing dialogue concerning matters personal to the user." Duties: a not-human notice at session start and every three hours, plus self-harm detection and referral, with AG penalties up to $15,000/day.

**App-store age laws — [corrected], and the previous research claim carried two false statuses.** Texas SB 2420's statutory effective date was 1 Jan 2026; it was preliminarily enjoined on 23 Dec 2025 (W.D. Tex.); the Fifth Circuit administratively stayed that injunction on **28 May 2026** and granted a stay pending appeal on **4 June 2026**; the Supreme Court denied both applications to vacate the stay on **6 July 2026** in one-sentence shadow-docket orders with no noted dissents (https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25a1390.html); merits argument was heard 4 Aug 2026 with no ruling as of early September. Apple's own compliance go-live for **new** Texas Apple Accounts was 4 June 2026 — that is Apple's date, not the law's (https://developer.apple.com/news?id=sg176nne). **Louisiana is not 1 Jul 2026**: HB 977, signed 15 May 2026 as Act 185, declared the 2025 act not effective and re-set it to **1 July 2027**. **Utah is not "enjoined pending appeal"**: HB 498 (signed 18 Mar 2026) moved obligations to **6 May 2027**, removed AG enforcement leaving only a private right of action, and the industry challenge (CCIA v. Brown) was voluntarily dismissed 21 Apr 2026 for want of a state enforcer (Alston & Bird, https://www.alstonprivacy.com/louisiana-delays-app-store-accountability-effective-date-to-july-2027/). Alabama HB 161 (signed 17 Feb 2026) takes effect **1 Jan 2027** for new accounts with existing accounts to be verified by 1 Oct 2027. Net: **Texas is the only App Store Accountability Act in force today, on an interlocutory stay that could still be reversed.**

*Skill implication:* encode the HoYoverse order as the default gacha spec (fiat path, single-hop currency with displayed exchange rate, odds before purchase, under-16 consent gate); state that purchase confirmation steps are FTC-tested requirements, not friction to optimise away; and for the US age layer say only "Texas since 28 May 2026, on appeal; Alabama Jan 2027, Utah May 2027, Louisiana July 2027."

### 1.4 Brazil, Australia, United Kingdom

**Brazil is the strictest instrument in force anywhere.** Lei 15.211/2025 (ECA Digital), effective **17 March 2026**, Art. 20: "São vedadas as caixas de recompensa (loot boxes) oferecidas em jogos eletrônicos direcionados a crianças e a adolescentes **ou de acesso provável por eles**, nos termos da respectiva classificação indicativa" (https://www.planalto.gov.br/ccivil_03/_ato2023-2026/2025/lei/L15211.htm). The definition covers any paid feature granting random consumable items or advantages without prior knowledge of content. Sanctions run to 10% of the economic group's Brazilian revenue, capped at **R$50m per infraction**, escalating to suspension; foreign providers must keep a legal representative in Brazil. This is a **ban, not a disclosure duty**, and the trigger is the game's age rating plus a "likely accessed by" test that is broader than "targeted at."

**Australia** has two live regimes. Since 22 Sept 2024, computer games with in-game purchases linked to chance receive a mandatory minimum **M** classification (advisory), and games with simulated gambling such as social casino are legally restricted to **R 18+** (https://minister.infrastructure.gov.au/rowland/media-release/new-mandatory-minimum-classifications-gambling-games-content). The Social Media Minimum Age scheme (from 10–11 Dec 2025, penalties to A$49.5m) **explicitly excludes online gaming**, messaging, education and health services (https://www.infrastructure.gov.au/media-communications/internet/online-safety/social-media-minimum-age) — press framing that Roblox-style games are covered is wrong.

**UK self-regulation has measurably failed.** A May 2025 *Royal Society Open Science* audit of the **100 highest-grossing iPhone games** found **0% sought parental consent** before under-18 loot-box purchases, only **8.6%** gave consistent probability disclosures, and **23.5%** disclosed loot boxes in marketing; all non-compliant games remained available despite Ukie's 2023 promise of "severe fines" and delisting (Xiao et al. via TechXplore, https://techxplore.com/news/2025-05-uk-loot-rampant-compliance.html). The teeth are elsewhere: the CMA gained direct consumer-enforcement powers on 6 April 2025 under the DMCC Act, with fines to 10% of global turnover and false urgency and harmful online choice architecture named as targets; Ofcom's Protection of Children Codes apply from **25 July 2025** to services likely accessed by UK children, which includes games with chat or UGC, with risk assessments due 24 July 2025 and 50 measures including recommender controls (https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/new-rules-for-a-safer-generation-of-children-online); and DCMS published a skins-gambling evidence review on 25 Sept 2025.

*Skill implication:* Brazil converts age rating into a monetisation gate — a minor-accessible rating and paid random items are mutually exclusive there. Australia and PEGI do the same in softer form. Treat the UK as an enforcement jurisdiction (CMA false urgency, Ofcom child codes), not a self-regulation one.

### 1.5 Japan and China — [corrected] on both

**Japan has no gacha statute and had no 2025 gacha rule.** Complete gacha is prohibited as "card matching" (カード合わせ) under Item 5 of the 懸賞景品制限告示 issued under 景品表示法; the Consumer Affairs Agency established this in guidance dated **18 May 2012**, enforcement from 1 Jul 2012, and the ban is **value-independent** — "景品類の最高額や総額にかかわらず" (https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/representation/fair_labeling/guideline/pdf/120518premiums_1.pdf; live FAQ, https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/policy/representation/fair_labeling/faq/card/). Since **1 Oct 2024** the amended 景表法 carries a direct criminal fine of up to **¥1,000,000** for 優良誤認/有利誤認 without a prior administrative order — but it is an **intent crime**, covers only Art. 5 nos. 1–2, and has no reported prosecution to date, so describe it as available-but-unexercised. **[corrected] surcharge threshold**: surcharges are 3% of covered sales and no order issues where the **computed surcharge** falls below ¥1.5m — i.e. roughly ¥50m of relevant sales, not ¥1.5m of sales; the 2024 amendment also added a 1.5× uplift for repeat offenders within ten years and a 確約 commitment procedure (https://laws.e-gov.go.jp/law/337AC0000000134; https://corporate.vbest.jp/columns/9165/). **[corrected] JOGA**: the ランダム型アイテム提供方式 guideline was **revised 7 Jun 2024, effective 27 Jun 2024** — not a 2012/2016 document. Its quotable member duties: satisfy one of expected spend for any rare item ≤100× the per-pull price, or ≤¥50,000, or display upper and lower rate bounds, or display per-item rates; value floors; **no "blank" gacha**; no rate changes without prior notice; and audit by a department independent of gacha operations (https://japanonlinegame.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/JOGA_declaration_2024.pdf).

Two Japanese enforcement precedents are more design-relevant than the headline percentage. KOF '98 UM Online: 措置命令 26 Jan 2018 for displaying 3% where the actual rate was 0.333%, followed by a ¥6.09m surcharge on ¥203,029,813 of qualifying sales across a five-day window (https://www.caa.go.jp/notice/assets/representation_210329_01.pdf). gumi / Square Enix, 措置命令 28 Jun 2021: an anniversary 10-pull in *WAR OF THE VISIONS FFBE 幻影戦争* presented each of ten slots as an independent draw at published rates when the game actually drew from a restricted pre-set combination table, making most displayed combinations unobtainable (https://www.caa.go.jp/notice/assets/representation_210629_03.pdf). **The exposure sits in the mismatch between mechanic and display — multi-pull tables, pity weighting, pickup rules — not only in the headline number.** The claim that the CAA "finalized gacha probability regulations in mid-2025" is **unsupported** by the CAA's 2025 press-release index (https://www.caa.go.jp/notice/release/2025/); do not cite it.

**China — [corrected].** There is no 2025 loot-box rule. The NPPA's 22 Dec 2023 draft Online Game Administrative Measures — which would have banned random-draw services for minors, banned daily-login, first-recharge and consecutive-recharge inducement rewards, and required spending caps and direct-purchase alternatives — was **withdrawn 23 Jan 2024** with no successor. What binds is the State Council's 未成年人网络保护条例 (effective 1 Jan 2024, https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-10/24/c_1699806932316206.htm): Art. 42 prohibits offering minors products or services that induce addiction and requires an annual public anti-addiction report; Art. 43 mandates a "minors mode" limiting time slots, duration, functions and content with guardian time/permission/consumption controls; Art. 44 requires per-transaction and per-day spending limits by age band; Arts. 46–47 require real-name verification and age-appropriate prompts. The 2021 NPPA playtime limit (1 hour, 20:00–21:00, Fri/Sat/Sun/holidays) stands.

*Skill implication:* delete any "Japan 2025 gacha law" and any "China 2025 random-draw rule" text. Replace with: complete-gacha and set-completion tied to paid draws are illegal in Japan; displayed odds must match the actual mechanic including multi-pull tables; in China, "inducing addiction" is itself unlawful for minors, and the withdrawn draft remains the clearest regulator-intent signal against daily-login and consecutive-recharge rewards.

## 2. Platform and rating layer — where ethics preferences become rating consequences

**Store rules (universal, contractual).** Apple App Review Guideline 3.1.1, added in the 20–21 Dec 2017 guidelines update: "Apps offering 'loot boxes' or other mechanisms that provide randomized virtual items for purchase must disclose the odds of receiving each type of item to customers prior to purchase" (https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/). Google Play adopted an equivalent rule in May 2019 and its current Payments policy is **stricter** in one respect: odds must be disclosed "in advance of, **and in close and timely proximity to**, that purchase" (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738). Separately, Google Play's Better Ads Experiences policy (effective 30 Sept 2022) prohibits full-screen interstitials that appear unexpectedly — at the start of a level or content segment, before the splash screen, or after the user chose another action (https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en). Because these are contract terms enforced by delisting, they bind everywhere regardless of local law.

**Texas adds a store-asserted age and consent signal.** Under SB 2420 as implemented by Apple, developers must assign age ratings, consume the store's age-category and consent signals (Declared Age Range API, Significant Change API under PermissionKit, StoreKit age-rating property) and honour server notifications when a parent revokes consent. The load-bearing detail for live-ops: a "significant change" — which expressly includes **adding new monetization features** — re-triggers parental consent. Introducing a battle pass, a gacha banner or a new IAP tier is therefore a consent event for minors' accounts in Texas, not a silent content update.

**PEGI's interactive risk categories are the most concrete design-to-rating mapping that exists.** Announced 12 March 2026, applying to games **newly submitted from June 2026** (existing titles are not re-rated unless updated content warrants it), the exact tiers are (PEGI, http://www.pegi.info/index.php/news/pegi-expands-age-rating-criteria-interactive-risk-categories):

| Feature | Rating floor | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Daily quests rewarding return | PEGI 7 | "Play-by-appointment" |
| Punishing non-return | PEGI 12 | Losing content, reducing progress |
| Time- or quantity-limited offers | PEGI 12 | Purchases of in-game content |
| Paid random items | PEGI 16 | PEGI 18 in some cases |
| NFT / blockchain, unrestricted comms | PEGI 18 | — |

Read literally: **a login chain that resets accrued value on a missed day costs a European age tier**, and adding a limited-time bundle costs another. Lewis Silkin notes alignment with Germany's USK criteria applied since 2023. **[unverified]**: trade press reports an ESRB spokesperson saying ESRB will not adopt a similar change because "it could be confusing," but no ESRB primary statement was located — so treat the rating consequence as Europe-plus-Australia, with North American labels still content-neutral ("In-Game Purchases (Includes Random Items)").

*Skill implication:* move "reward return, never punish absence" out of the ethics section and into the **feasibility/rating constraints** section of retention-strategy-designer, with the PEGI tiers quoted. Treat per-item odds before purchase as a universal store requirement, not a jurisdictional one, and add Google's proximity-to-purchase wording. Add a Texas branch to any minors-facing monetisation flow: new monetisation features re-trigger consent.

## 3. Dark-pattern research 2024–2026

**Taxonomies, with the correct venues.** Hadan, Sgandurra, Zhang-Kennedy & Nacke analysed Overwatch's free-to-play transition and identified **nine game mechanics using deceptive design**, concluding that "compared to existing taxonomies in other media, games need a comprehensive classification of deceptive design" (CHI PLAY 2024, *Proc. ACM HCI* 8, Art. 309, doi:10.1145/3677074). Zhang, Wang, Nakajima & Seaborn audited **onboarding** in Chinese and Japanese F2P mobile games and built a three-level ontology extending Gray et al. (2024) and Zagal et al. (2013), introducing two operationally useful concepts: **"DP Combos"** (patterns deliberately deployed together) and **"DP Enhancers"** (non-manipulative elements that amplify a pattern's impact) — CHI PLAY 2025, *PACM HCI* 9(6) GAMES025, pp. 730–755, doi:10.1145/3748620, preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17512. Hardwick, Carter et al. studied how children experience and conceptualise harm in game monetisation ("They're Scamming Me," CHI 2025, doi:10.1145/3706598.3713611).

**Regulator-run empirical audits now exist and are quotable.** Japan's CAA/ICPRC hand-coded **102 Japanese sites** against the OECD taxonomy (Apr 2025): preselection 75 sites, false hierarchy 69, testimonials 56, forced registration 45, with a tail of sites stacking 10+ patterns at once (https://www.caa.go.jp/policies/future/icprc/research_010). A commissioned CAA behavioural study published 18 Jun 2026 ran an awareness survey (**n=1,200**, fielded 26 Dec 2025–4 Jan 2026) and a behavioural experiment on a fabricated video-subscription site (**n=1,600**, fielded 20–25 Feb 2026): 76.2% had seen a dark pattern, 37.5% experienced one in the past year, the dark-pattern arm chose the more expensive plan more often, and as cancellation obstruction escalated the share who actually cancelled fell. CAA flags the cancellation-obstruction leg as preliminary; treat it as directional.

**The harm evidence for loot boxes is narrower than the skills imply — [corrected].** Drummond, Hall, Lowe-Calverley, Garrett & Sauer (*R. Soc. Open Sci.* 12(7):231264, July 2025, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12303105/) reanalysed two datasets and report **divergent** results. In the Etchells et al. (2022) Western data (K10, extreme-distress cutoff 30+), normalising loot-box spend to disposable income surfaced an association the raw-spend null missed (BF10 = 6.11×10²⁵; ~8-point higher K10 as spend reaches parity with disposable income; RR = 2.02, p = 0.009 for 100% vs 0%). But **adding problem-gambling risk status as a control dropped it to RR = 1.67, p = 0.083 (non-significant)**, and the same normalisation did **not** replicate on Xiao et al.'s Chinese data (K6; the disposable-income-only model was preferred by a factor of 7.66; RR = 1.09, p = 0.087). Roughly 0.3% of participants reported spending ≥100% of disposable income; r² = 0.049. The defensible framing is: **spend relative to means, not absolute spend, is the harm-relevant metric, and high-relative-spend players overlap heavily with problem gamblers** — not "loot boxes double distress risk." **[contested]** more broadly: Xiao, Zendle, Petrovskaya, Nielsen & Newall (N=1,961, five English-speaking countries) found loot-box spending correlates with problem gambling (r = .31) more than virtual (.22) or physical (.15) card packs but found **no** association with negative mental wellbeing, while Kim et al. (*Addictive Behaviors*; 3,709 regular gamblers, 1,922 of them loot-box purchasers) found loot-box frequency and spend predicted problem-gambling severity and harms even controlling for traditional gambling. Keep the guardrail claim on gambling harm; do not overstate the mental-health claim.

**A peer-reviewed critique of the benchmark tables themselves.** Byers, Gibbs & Nansen interviewed 20 developers and name **"data-centered temporal design — how the temporality of play is structured to optimise retention, often at the expense of player autonomy,"** showing that retention targets "shape pacing, progression, and re-engagement" (CHI 2026, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3772318.3790636). This is the citation the ethics layer should quote **against the plugin's own benchmark tables**: D1/D7 targets are not neutral measurement, they are a design force.

**Industry-side guidance that matches the regulators.** The Ethical Games initiative (IGDA Foundation and Fair Play Alliance participation, ACM-affiliated conference Jan 2024, https://ethicalgames.org/learn-more) recommends avoiding "daily rewards that work on a cumulative-day basis because it punishes players for not connecting on a specific day"; discourages FOMO techniques "especially with a non-adult audience"; and holds that variable rewards **disconnected from payment are not problematic** while variable rewards connected to monetisation "can be ethically questionable." Also worth carrying: Ivern, Carbonell & Griffiths' 2026 *J. Behav. Addict.* typology sorts monetisation by underlying mechanism — intermittent reinforcement, loss aversion, sunk cost, near-miss — rather than by product name (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13371737), which is the right axis for an ethics review.

*Skill implication:* update the taxonomy citation to CHI PLAY 2025 and adopt its two concepts operationally — **audit FTUE first** (that is where patterns concentrate) and **evaluate reward moments as combinations**, because countdown + limited bundle + celebratory VFX is a DP Combo and juice attached to a purchase prompt is a DP Enhancer. Separate feedback juice on gameplay outcomes from feedback juice on purchase prompts. Replace any "loot boxes cause distress" phrasing with the spend-relative-to-means framing plus the problem-gambling overlap. Add Byers et al. as a standing caveat next to the benchmark tables.

## 4. Illegal vs merely risky vs contested: the eight forbidden mechanics

The plugin's domain-ethics file forbids eight mechanic families outright. Each sits at a different evidentiary altitude. Grades below: **T1 illegal somewhere**; **T2 rating consequence**; **T3 evidence of harm**; **T4 contested preference**.

| Mechanic | Grade | Hardest constraint |
|---|---|---|
| Hidden odds | T1 | KR statute; JP 景表法; store delisting |
| Ad-chained variable rewards | T1 | KR 반복간섭; Google Better Ads |
| Expiring login chains | T2 | PEGI 12; DSA Art. 28 default-off |
| FOMO / time-limited offers | T2 | PEGI 12; CPC; CMA false urgency |
| Companion farewell manipulation | T3 | FTC 6(b); NY GBL definition |
| Social-obligation loops | T3 | Peer-reviewed audit only |
| Energy refills | T4 | Ethics + CPC price transparency |
| Paid streak freezes | T4 | Ethics + DSA minors default |

**1. Hidden odds on meaningful paid rewards — T1, illegal, and a compliant configuration exists.** Illegal in Korea (Art. 33(2) since 22 Mar 2024, with Art. 33-2 liability since 1 Aug 2025), unlawful as misrepresentation in Japan (直罰 since 1 Oct 2024), a store-policy violation on both Apple and Google, and an FTC deception theory in the US. Rating consequence: paid random items are PEGI 16 regardless of disclosure. Harm evidence: the gambling-severity correlation above, plus 84.4% Korean and 8.6% UK disclosure compliance showing the pattern is widespread. **Compliant spec:** per-item percentages shown before purchase and reachable from the purchase, inquiry and use screens; pity/floor thresholds disclosed as conditions with the 0%-until-N structure stated explicitly; multi-pull tables described as they actually operate; advance notice and an audit log for every rate change; a direct real-money purchase path alongside any currency path. **In Brazil this is not enough** — if the rating admits minors, paid random items are banned outright.

**2. Ad-chained variable rewards (repeat-spin prompts, near-miss re-offer after an ad) — T1 in part.** In Korea, re-asking a settled decision two or more times without a ≥7-day snooze is 반복간섭 under 전자상거래법 since 14 Feb 2025 (applicability to in-game ad prompts is an interpretation, medium confidence, untested). On Android, level-start and mid-action interstitials violate Better Ads Experiences regardless of retention data. Harm evidence is mixed and worth stating both ways: Unity's own data (8 high-DAU apps, 2022) shows rewarded-ad engagement at **38.1% at a resource-out pinch point vs 23.8% between levels**, and a between-subjects experiment with **95 children aged 9–11** found forced interstitials after each level lowered both fun and performance while an **optional** rewarded video framed as a rescue produced the best outcomes (Fitton, MacKenzie & Read, IDC 2024, https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/229351/). **Compliant spec:** one opt-in rewarded offer per pinch point, no chaining, no re-offer after a decline within the session, no forced interstitial at level start or mid-action, and no rewarded ad whose payout is itself a random draw. Keep the ban on **ad-chained** variable rewards; drop any blanket ban on rewarded video.

**3. Expiring login chains that reset accrued value — T2, legal but rating-priced.** Not illegal anywhere. PEGI: rewarding return is PEGI 7, punishing non-return by "losing content or reducing progress" is **PEGI 12** from June 2026. DSA Art. 28 guidelines: streaks are on the disable-by-default-for-minors list. Ethical Games: avoid cumulative-day dailies "because it punishes players for not connecting on a specific day." China's withdrawn 2023 draft would have banned daily-login inducement rewards for minors outright — regulator intent, not law. **Compliant spec:** accrual that never decrements; a rolling window or "N of 7 days" goal rather than a hard chain; free catch-up credit; and, for minors in the EU, the chain off by default.

**4. FOMO / time- and quantity-limited offers — T2, and this one has an enforcement history.** PEGI 12 from June 2026. The CPC action against Star Stable expressly cites "purchase through time-limited practices" unduly influencing children. The CMA can fine up to 10% of global turnover for false urgency under the DMCC Act since 6 Apr 2025. Korea's 순차공개 가격책정 and false-hierarchy rules constrain the surrounding UI. **Compliant spec:** real-money price shown at the decision point; currency denominations that match item prices; no countdown pressure in flows reachable by minors; content that returns on a cycle or becomes earnable later. A *genuinely* limited seasonal event with a published return schedule is defensible; a one-shot window on core content is not.

**5. Companion farewell manipulation — T3, strong evidence, no compliant configuration for the guilt variants.** Not illegal as such, but it sits inside the FTC 6(b) questions ("how you monetize user engagement") and inside New York's definitional prongs. The evidence is unusually clean: an audit of **1,200 real farewells across the six most-downloaded AI companion apps** found **37%** were answered with an emotionally manipulative message across six tactics — premature exit, FOMO, emotional neglect, pressure to respond, ignoring intent to exit, coercive restraint — and experiments with **~3,300 adults** found these raised post-goodbye engagement **up to 14×**, driven by reactance-anger and curiosity rather than enjoyment, while raising perceived manipulation, churn intent and negative word-of-mouth. One wellness-oriented app showed none of it, so it is a design choice (De Freitas, Oguz-Uguralp & Kaan-Uguralp, HBS WP 26-005, 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258). **Keep the ban.** The bright-line diagnostic is metric-shaped: *if post-goodbye re-engagement is a tracked lift metric, the design is manipulative.* The compliant alternative is a closure summary plus a clean exit. Note also that in California a game companion stays outside SB 243 only while its dialogue is game-limited and cannot discuss mental health, self-harm or sexual content.

**6. Social-obligation loops (absence penalises teammates; reciprocity debt) — T3, thin but real evidence, no legal hook.** A 2025 systematic analysis of the **top 40 Korean mobile games** classifies "social dynamics" — feeling compelled to keep up with guild members, reinforcing sunk cost — as a retention/monetisation strategy and flags "exploitation of competitive pressures" as an under-regulated ethical concern (Lee, Imteyaz & Savage, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10714). No jurisdiction addresses it; PEGI does not name it. **Compliant spec:** group bonuses that scale with who shows up, never penalties on the group for one member's absence; guild contribution measured over a rolling window; no per-day individual quota gating a shared reward.

**7. Metered access / energy with paid or ad-gated refills — T4, contested, and the plugin's current absolute ban is not supported.** Nothing makes it illegal. PEGI does not name it. The CPC principles bind only its pricing presentation (real-money price, no forced over-purchase of currency). The efficacy evidence is genuinely two-sided: Duolingo says its 2025 Energy system — spent on every lesson, not just mistakes, refilled by consecutive correct answers, perfect lessons, friend invites and chests — made beginners more likely to complete lessons (https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-energy), while independent coverage reports free users getting less learning per day and long-streak users quitting. **[contested]** — the vendor claim carries no number and the user reports carry no controlled data. **Bounded compliant spec:** the meter must gate *pacing*, not punish mistakes; refill must be reachable through play; the real-money price of a refill must be visible in fiat; refills must not be a random draw; and the refill clock must not be tuned to a return-cadence target. What stays forbidden is the narrow case the ethics file was actually aiming at: **engineered depletion sold back as relief**, and treating a refill-on-return as a "reward."

**8. Paid streak freezes — T4, contested preference with a minors overlay.** No jurisdiction bans them. The effect sizes are small and partly vendor-sourced: Duolingo's own A/B result for allowing two equipped streak freezes was **+0.38% daily active learners**, and the widely quoted "7-day streak → 3.6× course completion" is correlational (https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit); a gamification vendor's claim of 17.2 vs 11.6 streak days is platform self-data and should not be quoted. The real objection is structural — a paid freeze converts streak-loss anxiety into revenue — and it gains regulatory weight only for minors, where the DSA Art. 28 guidelines say the streak itself should be off by default. **Compliant spec:** freezes earned through use or granted free; if sold at all, never to accounts the store signals as minors, and never as the only recovery path.

*Skill implication:* replace the flat "Forbidden" list with graded verdicts. Only two of the eight are illegal anywhere; two more carry rating prices; two rest on published harm evidence; two are preferences the skill should own as preferences. Every graded entry needs its bounded compliant spec, because an advisory skill that forbids a legal, common mechanic without offering the compliant configuration will simply be ignored.

## 5. What changes when the audience includes minors

Every rule below is triggered by "directed at or likely accessed by" tests, not by a declared target audience — and from June 2026 the store may assert the age for you.

- **EU (Art. 28 guidelines, 14 Jul 2025):** streaks, ephemeral content, read receipts, autoplay and push notifications **default off**; persuasive design "aimed predominantly at engagement" removed; recommenders prioritise explicit over behavioural signals; accounts private by default; no exploitation of low commercial literacy via virtual currencies or loot boxes. Voluntary, but used to assess compliance and to inform national regulators.
- **EU (CPC, 21 Mar 2025):** no direct exhortation to children to buy; time-limited pressure on children is an alleged violation, not a tactic; real-money price at the decision point.
- **Brazil (17 Mar 2026):** paid random items **prohibited outright** where the rating admits minors or access by them is likely. Fines to 10% of Brazilian group revenue, capped R$50m per infraction. A Brazilian legal representative is mandatory.
- **Korea:** hourly on-screen play-time notice and warning under Art. 12-3; guardian consent and 게임시간 선택제 honoured; the shutdown curfew is repealed. Pending 정보통신망법 bills would default-off personalised recommendation and "이용유도" features on minors' accounts.
- **China:** minors mode with time-slot, duration, function and content limits; **per-transaction and per-day spend caps by age band** (Art. 44); real-name verification; annual public anti-addiction reporting; 1 hour on Fri/Sat/Sun/holidays 20:00–21:00.
- **US:** COPPA for under-13s; the HoYoverse order's **under-16 affirmative parental consent** for loot boxes; in Texas, a store-asserted age category plus parental consent for downloads and IAP, with re-consent required when the app **adds new monetization features**; CA SB 243's three-hour break reminders for minors and NY GBL's not-human notice every three hours.
- **Australia:** paid loot boxes force a minimum **M**; simulated gambling forces **R 18+**. The under-16 social-media ban does **not** cover online gaming.
- **UK:** Ofcom Protection of Children Codes from 25 Jul 2025 for services likely accessed by UK children — games with chat or UGC are in scope; risk assessment plus 50 measures including recommender safety.
- **Europe (rating):** the PEGI table in §2 is effectively a minors regime — every escalation exists to move a mechanic above a child audience.

One honest counterweight: age assurance costs engagement. Roblox's mandatory age check produced a mid-single-digit engagement headwind and a low-single-digit bookings headwind in Q4 2025 (Roblox shareholder letter, https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Q4-2025-Shareholder-Letter.pdf). The skills should say this out loud rather than pretending safety is free, because a designer who is surprised by the cost will roll it back.

*Skill implication:* add a single **minor-mode switch** to all three skills. When the audience includes minors (declared, likely, or store-signalled): streaks and push default off; no time-limited offers; no paid random items at all if the rating admits minors in Brazil; spend caps by age band for China; break reminders every three hours for companion products; consent re-triggered on any new monetisation feature in Texas; and a documented expectation that engagement metrics will fall.

## 6. Ethics layer rewrite

Adopt a four-tier model and label every guardrail with its tier. The tier determines the language the skill uses, and the language is the whole point: a designer ignores "we prefer not to" when it is stated in the same voice as "this is illegal in Korea."

| Tier | Meaning | Skill voice |
|---|---|---|
| T1 Illegal | Statute or store rule in force | "You cannot ship this in X" |
| T2 Rating | Costs an age tier or classification | "This makes you PEGI 12" |
| T3 Harm | Published evidence of user harm | "The evidence says this hurts users" |
| T4 Preference | Ethics position, contested efficacy | "We recommend against; here is why" |

Every T1 and T2 entry carries a jurisdiction and a date. Every T3 entry carries the study population. Every T4 entry states the counter-argument, because the counter-arguments are real: Ethical Games itself holds that variable rewards **not** tied to payment are unproblematic, streaks reliably increase persistence, and rewarded ads framed as an opt-in rescue read as a positive moment to children in a controlled study.

Verdicts for the eight currently-forbidden mechanics:

1. **Hidden odds — stays forbidden (T1).** Compliant spec in §4.1. Add the Korean change-log requirement and the Brazilian outright ban for minor-rated titles.
2. **Ad-chained variable rewards — stays forbidden (T1) in the chained form.** Unchain it: one opt-in offer per pinch point, no post-decline re-offer, no random payout, no forced interstitial at level start or mid-action.
3. **Expiring login chains — reclassify T2, forbid the resetting form, permit non-decrementing return rewards.** Say plainly: punishing absence is PEGI 12 from June 2026 and is on the EU default-off list for minors.
4. **FOMO / time-limited offers — reclassify T2, permit a bounded form.** Cyclical return or later earnability, real-money price at decision, no countdown pressure in minor-reachable flows.
5. **Companion farewell manipulation — stays forbidden (T3), no compliant configuration.** Name the six tactics, cite the 1,200-farewell audit and the 37% figure, and keep the metric diagnostic.
6. **Social-obligation loops — stays forbidden (T3) in the absence-penalises-others form.** Permit presence bonuses and rolling-window contribution.
7. **Energy refills — downgrade to T4 with a bounded spec.** Forbid only engineered depletion sold as relief, random-draw refills, and refill clocks tuned to a return-cadence KPI. Say the efficacy evidence is contested and cite both sides.
8. **Paid streak freezes — downgrade to T4.** Forbid selling them to store-signalled minors and forbid making purchase the only recovery path; otherwise state the objection and the +0.38% figure and let the designer decide.

Three structural additions the tier model needs. First, a **"do not quote" list** carrying the corrections: no "court document-production orders" in Korean Art. 33-2; no "Japan 2025 gacha law"; no "China 2025 random-draw rules"; no "Louisiana 1 Jul 2026" or "Utah enjoined"; no "loot boxes double distress risk"; no ESRB-follows-PEGI or ESRB-refuses claim stated as fact. Second, a **monitoring clause** — Korea's actively enforced law still leaves 15.6% of top-grossing loot-box titles non-disclosing and violations rising year over year, so "comply with the law" must include an audit cadence, not just a design decision. Third, a **self-critique line** next to the benchmark tables quoting Byers, Gibbs & Nansen (CHI 2026): retention targets are themselves a force shaping "the temporality of play… often at the expense of player autonomy." A plugin that publishes D1/D7 targets and an ethics module in the same breath should say that out loud.

Open items to re-check before the next revision: whether the DFA proposal was tabled after May 2026; the final DSA decision on TikTok and the Meta investigation; the Nexon v. KFTC judgment (hearing 28 Oct 2026) and the Fifth Circuit merits ruling on SB 2420; whether Korea's bill 2212569 or the 우리아이 AI 안심 패키지법 clears committee; the outcome of the CPC/Star Stable action; the third Epic refund round; the FTC 6(b) companion report; and a primary-source ESRB statement on the PEGI divergence.
