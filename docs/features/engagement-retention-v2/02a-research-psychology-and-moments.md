# Player Psychology and Reward Moments (2023–2026 evidence)

The mechanism vocabulary the three skills currently use — dopamine points, variable-ratio schedules, the flow channel, "satisfy the three needs" — has moved from *settled* to *contested or wrong* between 2023 and 2026, while the design advice that actually replicates has narrowed and sharpened: feedback must be contingent on success and legible, curiosity rather than enjoyment predicts continued play, absolute input-to-feedback delay degrades felt agency whether or not players notice it, and need *frustration* (not playtime) is the ethics axis with evidence behind it. This document gives the numbers, populations and definitions behind each of those, states where the evidence points both ways, and closes with a citation-by-citation rewrite map for `interaction-reward-moments/references/research-basis.md`.

Habit-formation timing (psych-8) and streak asymmetry (psych-9, learncomp-6, gap6-12) are psychology findings but belong to the retention/lifecycle document; only the parts that change a *moment* — reward devaluation in habitual users, informational vs controlling framing — are carried here.

## 1. The dopamine problem: what 2024–2026 neuroscience supports

### 1.1 What reward-prediction-error actually claims

Schultz's RPE account says a phasic dopamine burst encodes the *difference* between received and predicted reward: unexpected reward → burst, fully predicted reward → no burst, omitted expected reward → dip. It is a **learning/teaching signal about prediction, not a pleasure signal, and not a quantity a product emits**. A 2026 review still calls the core finding "likely one of the most replicated findings in all of neuroscience" (Dudhabhate & Costa, 2026, Frontiers in Computational Neuroscience, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/computational-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fncom.2026.1825622/full). So the skills' underlying intuition — that a *legible expectation* is the precondition for a strong outcome moment — is not baseless.

### 1.2 What broke

- **The field's own synthesis downgraded it.** Gershman, Assad, Datta, Linderman, Sabatini, Uchida & Wilbrecht (2024, *Nature Neuroscience* 27:1645–1655, 25 Jul 2024, https://gershmanlab.com/pubs/Gershman24_dopamine.pdf): "The RPE-based interpretation of dopamine signaling is, in its original form, probably too simple and fails to explain all the properties of phasic dopamine observed in behaving animals." Dopamine also tracks salience, novelty, vigor, causal inference and learning rate; some of those "demand fundamentally different theoretical explanations beyond encoding RPE."
- **Heterogeneity within the same review that defends RPE.** Dudhabhate & Costa (2026) document that "dopamine neurons are not all the same," that dopamine *ramps* toward fully predicted rewards (which "should not happen according to classical TDRL RPE definitions"), and that dopamine "mediates learning in the absence of any reinforcement."
- **A direct challenge.** Bakhurin, Hughes, Jiang, Hossain, Gutkin, Fallon & Yin (2025, *Nature Communications* 16, 13 Oct 2025, head-fixed mice, Pavlovian task, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12518538/): "Variations in force and licking fully account for DA dynamics traditionally attributed to RPE"; phasic dopamine was "neither necessary nor sufficient for stimulus-reward learning." **[contested]** — this is one lab, a single Pavlovian paradigm, and small n (n=4 DAT-cre + 6 WT in the inhibition arm). It is a reason to say *contested*, never *refuted*.
- **Wanting ≠ liking.** Robinson & Berridge (2025, *Annual Review of Psychology* 76, 30-year review, https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/berridge-lab/wp-content/uploads/sites/743/2025/06/2025-Robinson-Berridge-The-incentive-sensitization-theory-of-addiction-30-years-on-An-Rev-Psychol.pdf): consensus is that mesolimbic dopamine "mediates incentive motivation ('wanting')… but not their hedonic impact (liking)." Uncertainty amplifies wanting specifically: "an uncertain cue that predicts a reward on 50% of occasions becomes more attractive… than a cue that predicts a reward 100% of the time." In a 10-year human cohort developing alcohol use disorder, wanting rose while liking stayed "high and stable."

### 1.3 Why "dopamine point" is not a defensible product term

Three independent failures, any one of which is disqualifying for a skill that a designer will quote to a stakeholder:

1. **Unfalsifiable at the product layer.** No product team can measure striatal dopamine. A term whose referent cannot be observed in the artefact under discussion converts a design argument into an appeal to authority.
2. **It asserts pleasure, and the mechanism it names does not deliver pleasure** (Robinson & Berridge, 2025). "This screen gives a dopamine hit" claims *liking*; the cited mechanism is *wanting*. Popular "dopamine detox" framing is explicitly rejected by the same neuroscientists (The Scientist, 2024, via psych-10).
3. **It is legally and ethically loaded.** "We engineered a dopamine hit" is a sentence a regulator or plaintiff can read as an admission of intentional compulsion design — precisely the theory of harm behind the EU DSA "addictive design" work (see the regulation document).

### 1.4 Vocabulary replacement

| Drop | Use instead | Because |
|---|---|---|
| dopamine point / dopamine hit | reward moment, outcome beat | unmeasurable, asserts liking |
| triggers dopamine | resolves an expectation | RPE is a prediction signal |
| dopamine loop | anticipation → action → outcome → next goal | describes the observable |
| variable reward = engagement | uncertainty raises *wanting* | wanting ≠ liking ≠ playtime |
| this feels rewarding | players rated competence X / curiosity Y | measurable via PXI |

**Skill implication.** Rename the core model in `research-basis.md` from "dopamine point" to **reward moment** and delete every claim that a UI event "triggers dopamine." Keep RPE as *one contested explanatory lens for surprise and anticipation*, stated in three clauses that survive all the 2024–2026 evidence: (a) a legible baseline expectation is required before an outcome can exceed it; (b) fully predicted rewards stop producing a prediction signal, so fixed unvarying rewards decay in salience; (c) product-level dopamine claims are unmeasurable and should never appear in a design document. Add wanting-vs-liking as a *measurement split*, not a mechanism the designer controls: track return/spend urge separately from enjoyment, and flag any design whose urge metric rises while enjoyment stays flat.

## 2. SDT after the critiques

### 2.1 The critique is about use, not about the theory

Tyack & Mekler audited N=259 SDT-based HCI games papers plus SDT scholarship on games and an industry-practitioner conference (2024, *ACM TOCHI* 31(3), doi:10.1145/3673230, https://www.mendeley.com/catalogue/77f1a254-c924-3f7c-ada0-9805975862ef/). Finding: "perfunctory applications of the theory in HCI games research originate in part from within SDT scholarship on games, which itself exhibits limited engagement with theoretical tenets"; SDT operates as an "oft-unquestioned paradigm." The failure mode they name is exactly the one in the current skill file — autonomy/competence/relatedness rendered as a three-item design checklist, with no named mechanism and no falsifiable prediction.

### 2.2 Need frustration is a separate axis, not low satisfaction

The Basic Needs in Games (BANG) model (Ballou & Deterding, 2024, *Interacting with Computers* 38(3), doi:10.1093/iwc/iwae042, https://academic.oup.com/iwc/article/38/3/469/7816687) states the propositions a skill can act on: "both the quantity and the quality of video game play can affect players"; "Need satisfaction fosters wellbeing… while need frustration causes illbeing"; population-level playtime nulls arise because "people in the main successfully and flexibly self-regulate selective gaming exposure"; displacement harms only when "life fit… is low." Crucially the paper **does not identify which design elements cause need frustration** — only that "certain social contexts, game design elements and individual factors" do. That gap is where a design skill has to be honest: need frustration is a *review question*, not a lookup table.

Concretely, need frustration is coercion (autonomy), competence *undermining* (not merely absent competence support — e.g. rank loss, retroactive nerfs, punished absence), and exclusion or obligation (relatedness — alliance duty, guild guilt).

### 2.3 Competence is three things that predict opposite difficulty

Deterding, Guckelsberger, Lintunen & Ady (2025, *Motivation and Emotion*, open access 26 Sep 2025, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11031-025-10156-8): "competence need satisfaction as task performance is logically maximised under minimal challenge, while competence as capacity growth is maximised under high challenge. This incoherence may have been occluded by the verbal ambiguity of the optimal challenge construct." SDT texts variously define optimal challenge as "<50% success" and as "succeed at the vast majority of tasks." The three facets are **effectance** (I caused that), **task performance** (I succeeded), **capacity growth** (I am better than I was).

### 2.4 The BANGS status dispute **[corrected]**

- The scale: BANGS (Ballou, Denisova, Ryan, Rigby & Deterding, 2024, *IJHCS* 188:103289, accepted 30 Apr 2024, online 8 May 2024, CC BY) — 18 items, 6 subscales (satisfaction + frustration for each of autonomy, competence, relatedness), validated over five studies with **1,246 unique participants**. Its own predictive evidence: R²=.50 for intrinsic motivation (vs .42 for BPNSFS) and **8.4% of variance in objectively logged Xbox playtime**, which the authors themselves called "modest evidence."
- The withdrawal: the Center for Self-Determination Theory page now reads, verbatim, "Although the preliminary data in Ballou et al showed potential promise, subsequent research has shown a lack of predictive validity. We are thus no longer recommending its use." (https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/basic-needs-in-games-bang/). **[corrected]** The exact string is "no longer recommending its use," and CSDT names **no** source; verification dated the change to between 2025-12-15 and 2026-04-18 via Wayback captures.
- The likely source, by BANGS's own lead author: Ballou, Földes, Vuorre, Hakman, Magnusson & Przybylski (2026), "All BANG, little buck," PsyArXiv, posted 26 Jan 2026 (https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/wqr6u_v1) — Study 1 preregistered, 11k daily observations from 555 U.S. players with 30 days of multi-platform trace data; Study 2 exploratory, 102k sessions from 9k PowerWash Simulator players. "Need satisfaction was robustly associated with subjective states but showed weak or null associations with short-term gaming behavior, including subsequent play, session length, and return latency." Still a preprint, not peer-reviewed as of 2026-09-05; nickballou.com still distributes BANGS without a caveat.
- **The scope correction the skills must not lose:** this is a **motivation→behaviour gap, not a measurement failure**. Need satisfaction still tracks experience robustly. It just does not forecast retention.
- **[contested]** Counter-reading worth carrying: BANGS was validated across five studies and is still distributed by its authors; CSDT (home of the proprietary PENS lineage) withdrew a competing open scale citing unnamed research. The withdrawal may reflect a camp dispute as much as settled evidence.

**Skill implication.** Rewrite the SDT section of `research-basis.md` to (1) name the *specific* need and the *specific* mechanism claimed for each recommendation, never listing the three needs as a checklist; (2) add **need frustration as a first-class, separately-audited ethics check** across all three skills — coercion, competence undermining, exclusion/obligation — since frustration causes illbeing on a different axis than satisfaction causes wellbeing; (3) require the designer to state **which competence facet** a reward moment targets, and define *mastery moments* as visible **capacity-growth** signals (first-time clears, personal bests, before/after comparison, a newly unlocked technique) rather than generic success feedback; (4) date-stamp Ryan/Rigby/Przybylski 2006 as classic with the 2024 caveat; (5) state plainly that need-satisfaction scores are **not** a leading indicator of D1/D7/D30 — do not sell SDT as a retention forecast.

## 3. Instruments a designer can actually use

| Instrument | Validation status | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| PXI (10 factors) | n=1,518 independent, CHI 2024 | default playtest survey |
| miniPXI (11 items) | 15 experts, 628 players; r .51–.83 | fast iteration loops |
| PENS | proprietary (Immersyve) **[unverified]** | licensed studio work only |
| BANGS (18 items) | 5 studies, 1,246; CSDT withdrew **[corrected]** | experience, not behaviour |
| Behavioural pair | required alongside any scale | voluntary replay, return |

- **PXI** — independently validated at n=1,518 players (CHI 2024, doi:10.1145/3613904.3642270, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642270). The original **10-factor model fit best** (AIC 120417, lowest of the compared models). Two published caveats a designer must carry: a section titled "Challenges of the immersion construct" (the immersion factor is weak) and "Weak evidence of a general player experience score" — **do not collapse results into one 'fun score'**. Its factor split is directly usable as reward-moment vocabulary: *functional consequences* = ease of control, challenge, progress feedback, goals and rules, audiovisual appeal; *psychosocial consequences* = meaning, mastery, curiosity, immersion, autonomy.
- **miniPXI** — 11 single items (Haider et al., PACM HCI 2022, doi:10.1145/3549507), single-item reliabilities .51–.83, mean .68. Fine for A/B iteration; too noisy for a shipping decision on one construct.
- **PENS** — proprietary/licensed, descends from the Ryan/Rigby/Przybylski 2006 lineage. **[unverified]** No independent 2023–2026 revalidation was located in this corpus; treat it as unavailable for an indie or plugin-user workflow rather than as validated.
- **[unverified]** Korean-language validation status of PXI/miniPXI, and of any need-satisfaction instrument, could not be established. A Korean-market team should pilot with back-translation and report item-level reliability rather than assume the published factor structure holds.

**Skill implication.** Add a measurement block to `interaction-reward-moments`: PXI as default, miniPXI for rapid loops, an explicit prohibition on a single fun score, and a mapping from the skill's own vocabulary (feedback, challenge, curiosity, mastery, agency) onto PXI constructs so a designer measures the thing they changed. Every scale recommendation ships paired with **one behavioural measure** (voluntary replay, time-to-next-action, return within 24h), because 2026 evidence says experience scores and behaviour dissociate.

## 4. Flow and challenge–skill balance

**What the strongest test found.** Cutting, Deterding, Demediuk & Sephton (2023, *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 10:220274, pre-registered, n=311, https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/217887/1/cutting-et-al-2023-difficulty-skill-balance-does-not-affect-engagement-and-enjoyment-a-pre-registered-study-using.pdf) used an MCTS opponent to hit target win margins (easy +15, balanced 0, hard −15). The manipulation worked — win rates 100% / 12.8% / 5.9% — and players did not notice it. Objective difficulty–skill ratio had **no significant effect on enjoyment** (F=1.29, p=.277) or engagement (p=.183): IMI enjoyment 39.3 / 38.2 / 37.5, "play again" 20% / 29% / 30%. The companion CHI PLAY 2023 study (doi:10.1145/3611065, n=309) found the draw condition was *perceived* as most balanced yet "balance did not impact play behaviour, nor did we find the predicted uniform inverted-U." Authors' own limits: no no-DDA control, only two matches, so long-run learning-curve effects are untested; and flow theory is about *perceived*, not objective, balance.

**The citation currently in the skill is misread.** PMC8943660 is Larche & Dixon (2020, *J. Behav. Addict.* 9(3)), **n=60, Candy Crush**. Easy levels produced the least flow; regular and hard produced comparable flow; flow added 21.8% of variance in "urge to keep playing" beyond arousal. The authors frame that urge as a **risk marker**: "high flow may in part account for why problem players report playing for longer than intended." The skill cites it as a design objective. **[unverified]** No direct replication of Larche & Dixon was found; generalisation beyond Candy Crush is untested.

**[contested] — difficulty does move behaviour, just not through the flow channel.** Ascarza, Netzer & Runge (*International Journal of Research in Marketing* 42(4):975-995, 2025; ~330,000 users, 12-week randomized rollout in a free-to-play mobile puzzle game, https://evaascarza.com/papers/Ascarza_Netzer_Runge_IJRM25.pdf): lowering difficulty for at-risk players raised Retention-1/7/14 by +2.7 / +2.5 / +2.0 pp (p<0.001), rounds +1.25, and IAP spend, net +$0.07 per average user. The same paper reports easier difficulty "significantly decreases purchases in the specific round played" — DDA is not costless. Pulling the other way, Drachen et al. (AIIDE 2016, https://andersdrachen.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/aiide_rapid-prediction-player-1.pdf) found early *skill* signals (fewer moves, more stars) inversely related to retention — "too easy for skilled players" is a real, separate failure mode.

**What replaces the flow channel.** Convergent 2025 work points at **learning progress** and the **difficulty–expectation gap**, not difficulty level: "Leveling up fun" (*Scientific Reports*, Oct 2025) reports across real game datasets plus two experiments that learning progress and difficulty–expectation disparity predict engagement, and that when learning progress is suppressed the difficulty–enjoyment link disappears **[unverified — authors not confirmed, full text not opened]**. This dovetails with the competence-facet result (§2.3): "balance" is ambiguous precisely because task performance and capacity growth pull in opposite directions.

**Skill implication.** Demote flow/challenge–skill balance and DDA from *core lever* to **weak, contested lever requiring per-game validation**. Correct the PMC8943660 citation to Larche & Dixon 2020 (n=60, Candy Crush) and **reverse its framing** — stop using "urge to keep playing" as a design objective; it is the outcome its authors flag as a risk marker. Replace "tune difficulty into the flow channel" with three testable instructions: make success gradations legible, expose learning progress explicitly, and set difficulty *expectations* before the attempt (previews, ranked labels, "this one is hard") since the expectation gap is itself the lever. Where DDA is proposed, frame it as **churn-risk relief for at-risk cohorts** with a measured in-round monetisation cost, not as flow tuning.

## 5. Game feel and juice: contingency, dose, and three make-or-break features

### 5.1 The contingency condition and the dose curve

Kao, Ballou, Gerling, Breitsohl & Deterding (CHI 2024, pre-registered, **n=1,699** Prolific participants, ~340 per condition, purpose-built action RPG, 2×2+control varying amplification × success-dependence × variability, https://people.csail.mit.edu/dkao/pdf/3613904.3642656.pdf):

- Success-**dependent** amplified feedback raised competence, curiosity, effectance and enjoyment.
- Amplified feedback that was **not** success-dependent *lowered* effectance (−.19) and competence (−.43).
- Voluntary playtime ranged from 6.17 min (standard feedback) to 10.18 min (amplified + success-dependent + varied).
- Authors: "There can therefore be too much of a good thing in amplified feedback where it occludes causal action-feedback links or flattens success gradations." They flag that their amplified condition may have violated the "Unambiguous" juiciness criterion — sword feedback engulfing enemy-hit feedback.

Dose evidence: Kao (2020, *Entertainment Computing*, N=3,018) found an inverted-U — **both "None" and "Extreme" juiciness reduced playtime, player experience, intrinsic motivation and performance** versus Medium/High.

So juice is not a volume knob. It has three preconditions: **(1) fires only on success and scales with degree of success; (2) each effect maps unambiguously to one player action, occluding nothing; (3) it amplifies a functional response rather than decorating a screen.**

### 5.2 Hit-stop, sound coherence, camera **[corrected]**

Lin, Duan, Wen & Cai, **IEEE GEM 2022** **[corrected — not IEEE CoG]** (arXiv:2208.06155 v3, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.06155): an open corpus of 281,720 Chinese-language Steam comments across 96 fighting-tagged games, 5,000 manually annotated by two experienced annotators to train a Naive Bayes classifier (88%/92% test accuracy); 44 games passed a 20% impact-feel relevance threshold and were ranked; the top-8 and bottom-8 were scored against a 19-feature framework. Conclusion, verbatim: "hit stop, sound coherence, and camera control may strongly influence players' impact feel. A lack of dedicated design on one of these three features may ruin players' impact feel."

**[corrected]** Two attribution fixes: the two games lacking hit stop (Sacred Citadel, Draw Slasher) rank **39th and 40th of 44** — the only two of the sixteen examined games lacking hit stop, *not* the overall bottom two; ranks 43–44 do use hit stop, and one is placed low for sound-coherence failure. And the paper's term is "sound coherence," which it defines to include audio–visual desync ("improper sound effects and time delay between visual and audio feedback"). Scope caveat: a descriptive feature inventory over 16 hand-coded games and a Chinese-only corpus; the camera-control finding rests on five beat-'em-ups. Cite with the paper's own hedge ("may"), never as causal.

### 5.3 Dose, per attack type, per player **[corrected]**

Seki & Ishikawa, "Quantifying Hit Feedback Preferences in 2D Action Games," **CHI EA '26 poster / extended abstract** **[corrected — not a full ACM paper]**, Barcelona, 13–17 Apr 2026, article 539, doi:10.1145/3772363.3798911. Eight visual and temporal parameters, adjustment N=16, gameplay validation N=116: charge attacks require **1.7× stronger presentation**; individual differences span **6-fold ranges with three distinct preference types**; random settings are judged **"excessive 45% of the time, indicating comfortable ranges are biased toward lower values"**; averaged 16-participant settings achieved **>80% optimal ratings** from new users. The paper's own takeaway is "upper-bound constraints and customization options." Scope: one 2D prototype; the 1.7× is a *within-prototype ratio between attack types*, not a transferable multiplier for 3D or other genres.

Counter-directional: Ban & Ujitoko (IEEE WHC 2021, doi:10.1109/whc49131.2021.9517129) found long hit-stop in VR increases real/virtual body-motion mismatch and can **reduce sense of agency**. Direction of the practical rule is therefore: **cap the effect, scale it by attack weight, let players tune it, err low.**

### 5.4 Responsiveness is a precondition, not polish

Sense of agency tracks **absolute** action–outcome delay rather than its subjective discriminability (Erdogan & Balcı, 2024, *QJEP*) — agency degrades whether or not the player consciously notices the lag. Expectation framing biases judgment: with **identical delays**, 57.36% of comparisons favoured the sequence labelled "optimized" and only 13.61% went against it (60 participants; >70% of subjective ratings affected by labels; MDPI *MTI* 9(5):47, 2025, https://www.mdpi.com/2414-4088/9/5/47). Industry practice matches: Riot's 2XKO adds **a fixed three frames of input delay to all inputs, online and offline**, so offline muscle memory transfers directly — *consistency beats raw minimum*. DICE's Battlefield 6 game-feel lead frames feel as a body→input→screen→body loop where "perceived latency" and "the qualitative behavior of that response" matter as much as raw performance (Game Developer, 17 Mar 2026, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/using-choreography-to-enhance-battlefield-6-s-game-feel).

### 5.5 First-party practice worth codifying

Nintendo, *Ask the Developer* Vol. 19 (Donkey Kong Bananza, Jul 2025, https://www.nintendo.com/en-gb/News/2025/July/Ask-the-Developer-Vol-19-Donkey-Kong-Bananza-Chapter-2-2871378.html): "At 30 fps, we couldn't fully capture everything that happens in that instant. But at 60 fps, we saw that sense of destruction coming through much more clearly"; and for sounds heard "thousands of times… we aimed for sounds that are pleasant to the ears without being tiring or repetitive… We recorded lots of variations… added subtle randomness." Team Asobi (GDC 2025, via The Verge, 20 Jun 2025) shortened the delay before Astro's impatient idle animation "as a way to more quickly remind the player of the goal" — a near-free anticipation/tempo cue.

### 5.6 Accessibility is a hard constraint on juice

Xbox Accessibility Guideline 117 (updated 3 Mar 2026, https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/gaming/accessibility/Xbox-accessibility-guidelines/117): "Avoid the use of camera shake, camera bobbing effects, motion blur, mouse blur, and more or provide an option to turn off these behaviors" (Halo Infinite's 0–100% sliders are the cited exemplar). The ESA Accessible Games Initiative (launched Mar 2025 with Nintendo, Microsoft, EA, Google, Ubisoft; later Riot, Square Enix, Amazon, WB) defines a storefront **"Camera Comfort"** tag requiring such effects be absent or switchable, with intensity sliders that reach zero.

**Skill implication.** Rewrite the game-feel section as an ordered checklist, not a list of layers: **(0) input-to-feedback latency measured *and stabilised* — consistency over minimum; (1) frame rate adequate to the impact moment; (2) feedback contingent on success and graded by degree; (3) one effect ↔ one action, no occlusion of the state the player needs; (4) hit-stop scaled by attack weight, audio synced to the impact frame, camera response reserved for heavy/finisher hits; (5) variation and subtle randomisation for anything fired hundreds of times per session, haptics included; (6) per-effect intensity slider that reaches zero, conservative default, no competitive advantage from disabling.** Add an explicit **over-juicing failure mode** with the Kao 2020 inverted-U and the "45% judged excessive" figure. Never label a build "optimized" without a real change — the labelling effect is real but is a manipulation, not a lever.

## 6. Curiosity, not enjoyment, predicts continued play

The single most consequential result for a skill about "satisfying moments" is inside the same CHI 2024 experiment (n=1,699): **"curiosity emerged as the strongest enjoyment and only playtime predictor, while success dependency drove curiosity, effectance, and competence alike. Contrary to theoretical predictions, enjoyment did not predict playtime."** Reported associations: curiosity–enjoyment .75–.76; curiosity–voluntary engagement .86; enjoyment–playtime not significant. The authors' mechanism: "motivation and enjoyment arise from reducing uncertainty over action success, which depends on legible and differentiated amplified feedback."

Read together with §2.4, two independent 2024–2026 results say the same thing from opposite directions: **self-reported positive experience does not forecast behaviour.** Kao found enjoyment ↛ playtime in an experiment; Ballou et al. (2026 preprint) found need satisfaction ↛ subsequent play, session length or return latency in trace data.

This changes what a "satisfying moment" is designed to be. A satisfying moment is not one that maximises rated pleasure at the outcome; it is one that leaves the player with **a resolvable open question about their own success** — will the timing work, can I get the S-rank, does this build come online — that the next attempt can answer. Uncertainty about *the outcome of one's own action* (curiosity) is the engine; uncertainty about *a dice roll* is a different mechanism with a different ethical profile (§7).

**Skill implication.** Replace "enjoyment/delight drives retention" in all three skills with: **curiosity about action success drives continued play; enjoyment did not predict playtime (Kao et al., CHI 2024, n=1,699).** Give the designer a curiosity test for any proposed moment — *after this beat, does the player hold a specific question about their own capability that another attempt would answer?* If the only open question is "what will I get," the design is leaning on chance, not on curiosity. And stop treating a good survey score as a retention forecast: pair every PX measure with a behavioural one.

## 7. Uncertainty, near-miss and variable-ratio: what replicates

### 7.1 Variable-ratio scheduling does not replicate as an engagement lever

Direct tests using a modified open-source loot-box simulator (Hancock, Kansas State thesis; Study 1 FR vs VR vs RR schedules; Study 2 variable reward magnitude) found: "Participants in Study 1 initially opened loot boxes more on highly variable schedules, but the effect was short-lived. Study 2 found no effects of variable magnitude on loot box opening decisions" — the author "failed to find support for any of our hypotheses." The companion methods paper notes human operant work "often fails to replicate the results of animal learning studies," and that whether loot boxes even *are* VR is contested (pity timers resemble VR; magnitude-variable continuous reinforcement is an equally good description) (Young, Hancock, Watson, Howatt, Southern & Payne, 2026, *Perspectives on Behavior Science*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12781166/). Newall (2023, *Addiction Research & Theory*) adds that loot-box spending correlates weakly, nullly or negatively with impulsivity and is driven by enjoyment, meaning and social factors rather than money-seeking.

### 7.2 Uncertainty itself does move behaviour — through wanting and through beliefs

- **Wanting.** A 50%-predictive cue becomes *more* attractive than a 100% cue (Robinson & Berridge, 2025); "by rendering mesolimbic circuitry hyperresponsive, uncertainty can produce sensitization-like effects."
- **Beliefs about odds.** In an incentivized between-subject experiment on three-prize monetary lotteries, censoring exact top-prize odds raised willingness-to-pay ~**45%**; showing a selected sample of best outcomes raised it ~**45%**; both together **doubled** it (+100%). Adding unbiased distribution information did not change WTP. The authors call these features ones that "do not provide any utility to gamers" and note their estimate is a lower bound because real loot boxes add visual effects and game context (Cordes, Dertwinkel-Kalt & Werner, 2024, *JEBO*, https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/505957/1/1-s2.0-S016726812400369X-main.pdf).

### 7.3 Near-miss: real motivational effects, no persistence effect **[contested]**

Palmer, Ferrari & Clark (2024, *Psychology of Addictive Behaviors* 38(6):716–727, four pre-registered online slot studies, n=169/148/170/172, 1-in-3 near-miss rate, https://europepmc.org/article/MED/38709628): near-misses increased urge to continue, **sped up the next spin** and **increased bet size** — and were rated *more positively* than full misses, the opposite of frustration theory. "No single traditional theory of near-misses accounted for our pattern of results." Against this, Pisklak, Yong & Spetch (2020, *J. Gambl. Stud.* 36:611–632) used pigeon and human resistance-to-extinction designs and "failed to provide evidence that near misses have a conditionally reinforcing function," calling any effect "limited or idiosyncratic." **[unverified]** No 2023–2026 experiment on near-miss in *non-gambling* games was located; the latest game-specific evidence remains Larche et al. 2017 (Candy Crush near-misses increased frustration and urge).

Net: near-miss reliably produces **faster and larger spending**, not durable retention.

### 7.4 What actually sustains repeat opening

An observational study of **490 livestreamed loot-box openings** across 13 Twitch/YouTube streams (~27h16m analytic phase; EA Sports FC 26, Brawl Stars, Clash Royale, Counter-Strike, Pokémon TCG Pocket) found **93.1% occurred inside multi-opening streaks**, median 14.5 s between openings, 72.7% within 30 s, ~88% of in-streak openings followed by another, explicit stopping statements in only 3.7% of coded verbal moments. Streak *initiation* was tied to visible monetisation cues (temporary offers, virtual currency); *continuation* was **not** explained by rarity, affect or near-misses; streaks ended mainly when "the ongoing flow of the sequence" was disrupted (Borja-Block et al., 2026, *Frontiers in Public Health*, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2026.1858751/full).

The design-relevant reading: the **"open again" affordance and the on-screen currency/offer cues** sustain repeat opening — not the reward content. Removing near-miss animations is therefore **not** a sufficient mitigation.

**Skill implication.** Delete any statement that variable-ratio scheduling is a reliable engagement lever, and delete near-miss from the pattern library as a technique. Reframe: *uncertainty raises wanting (Robinson & Berridge 2025) and hidden or curated odds raise willingness-to-pay by 45–100% (Cordes et al. 2024), while schedule variability per se produces at most a transient bump (Young et al. 2026).* Draw the ethical line at **coupling**: uncertainty attached to skill outcomes and non-paid rewards is ordinary game design; uncertainty attached to money, or to a one-tap repeat loop, is the harm surface. Prescribe as ethics defaults for any random-reward reveal: per-item exact odds at the purchase and reveal surface, ceiling/pity odds by count, no auto-advance, no one-tap "open again," a post-N summary screen with **spend-so-far visible**, and cool-downs after rare outcomes (the documented escalation point, §8.1). Classify global "X just pulled a Legendary!" tickers, banded odds and streamer-only showcases as **belief-distorting demand inflation**, not social proof.

## 8. Anticipation, progress, choice, earned vs given

### 8.1 Anticipation is measurable and is where the arousal lives

Larche, Chini, Lee, Dixon & Fernandes (2019, *J. Gambl. Stud.*, 49 real Overwatch opening videos: 2 s box-shake, coin reveal ~2 s, full reveal at 5 s, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7882574/): skin-conductance-level slopes were significantly greater **during the 2-second anticipatory shake** than baseline (t(39)=−3.88, p≤.001). Post-reveal, rarer outcomes produced larger SCRs, harder mouse presses, longer post-reinforcement pauses, and a stronger urge to open more (legendary M=55.63, SD 29.46 > epic M=40.00 > rare M=32.84 on 0–100, all p≤.001). The authors note the shaking box is an engineered anticipation cue with "no comparable feature in standard slot machine games."

The generalisable, non-monetised lesson: **the pre-outcome window is the designed moment.** A cue that establishes a bounded expectation and holds it for 1–2 seconds carries the arousal; the consummation only confirms it. This is the honest version of the RPE intuition — and it works identically for a boss stagger bar, a charge attack, a chest tier glow or a card flip, none of which need money attached.

Pacing is a lever: a 2026 HCI study of Pop Mart's "instant reveal, ship later" blind-box flow reports that instant reveals "compress arousal into brief peaks and can reduce felt control," while configurable pacing and consequential micro-choices during the reveal "extend the suspense arc and improve perceived control and fairness" (Tran & Zhao, doi:10.1145/3772363.3798946, https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3798946). **[unverified — abstract only, full text not opened.]**

### 8.2 Progress mechanics need updating **[contested]**

- **Progress bars distort estimation.** Across nine studies (**N=3,735**, including a field experiment with physical loyalty cards): "when actual reward progress is displayed more visually, loyalty program members overestimate their progress when they are far from their reward but underestimate it when they are near their reward," driven by lower confidence; robust across bar, ring and marker formats (Bauer, Khamitov, Isaac & Sevilla, *JAMS*, online 8 Jan 2026, https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-025-01133-1). **[unverified — abstract-level; full text not opened.]**
- **The goal gradient is not monotonic in gamified tracks.** Four experiments found "gamified loyalty programs produce non-monotonic motivation patterns across the stages, in contrast to the incremental patterns observed in conventional loyalty programs," with "to-go" vs "to-date" framing mattering at advanced stages (Hwang & Choi, *JRCS*, Feb 2026). This directly complicates the classic goal-gradient assumption that motivation rises smoothly toward the goal — expect a **mid-journey dip**.
- **Endowed progress rests on 2006 field data.** The canonical numbers remain Nunes & Drèze's car-wash study (**34% vs 19% completion**). **[unverified]** No pre-registered replication in a game or app context (2022–2026) was found.

### 8.3 Meaningful choice

Qualitative 2024 work (literature review + survey n=30 + video-elicitation interviews n=11, six moral-choice mechanics; *Computers in Human Behavior*, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0747563224002929, **full text not opened**) found players rate **relationship-specific NPC feedback above binary karma meters** — praised feedback "doesn't say this is good or this is bad, it says this is how it affects your relationship with this person" — want visible consequences ("if you have consequences, it's always a better experience"), and experience impact-free dialogue and QTEs as **"a bit emotionally manipulative."** Some found permanently locked pathways more stressful.

**[contested]** Classic experiments show the cheap version mostly works: a non-branching story with immediate choice-specific feedback preserved agency on 4 of 5 measures versus a branching story (Fendt et al. 2012), and agency was higher when foreseeable outcomes differed in *situational content* (Cardona-Rivera et al., AIIDE 2014, n=88). The reconciliation: **illusion of agency preserves felt agency until it is detected; detection is the harm.** Iten, Steinemann & Opwis (CHI 2018) found meaningful choice raised **appreciation, not enjoyment** — a different outcome variable than the skills assume. And practitioners (O'Brien, Game Developer, 2021; Vanaman 2015) argue overt "X will remember that" notices signal linearity whenever the notice is *absent*. **[unverified]** No study links visible choice consequences to real D7/D30 return; replay decisions instead track "the likelihood of seeing something new… versus the effort required to replay" (Wang, Ang & Mitchell, PACM HCI 2021, n=12, https://doi.org/10.1145/3474684).

### 8.4 Earned vs given = informational vs controlling

The operative distinction is not whether the player worked for the reward but **what the reward tells them**. Deci, Koestner & Ryan's 1999 meta-analytic base rates on free-choice intrinsic motivation: engagement-contingent tangible rewards **d=−0.40**, completion-contingent **−0.36**, performance-contingent **−0.28**, **positive feedback +0.33**. A Duolingo-context SEM study (n=162) using Cognitive Evaluation Theory found "the informational significance learners ascribe to virtual rewards positively affects competence satisfaction, and that autonomy and competence satisfaction together affect intrinsic motivation" (Jedel & Palmquist, 2026, doi:10.1145/3772318.3791046, https://doi.org/10.1145/3772318.3791046) **[unverified — full text not opened]**. Two constraints follow: *removing* gamification later also produces a small undermining effect (Bitter et al. 2022, pre-registered), so a reward layer is a permanent commitment or must fade gradually; and gamification's overall effect is modest anyway — a 2024 meta-analysis (35 interventions, ~2,500 participants) found g=0.257 for intrinsic motivation and g=0.277 for competence.

**Skill implication.** (1) Treat the **pre-outcome window** as the designed unit — name the cue, its duration, and what expectation it sets — and make reveal pacing player-configurable (savor vs skip) with a small consequential micro-choice where one fits. (2) Pair visual progress bars with **numeric remaining-steps near the goal** (where visual displays undercount), prefer **"to-go" framing late**, and plan for a mid-journey motivation dip rather than assuming a smooth goal gradient; label endowed-progress numbers as 2006 field evidence pending digital replication. (3) Specify meaningful choice as **foreseeable difference between options + immediate acknowledgment + at least occasional visible downstream consequence + relational rather than moral-score framing**, with an anti-pattern for consequence-free QTEs and fake branches; state plainly that no evidence ties visible consequence to D7/D30 return, and instruct measuring replay starts and next-session return per variant. (4) Rewrite "earned vs given" as **informational vs controlling**: rewards should carry competence information (what improved, why it was earned); avoid engagement-contingent framing ("log in to get X") especially in learning and journaling products; and note that escalating incentives for already-habitual users are wasted, since habitual behaviour becomes insensitive to reward devaluation (see the retention document, psych-8).

## 9. Wellbeing: playtime is the wrong ethics axis

**The nulls.** A registered report with **n=414 Xbox players, 12 weeks, 6 waves, ~100,000 hours of logged play** found no effect of playtime on wellbeing larger than the pre-registered smallest effect of interest (**0.06 scale points per extra daily hour**), with >95% power to declare equivalence (Ballou, Sewall, Ratcliffe, Zendle, Tokarchuk & Deterding, 2024, *Technology, Mind, and Behavior* 5(1), https://psycnet.apa.org/journals/tmb/5/1/28.pdf). A 2025 replication (**n=703 U.S. adults, >140,000 hours across 150 Switch games**, *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 12:241174) found the same null at every timescale — "relationships, if present, dissipate within two hours of gameplay" — while **perceived value of one's own gaming ("gaming life fit") did predict wellbeing**, and recommended "focusing on how players integrate gaming into their lives rather than the amount of time spent."

**The contradicting causal study [contested].** Egami, Hoshino et al. (2024, *Nature Human Behaviour*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11493677/) exploited lottery-based console allocation during Japan's 2020–2022 shortages: **n=97,602 survey respondents, 8,192 in console lotteries**. Console ownership reduced psychological distress by **0.60 SD (Switch)** and **0.12 SD (PS5)**, and raised life satisfaction **0.23 SD (PS5)**; each extra daily hour improved mental health **0.20 SD** and life satisfaction **0.27 SD**, with benefits **diminishing beyond ~3 hours/day**. Authors' caveat: data were collected "during the exceptional circumstances of the COVID-19 pandemic" and "our current estimates may be higher compared with those obtained in pre- or post-pandemic settings."

These are compatible: Egami estimates the effect of **access/ownership** during lockdown; Ballou estimates **marginal hours among established players**. Neither licenses "more play is good" or "more play is bad."

**Clinical boundaries are not time-based.** Gaming-disorder prevalence is ~1–3%, time correlates only moderately with symptoms, and "even daily gaming for multiple uninterrupted hours is clinically unremarkable" (King, Billieux & Delfabbro, 2024, *JBA*, doi:10.1556/2006.2024.00081). The prevalence literature is also contaminated: of the 500 most-cited GD studies, only 1 of 13 that shared materials excluded gambling and roughly 30% of 358 full texts did; **48.7% of English speakers (59.1% of Slovak) read "gambling" as including "gaming"** (Adamkovič, Jin, Martončik & Karhulahti, 2026, *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 13:251452, https://converis.jyu.fi/converis/portal/detail/Publication/279015924?lang=en_GB); 6 of 7 recruited "gaming" patients in a clinical study had sought treatment for gambling. Korean-language products have a measurement advantage here: 게임 and 도박 are distinct terms, so Korean survey data are less affected than English-language data.

**Where the harm evidence actually is: money, not minutes.**
- Loot-box spending correlates with problem gambling at **r=.31**, above virtual card packs (.22) and physical card packs (.15), in an N=1,961 English-speaking Western card-game player survey; **no** association with negative mental health in that dataset (Xiao, Zendle, Petrovskaya, Nielsen & Newall, *Psychol. Addict. Behav.* 40:66–85, https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/en/publications/physical-card-pack-and-especially-video-game-loot-box-spending-ar).
- Early prospective evidence of migration: **+27% odds of gambling initiation per doubling of baseline loot-box spend** (BF10=7.46), and baseline loot-box spend predicted follow-up gambling expenditure (B=0.22, BF10=40.11), controlling for direct-purchase spend — which carried **no** such signal (n=126, ages 19–25, British Columbia, 6 months; Palmer, Brooks & Clark, 2025, *BMC Psychology* 13:459, https://europepmc.org/article/MED/40307922). Small, single-region.
- **[corrected] [contested]** **Spend relative to means, not absolute spend, is the harm-relevant metric, and high-relative-spend players overlap heavily with problem gamblers** — not "loot boxes double distress" (Drummond, Hall, Lowe-Calverley, Garrett & Sauer, 2025, *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 12(7):231264, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12303105/). Full reanalysis — both datasets, the divergence between them, and the problem-gambling control: see 02f §3.

**Session exit belongs on this axis.** Positive disengagement — ending a session voluntarily, with closure — is designable: "disengagement from games should be constructively designed, allowing players to exit play sessions in a self-determined way" (Alexandrovsky, Gerling, Opp, Hahn, Birk & Alsheail, CHI PLAY 2024, 16 interviews + 111-person survey, doi:10.1145/3677066, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00189). A 2025 companion study of 13 children aged 4–9 plus 12 parents found children struggle to exit before reaching closure.

**Skill implication.** Remove every implication that session length or DAU is itself harmful or beneficial, in either direction, and stop defending retention KPIs with wellbeing claims. Replace the time axis with four instrumented checks: **(1) need frustration** — does the design coerce, undermine competence, or manufacture obligation; **(2) life fit** — self-reported regret, play continuing past an intended stop, "was that worth it" prompts; **(3) spend relative to means** — cap exposure, cool off after rapid successive purchases, monitor the share of users at implausibly high monthly spend; **(4) exit quality** — natural stopping points, closure beats, no autoplay "one more" chaining, and a visible structural end-point that matches the player's planned duration. When the skills discuss clinical risk, use ICD-11 functional criteria (impaired control, escalating priority, continued use despite harm), quote GD prevalence only with the contamination caveat, and never use a playtime threshold as a harm heuristic. Classify proposed mechanics **by mechanism** — intermittent reinforcement, loss aversion, sunk cost, near-miss — rather than by product name, so a battle pass, a paid streak freeze or a paid extra life gets the same scrutiny as a loot box when it shares the mechanism (Ivern, Carbonell & Griffiths, 2026, *JBA*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13371737), while stating that most of that mechanism evidence is imported from gambling research and is not causal for games.
