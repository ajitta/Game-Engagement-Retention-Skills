# LiveOps, Lifecycle Mechanics and Channels

The 2025–2026 evidence moves LiveOps advice away from "more events, longer passes, more push" and toward four defensible positions: owned progress should not expire but purchase windows still do; cadence stabilises a long tail rather than reversing decay; absence should cost nothing while return should pay something; and every outbound channel is now filtered by an OS, a regulator, or both. Ad load turns out to be the best-measured lever in the whole set — and the one the three skills currently treat most casually. Where evidence is missing (battle-pass completion, win-back lift, login-calendar A/Bs) this document says so instead of substituting vendor folklore.

## 1. Battle passes in 2025–2026

### 1.1 What the four reference implementations actually do

| Title | Owned progress | Purchase window | Order |
|---|---|---|---|
| Marvel Rivals | never expires | closes at season end | linear |
| Overwatch | current pass expires | seasonal | 5 pickable tracks |
| Fortnite | expires (dated) | seasonal | pick-6 "Unlock Set" |
| Halo Infinite | premium never expires | buy any time | linear, 50 tiers |

**Marvel Rivals is scarcity-preserving, not FOMO-free.** NetEase's own text: "Luxury Battle Passes do not expire… it is not possible to purchase Luxury Battle Pass from the previous seasons." The one re-release (Season 0, 15–29 May 2025) was time-limited, priced at 590 Lattice against the original 490 (+20%), carried "no free rewards in the return Battle Pass", and came with a standing rule that future returns "will span at least two seasons and be priced at a minimum of 20% higher than the original seasonal rate". Chrono Tokens must be re-earned in later seasons (NetEase, 2025, https://www.marvelrivals.com/devdiaries/20250513/40954_1233900.html). Three separate levers preserve urgency underneath a "never expires" headline: re-earned currency, closed purchase window, delayed and marked-up re-release.

**The real 2025–26 innovation is non-linear reward ordering.** Blizzard replaced Overwatch's "100% linear Battle Pass system" with five selectable 8–10 tier tracks plus one skin swap, framed as "Personalization and Player Agency", and stated "the number of tiers… isn't changing and you won't have to work harder to complete your Battle Pass" (Blizzard, 2026, https://overwatch.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24296053/director-s-take-battle-pass-revamp). "Unvaulted Passes" — old passes become permanently available once owned, minus premium currency, Mythic Prisms, prestige titles and Mythic skins — were announced for Season 4 mid-cycle and then delayed to Season 5 for QA. Current passes still expire; only unvaulted ones are permanent. Fortnite made the same move at Chapter 7 (Dec 2025): after Level 1, six additional skins become selectable via an "Unlock Set" button, with the season's rewards still dated.

**The pass economy has drifted to break-even.** Fortnite Chapter 7 Season 4 costs 800 V-Bucks and returns "up to 800 V-Bucks… enough to get the next one at no extra cost" (Epic, 2026, https://www.fortnite.com/battle-pass), versus the earlier 1,000 cost with up to 1,500 earnable. The "the pass pays for itself with change left over" pitch that designers still quote is one generation out of date.

### 1.2 The counterevidence: non-expiry is not consensus, and cohort synchrony is the cost

Halo Infinite Season 5 (Oct 2023) kept premium passes non-expiring but deliberately made the free track expire and moved to 4–6 week "Operations" — explicitly "to move the bulk of Halo Infinite's players forward together, experiencing the same new content at the same time"; free content "will no longer be available to unlock if you haven't already completed those tiers" after 14 Nov 2023 (343 Industries, 2023, https://www.halowaypoint.com/news/battle-pass-operations-season-5). Fully non-expiring content dilutes the shared "everyone is doing X this week" moment that drives social play and content peaks.

Two claims must be struck from any skill text. First, **"64% of players report battle-pass burnout" is a misattribution** — Bain's Gaming Report 2025 (5,243 respondents, six countries, 12 Aug 2025) says 64% report that *ads* interrupt their gaming experience; it contains no battle-pass statistic (Bain, 2025, https://www.bain.com/insights/gamer-survey-great-gameplay-is-no-longer-enough-gaming-report-2025/). Second, **no pass-completion rate, season-over-season repurchase rate or quantified pass-fatigue metric was published by any vendor, first party or academic source for 2024–2026** — a skill that supplies one is inventing it.

Also unmeasured: whether Marvel Rivals' non-expiring pass changed attach rate or D30 at all. NetEase reports only that the feature "has been met with resounding approval".

**Skill implication.** Replace "non-expiring passes are the player-friendly default" with a two-axis spec: (a) owned progress never expires — removes completion anxiety; (b) purchase window and re-release scarcity are retained — keeps buy urgency. Add "choose-your-order tracks + one swap" as the default flexible structure and "permanent unvaulting of past passes, cosmetics only, no currency" as the lapsed-player catch-up. Add a cohort-synchrony line: pair non-expiring paid progress with a short time-boxed free track so the population still moves together. Ban the burnout percentage and any invented completion rate.

## 2. Seasons and cadence

Marvel Rivals cut seasons from ~3 months to 2 months from Season 3 (announced Dev Vision Vol. 5, 2 Apr 2025; effective 11 Jul 2025) with a new hero roughly monthly, against Overwatch 2's roughly one hero per six months (GameSpot, 2025, https://www.gamespot.com/articles/marvel-rivals-shortening-seasons-releasing-a-new-hero-every-month/1100-6530612/). The observed outcome, on Steam-only monthly average concurrents (console excluded): 279,402 (Dec 2024) → 306,066 (Jan 2025) → 82,825 (Jul 2025) → 63,716 (Oct 2025) → 88,790 (Jan 2026) → 65,150 (Mar 2026) → 84,281 (Jul 2026, peak 196,974) (Steam Charts, app 2767030, https://steamcharts.com/app/2767030). A player-friendly pass plus monthly hero cadence coexisted with a ~75% decline from peak and then a stable band at roughly 25–30% of peak held for 12+ months. Cadence compression is a long-tail stabiliser, not a growth lever — and the causal claim in either direction is unverified.

Cadence must be sized to team capacity, and the arrow points both ways. HoYoverse-style gacha runs a fixed 42-day version cadence split into two 21-day banner phases (Genshin v7.0 ran 12 Aug – 22 Sep 2026), with spend spiking on update day — Zenless Zone Zero v2.0 (6 Jun 2025) lifted daily spend +740% to $8.5M and held above pre-update levels for 10 days (AppMagic estimates via PocketGamer.biz, 2025, https://www.pocketgamer.biz/zenless-zone-zero-celebrates-first-anniversary-with-442m/). NetEase's Justice Mobile "periodically climbs back to iOS Top-10 on every major version" (NetEase Q3 2025 call coverage, https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2025-11-21/4152168.html). Arc Raiders moved the opposite way, abandoning frequent small updates for fewer larger ones after early rushed patches "felt underwhelming" **[contested]** — same year, same market, opposite conclusion.

The subtractive case is under-represented in every LiveOps deck. Supercell's GDC 2026 Clash Royale session frames 2021–2025 as a swing "from steep declines to its strongest year yet" on three levers: find the overlap between what players want and what works for the game; simplify to make space to grow the audience; stick to a multi-year strategy over long iterations (GDC 2026 schedule, https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/recent-bets-outcomes-from-supercells-clash-royale/917002). The talk contents themselves were not retrieved **[unverified beyond the session abstract]**.

**Skill implication.** Separate cadence decisions from retention diagnosis: a cadence change is a content-supply decision constrained by team capacity, and should never be prescribed as the fix for a decaying D7. Give three reference cadences — 42-day banner (gacha), 8–9 week season (hero shooter), 4–6 week operation (synchrony-focused) — and require a capacity check before recommending compression. Add "subtractive LiveOps" (remove systems, simplify) as a first-class option alongside additive events.

## 3. Daily/weekly quests and login rewards

The rule that survives all 2025–2026 evidence is **reward return, never punish absence** — and it now has three independent backers.

- PEGI's interactive risk categories, for games submitted from June 2026: "Play-by-appointment: mechanisms that reward returning to the game (e.g. daily quests) will get a PEGI 7. If these mechanisms punish players for not returning (e.g. by losing content or reducing progress) they will become PEGI 12." Time-limited or quantity-limited offers also draw PEGI 12; paid random items draw PEGI 16 by default (PEGI, 2026, http://www.pegi.info/index.php/news/pegi-expands-age-rating-criteria-interactive-risk-categories). Existing titles are not re-rated unless updated content warrants it. ESRB has reportedly said it will not follow **[unverified — trade press only]**.
- The Ethical Games initiative (IGDA Foundation and Fair Play Alliance participation): avoid "daily rewards that work on a cumulative-day basis because it punishes players for not connecting on a specific day" (https://ethicalgames.org/learn-more).
- The Commission's Art. 28(1) DSA guidelines on the protection of minors (14 Jul 2025) recommend disabling by default, for minors, "features that contribute to excessive use, like communication 'streaks', ephemeral content, 'read receipts', autoplay, or push notifications" (European Commission, 2025, https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors). Voluntary, but used to assess compliance.

**The non-resetting long-window bucket is the concrete pattern.** Marvel Snap's 16 Sep 2025 patch added a 30-day "Bonus Challenge" of 50 missions that advances from the *same* daily missions as the weekly challenge: "Players have 30 days to complete the Bonus Challenge, and unlike the Weekly Challenge, it does not reset weekly", inside a 35-day calendar window, explicitly so players who "miss a couple of days… still have the opportunity to earn everything" (Second Dinner, 2025, https://marvelsnap.com/patch-notes-september-16-2025/). This is the citable 2025 instance of a three-layer cadence: dailies set the rhythm, a weekly bucket catches a missed day, a monthly non-resetting bucket catches a missed week.

The Korean and Chinese variants sharpen the picture. Naver Series' 매일 10시 무료 issues one rental ticket at 22:00 daily that expires in 24 hours, with a day-1 welcome bundle and a 22:00 push (브리드컴퍼니 guide, 2021, https://m.blog.naver.com/breatheco/222206168179); Kakao Page's 기다무 플러스 issues 5 tickets at 00:00 and 5 at 12:00, expiring in 12 hours, and does not accumulate. A fixed clock creates a shared appointment; expiry adds loss aversion. Note that China's withdrawn 22 Dec 2023 NPPA draft would have banned daily-login, first-recharge and consecutive-recharge inducement rewards outright — withdrawn, but a signal of regulatory direction.

**Unknown:** no A/B results for login-calendar redesigns, welcome-back calendars or catch-up mechanics were found for 2024–2026; only pattern descriptions exist. Any "welcome-back calendar lifts D7 by X%" claim is unsourced.

**Skill implication.** Specify the three-layer quest stack (daily rhythm → weekly bucket → monthly non-resetting bucket, all fed by the same daily missions) as the default, and state the PEGI 7 / PEGI 12 boundary verbatim so designers know that adding an absence penalty changes their European age rating. Mark expiring free credits as a loss-aversion pattern that is legitimate for content pacing but must not be combined with re-prompting pop-ups (see §6 on 반복간섭). Say explicitly that no login-calendar effect sizes exist.

## 4. Energy, stamina and metered access

Energy is legitimate when it does two jobs the designer can name: **cap session length** (an intentional stopping cue) or **control an economy** (rate-limit a currency or reveal). It is illegitimate when its only function is to sell time back.

Three configurations worth naming:

- **Fixed-interval free reveal + optional acceleration.** Pokémon TCG Pocket refills one pack per 12 hours (two free packs a day), with Wonder Pick stamina on the same 12-hour clock and hourglasses as accelerators (Sensor Tower, 2026, https://sensortower.com/blog/pokemon-tcg-pocket-100-million-downloads; timer specifics from Game8/Dexerto guides, search-level **[unverified against a first-party source]**). Two sessions a day, generous enough to avoid backlash. But the counterexample is in the same title: DeNA disclosed average MAU falling 51M (Jan–Mar 2025) → 39M (Apr–Jun) → 30M (Jul–Sep) → 28M (Oct–Dec 2025) and stated 「ログイン頻度やユーザーの継続率の向上に課題がある」, with the remedy being collection-experience updates and events, not more pack-opening (gamebiz on DeNA Q3 FY3/2026, 2026, https://gamebiz.jp/news/420505). A metered reveal maximises appointments; it does not supply a reason to keep the appointment.
- **Mistake-penalty gate vs volume gate.** Duolingo replaced Hearts with Energy in 2025: energy is spent on *every* lesson, not just mistakes, refills from consecutive correct answers, perfect lessons, friend invites and chests, and recharges in about a day; Super/Max users get unlimited (Duolingo, 2025, https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-energy). Duolingo says beginners were 2× as likely to run out of hearts mid-lesson and that Energy "helps more learners complete lessons" — **no number given**. Independent coverage reports free users getting less learning per day and long-streak users quitting **[contested — vendor claim and user reports point in opposite directions]**. The design lesson is structural: converting a mistake penalty into a total-volume meter changes who is gated (perfect players too) and turns the meter into a monetisation lever.
- **Per-user, per-title wait timer.** 기다리면 무료 (Kakao Page, 2014, copied from 애니팡's 1-heart-per-8-minutes timer): when free tickets run out, one refills after a per-title period, so the wait is personalised per user and per title — the platform frames it as "selling the time you don't wait" rather than selling content. Historical figures, clearly dated: purchase conversion up to 25% on some titles and daily GMV doubling from ~3,000만원 to an average 6,800만원 in the first month (DBR case via 인터비즈, 2019, https://m.blog.naver.com/businessinsight/221625936585). The 2025–26 shape is a quadrant — wait / watch an ad / pay / subscribe — with WEBTOON Premium launched 17 Sep 2025 ($3.99/20+2 coins through $49.99/500+75, ad-free Originals, episode discounts) (WEBTOON, 2025, https://www.webtoons.com/en/notice/detail?noticeNo=3502). **No platform publishes wait-or-pay conversion rates**; Korean webtoon paid-payment experience sits at 45.6% (2023) and 47.2% (2024) of surveyed users (KOCCA user surveys).

**Skill implication.** Give energy a two-axis model (mistake gate vs volume gate; economy control vs session cap) and require the designer to state which job it does before configuring it. Defensible configuration: publish the fairness rule (what consumes, what refills, how fast), refill on a clock the player can predict, keep at least one meaningful free session per day, and never let the meter block the *stopping* action (saving, reviewing, collecting). Flag Energy-style volume meters as monetisation-adjacent and expect backlash risk. Pair any metered reveal with a completion goal (collection, meta objective) — the DeNA case is the cleanest evidence that reveals alone do not sustain MAU.

## 5. Streaks

**Persistence evidence is real and mechanistic.** Streaks work through goal commitment, and they break asymmetrically: across 7 studies with >4,000 participants including language-app field data, highlighting an *intact* streak raised re-engagement while highlighting a *broken* one lowered it — 66.23% vs 57.86% continued the same exercise with an intact vs broken logged streak despite identical underlying behaviour; the drop is amplified when the user feels responsible and reduced when repair is offered (Silverman & Barasch, J. Consumer Research 49(6), 2023, https://knowledge.insead.edu/marketing/consumer-streaks-are-motivating-key-keeping-them-alive). Separately, escalating streak incentives (1c/2c/3c for consecutive tasks) produced more completed tasks than a flat higher per-task payment (6c vs 9c total) across six preregistered experiments, n=4,493 — the effect came from the consecutive-completion requirement raising goal commitment, not from the increasing amounts, and the payout gap must stay small or money dominates (Mehr, Silverman, Sharif, Barasch & Milkman, OBHDP 187:104391, 2025, https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2025/03/hot-streaks-boost-productivity.html). This is a short-task lab paradigm, not months-long habit field data.

**Duolingo's numbers, correctly attributed.** From the 2022 post: learners reaching a 7-day streak are 3.6× more likely to complete their course (**correlational — selection into streaks**); allowing two equipped Streak Freezes raised daily active learners by **+0.38%**; new streak animations raised 7-day retention of brand-new learners by **+1.7%** (the last two described as A/B results) (Duolingo, 2022, https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit). Friend Streak (Aug 2024): learners with at least one shared streak are 22% more likely to complete their daily lesson — vendor observational data (https://blog.duolingo.com/friend-streak). The programme-level figures often mangled into "streak freeze cut churn 21%" are actually CURR +21%, daily churn of best users cut >40%, DAU 4.5×, and the DAU share with a 7+ day streak roughly tripled to more than half — over ~2018–2022 (Mazal in Lenny's Newsletter, 2023, https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth).

**Two figures must never be used.** A blog-circulated "2020 JPSP study: 63% higher abandonment after one missed day" could not be located in any journal and appears fabricated. A gamification vendor's "freeze users average 17.2 vs 11.6 streak days (+48%)" is that vendor's own platform data and is not an independent effect.

**Backfire evidence exists but is thin.** Qualitative work on run-streakers who broke streaks of 100 to 5,000+ days (n=17, US/Canada/Scotland) records sadness, frustration and anger on breaking, a "double downer" when the break coincides with injury, and willingness to run through injury — the authors still conclude streaking supports long-term change "like few other individual techniques" (Dombrowski group, medRxiv preprint, Dec 2024, https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.12.26.24319676v1.full-text). Companion qualitative studies: n=21 runners motivated by anticipated regret; n=27 focus-group participants with all-or-nothing standards choosing complete inaction over a reduced version. **Status: [contested] — all qualitative, small-n, exercise domain, one still a preprint. No quantitative estimate of post-break abandonment in apps exists.**

**Recovery designs that are defensible:** rolling definitions ("4 of 7 days this week") instead of consecutive-day resets; a minimum-viable action that counts (one line, one mood tap) so the all-or-nothing cliff has a floor; free or earnable repair rather than paid-only repair; and status restoration as a return offer (§7). Paid streak repair should be flagged as monetised loss aversion — its wellbeing and spending effects have not been studied.

**Skill implication.** Keep streaks, specify them tightly: surface intact streaks, never shame broken ones in notification copy, prefer rolling windows for learning/journaling/habit products, ship free repair, and default streaks OFF for minors in the EU per the DSA guidelines. Quote only the A/B numbers (+0.38%, +1.7%) as effects and label 3.6× and 22% as correlational. Delete the fabricated JPSP figure and the vendor freeze figure. Carry the backfire evidence as a labelled ethical cost, not as proof that streaks fail.

## 6. Notifications

**Reach is no longer assumable.** Android push opt-in fell across all percentiles between 2023 and 2024 (high/median/low 88.0/71.3/42.1% → 79.7/59.5/37.1%) while iOS stayed flat (73.9/49.1/27.3% → 74.1/49.4/27.1%); median direct open rates were ~3.4% Android and ~3.1% iOS (Airship, Jan–Dec 2024 data published Mar 2025, https://growth.airship.com/rs/313-QPJ-195/images/Airship-2025-Push-Notification-Benchmarks-EN.pdf?version=0). The 2026 edition (681B pushes, 3B users, published 31 Mar 2026) says Android 13+ runtime permissions have "permanently closed" the gap to "near parity", and that Android median and low-tier apps cut send volume 15% YoY. **The "Android 97% / iOS 54%" figures circulating on stat blogs appear nowhere in Airship's text** and likely derive from 2021-era ranges.

**The OS now filters promotional push.** Android 16 QPR2's Notification Organizer (rolling out Dec 2025 on Pixel 9/10, English-only, select regions — Korea not included as of Dec 2025) uses on-device AI to bundle "Promotions" and "News" by message content into a Silent section, enabled by default for those categories; iOS 18 Apple Intelligence adds priority notifications and summaries. 48% of marketers say AI filtering already affects which messages surface and 17% report deliverability impact; behaviour-triggered messages beat standard sends by 4–9× on CTR, and 63% of those using automated journeys "report better results with fewer messages" (OneSignal 2026 State of Customer Engagement, 200,000+ apps, 500+ marketers, https://onesignal.com/blog/the-2026-state-of-customer-engagement-report-is-here/). Whether Notification Organizer expands beyond Pixel/English/six regions, and whether Samsung One UI adopts an equivalent, is **unknown** and directly affects Korean planning.

**Frequency is a retention hazard through opt-out, not through annoyance alone.** Uplift work on push frequency states the mechanism plainly: "inappropriate push frequencies often trigger users to close notification switches, directly harming long-term user retention", validated on an A/B test with over 1 billion users across 14 days (Zheng et al., AAAI 2026, cited in Wei et al., https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.12803). The corollary is that the target is *uplift* (persuadables), not churn probability — pushing the highest-risk users hardest is measurably counter-productive.

**Copy novelty decays; rotate with a recency penalty.** Duolingo's sleeping/recovering bandit over reminder templates — template eligibility as "sleeping arms", a decaying recency penalty as "recovering arms", conversion defined as a lesson within two hours — produced +0.5% DAU and +2% new-user retention *over an already A/B-optimised random-template baseline* (Yancey & Settles, KDD 2020, https://www.kdd.org/kdd2020/accepted-papers/view/a-sleeping-recovering-bandit-algorithm-for-optimizing-recurring-notificatio.html). It remains Duolingo's only published notification study; no newer effect sizes exist.

**Tone is a real lever with a real cost.** Co-Star's product is effectively one daily horoscope push, and its blunt/"trolling" tone is simultaneously its viral engine and its most cited user-harm complaint (Apple App Store listing, 2026, https://apps.apple.com/us/app/co-star-personalized-astrology/id1264782561) **[contested — no controlled evidence that a gentler tone retains better, only that it generates fewer complaints]**. 헬로우봇's claim of 10% push conversion versus a 1.5% industry average comes from its own ~2020 fundraising deck and did not translate into share (≈90K MAU, 4.6% of Korean fortune apps, Feb 2025) **[unverified vendor self-report]**.

**Korea has hard rules, and "your reward expires" is an ad.** KISA's 7th-edition guide to the anti-spam provisions of 정보통신망법 (posted 4 Mar 2026): consent requests may not use vague wording such as '혜택 알림' or '정보제공'; opting out of app-push advertising may not require login or complex steps; and notices about coupons/mileage/points granted unilaterally — including issuance or expiry alerts — require explicit prior consent (KISA, 2026, https://www.kisa.or.kr/401/form?postSeq=3608). Standing 제50조 rules: separate consent for 21:00–08:00 sends, a "(광고)" prefix, consent re-confirmation every two years, 과태료 up to 3,000만원 per violation. Adjacent: Korea's amended E-Commerce Act (in force 14 Feb 2025) bans 반복간섭 — re-asking a consumer (the Oct 2025 KFTC guideline says 2 or more times) to reverse a decision already made unless the prompt offers a "do not ask again for 7+ days" option; the enforcement grace period ended Aug 2025 (KFTC, 2025, https://www.ftc.go.kr/www/selectBbsNttView.do?bordCd=3&key=12&nttSn=46527). Published examples cover marketing-consent and purchase pop-ups; application to in-game "watch an ad?" or "remove ads?" re-prompts is **untested [unverified]**.

**The ≤1/day cap is [contested].** High frequency does not always cause churn when the payload is high-value and content-gated; some engagement-primary products retain well above it. The defensible framing is a per-segment budget with an opt-out guardrail, not a global number.

**Skill implication.** Rewrite notification guidance from "frequency and timing optimisation" to "earn priority": every push must be behaviour-triggered and lead to a concrete in-app payoff, because generic promotional blasts are auto-silenced on newer Android and demoted on iOS. Add a payload typology (value-ready, social, achievement, content-drop, reminder) crossed with segment (new / active / at-risk / lapsed) and product type, replacing the flat ≤1/day rule with a per-segment budget. Add the Duolingo bandit mechanism (rotate templates, penalise recent ones, gate on eligibility) with its modest effect sizes. Add a Korea compliance block: night-send toggle, "(광고)" prefix, one-tap opt-out without login, expiring-reward alerts as regulated advertising, and a ≥7-day snooze on any re-prompt. Default push OFF for minors in the EU.

## 7. Resurrection and win-back

**The evidence here is the thinnest in this document, and the skills must say so.** No 2024–2026 primary dataset on win-back campaign effectiveness was found. Vendor claims of "10–25% of lapsed users recovered" and "5–10× cheaper than acquisition" carry no methodology and must not be quoted. AppsFlyer's remarketing uplift figure for gaming predates 2024. What exists is fragmentary:

- **Restore lost status for a small, bounded effort.** Duolingo's one-time June 2026 "Streak Revival" let lapsed learners restore their longest-ever streak by completing three lessons: 15.4 million revived, including nearly 8 million with no active streak, and the revived cohort retained better than a typical re-engaged cohort; DAU +23% YoY, MAU +10%, CURR at an all-time high (Duolingo Q2 2026 shareholder letter, https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/3c8277ee-bc94-4f5d-9b77-0db3e46f88b8). Same letter set: the Q4 2025 letter attributed decelerating DAU growth partly to "our increased focus on monetization".
- **Remove friction rather than adding bribes.** NetEase revived a 22-year-old MMO by launching 畅玩服 for Fantasy Westward Journey PC — removing time-based fees and simplifying systems — 「召回大量流失用户」 with peak concurrency reaching a record 3.58M (NetEase Q3 2025 coverage, https://www.nbd.com.cn/articles/2025-11-21/4152168.html). This matches Korea's stated top churn cause: KOCCA's 2025 게임이용자 실태조사 (n=10,000, published 18 Dec 2025) attributes non-use mainly to lack of time (44.0%), with video/OTT the substitute (86.3%) (via ZDNet Korea, https://zdnet.co.kr/view?no=20251218172447).
- **Non-push channels reach people push cannot.** In Marvel Rivals' gifting rollout, 41% of purchases were gifts and 25% of gift buyers had never played or had lapsed (Discord, 2026, https://discord.com/blog/building-on-the-social-layer-of-games-whats-new-from-gdc-2026 — **vendor with a commercial interest**). Friend "recall" events that reward both inviter and returner are documented as a pattern (GameRefinery, PUBG Mobile; **no URL captured in the source set**).
- **Publish a schedule against the expected decay curve.** NCSOFT said Aion2 traffic "declined along the expected decay curve" and pre-planned a 6-month anniversary event plus a Season 4 update to pull lapsed users back (FETV concall coverage, 2026, https://www.fetv.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=302274).
- **Email is weak.** Jagex's older GDC "Returners and Retention" talk reported win-back emails at ~16% open and under 2% click-through (**no retrievable URL in the source set — [unverified]**). A social-casino LiveOps vendor case reports a "likely to churn" journey lifting retention 7% and conversions 10%, and a 30% weekly revenue drop when LiveOps events were switched off (Dive, 2023, https://www.dive.games/2023/07/23/25-uplift-in-arpdau-with-segmentation-and-liveops-tools — **vendor, uncontrolled**).
- **Design the return experience, not just the offer.** Games do not recognise that a player is returning rather than continuing; the CHI 2021 "Pivot Point" — a moment that reminds the player of their expert self — is the anchor for a return flow (Hammad et al., CHI 2021, https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3411764.3445357). Telltale added "Clementine will remember that" precisely because "there were six to eight weeks between episodes, so people might not remember that their save file mattered" (Vanaman, Game Developer, 2015). **No experiment on recap systems and returning-player retention or comprehension exists.**

**Re-dormancy is the metric, and no benchmark exists for it.** Because reactivation rates are trivially inflatable by a large bribe, the success criterion for a win-back programme is the share of reactivated users still active at D14/D30 after return, measured against a randomized holdout of equally lapsed users. No published figure exists; each product must build its own baseline.

**Skill implication.** Rewrite the resurrection block to (1) state that no reactivation-rate benchmark exists and refuse to supply one; (2) mandate a randomized holdout of matched lapsed users and re-dormancy at D14/D30 as the primary metric; (3) target by uplift, not churn probability, and model delayed response; (4) rank channels by plausibility — status restoration and friend gift/recall > in-app return calendar > push > email; (5) require a return flow: detect the gap, give a spoiler-safe recap that restates the player's own prior choices, refresh controls, and stage an early expert-self moment before any new content; (6) prefer friction removal (a lower-commitment mode or server) over reward bribes where time scarcity is the stated churn cause.

## 8. Social systems as retention drivers

**Positive evidence, with its caveats attached.**

- Games integrating Discord's Social SDK saw linked players gain a median +25% active game days and +16% sessions ("causal study… 15+ games launched since December 2024"); PC players spent a median 6× longer in-game with one friend present and 8× with three (Dec 2025); voice-channel players play ~66% more days (Discord GDC 2026, https://discord.com/blog/building-on-the-social-layer-of-games-whats-new-from-gdc-2026). **Vendor-run study on self-selected integrating games.**
- A stratified Cox proportional-hazards model with propensity-score matching on 1,204 MMORPG users found both guild and friend systems lengthen retention, with the guild effect stronger than the friend effect (Hwang & Han, SSRN 4606486, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4606486). **[unverified in detail — SSRN returned 403/429; hazard ratios, game identity and exact date unread; working paper, n small.]**
- Korean log data support instrumenting the social graph rather than a membership flag: in NCSOFT Blade & Soul data (10,000 users, Apr 2016–Feb 2017), retained users showed markedly higher party, raid and guild-graph activity, and a dynamic social-graph model over guild/party/raid/trade edges beat static models (accuracy 0.75–0.77, F1 0.65) (Lee & Woo, PLoS One, 2025, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12148118). Correlational; guild effect not isolated.

**The causal peer-effect study cuts the other way on retention.** An instrumental-variable study of 108 million users on the largest PC game platform found strong causal peer effects of friends on *adoption* — long-standing friends more influential than network "key players" — but "players influenced by either key players or old friends subsequently spend less time playing compared to those without peer influence" (arXiv 2409.14351, 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14351). Social acquisition and social retention are different products.

**Social features are not unconditionally positive.** In a Cox survival analysis of 295,008 League of Legends players, social connectedness reduced churn overall, but playing with teammates was associated with lower win rates, which independently raise churn; churn probability rises about 8% after 60 days and 20% after 90 days, with role-based variation (Kim, Kim, Kim & Li, Sport, Business and Management, 2026, https://snu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/analyzing-player-churn-in-esports-games-a-survival-analysis-of-le). And co-play without a meta layer produces spikes, not D30: friend co-op hits without meta progression average ~3% D30 (Content Warning 3%, Ball x Pit 4.7%, Phasmophobia 5.3%) against 11.3% for Dead by Daylight — "Fast fun, and fast churn" (AppMagic via GameDev Reports, 2026, https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-friendslop-games-in-2025).

**Team punishment is the line not to cross.** A systematic analysis of the top 40 Korean mobile games classifies "social dynamics" as a retention/monetisation strategy: "Players often feel compelled to keep up with their guild members or peers, which can reinforce the sunk cost effect… guilds compete for limited rewards [and] players are driven to invest more time and resources to maintain standing", and flags "exploitation of competitive pressures" as poorly regulated (Lee, Imteyaz & Savage, arXiv 2504.10714, 2025 — **preprint**, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10714). The commercial version of this loop is not proven to retain: Last War: Survival's alliance-obligation design coexists with US iOS D1/D7/D30 of 34/11/4 against Whiteout Survival's 42/17/8 (Sensor Tower via Naavik, 2025, https://naavik.co/digest/how-last-war-is-winning-the-4x-game), while Korea is 18–23% of its lifetime revenue and it was Korea's #2 top-grossing mobile game of 2024. **The "alliance obligation drives retention" thesis is analyst assertion with no measurement behind it.**

Toxicity is the one social-side mechanism with a clean causal estimate: exposure to toxic chat increased time to next match by 16.18–60.68 hours against a 3.66–4.17 hour baseline (56,464,489 observations, 4,167,325 matches, 4,539,599 players, Call of Duty: MWIII Team Deathmatch, 10 Nov – 10 Dec 2023, 2SLS leave-one-out instruments), largest for opponent toxicity after a loss; same-party (friend) toxicity had no significant engagement effect but the strongest contagion (Morrier, Mahmassani & Alvarez, PLoS One, 2025, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12157061).

**Skill implication.** Split the social block into acquisition and retention. Referral/invite loops drive installs but the invited cohort under-indexes on playtime, so they must be paired with co-play retention loops and never counted as retention wins. Make guild-join (not friend-add) the primary social activation milestone, log guild/party/raid/trade edges as time-varying churn features, and require a progression or meta layer before predicting D30 from social design. Add a hard test for social-obligation patterns: if a player's absence costs their guild or alliance (missed rallies, lost ranking), it is a sunk-cost/competitive-pressure pattern and needs an opt-out or grace mechanism. Add "time-to-next-session after a toxic-exposure match" as a churn leading indicator and segment churn dashboards by gender and identity, since withdrawal is demographically skewed.

## 9. UGC platforms

Roblox reached 144M DAU (+69% YoY) and 35B hours (+88%) in Q4 2025, FY2025 bookings $6.8B (+55%), with users engaging over 24 unique experiences per month and experiences outside the top 10 supplying more than half of Q4 Robux-spend growth; a 2025 Creator Rewards programme pays creators specifically for retention and engagement, and age-checking produced a mid-single-digit engagement headwind, mostly from less-engaged users (Roblox Q4 2025 shareholder letter, https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Q4-2025-Shareholder-Letter.pdf). The 2025 Roblox benchmark (47% of platform engagement) shows discovery amplifying experiences that drive friend invites and "intentional co-play", core-user cadence up 33% YoY, and retention scaling with session-length band: median D1 4.3% / D7 0.41% for 0–3 minute experiences versus D1 11.46% / D7 1.61% for 19–24 minute ones; median session 6.62 min, 2.02 sessions/day (GameAnalytics, https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-roblox-report). Against the all-mobile median D1 of ~22% (GameAnalytics 2026, 16,000+ games, ≥1,000 MAU, 2025 calendar data), applying mobile benchmarks to UGC experiences is an order-of-magnitude error.

Fortnite Creative makes retention a direct revenue input. Since 1 Nov 2025 the engagement payout formula has five components — Active Playtime; **Island Retention** (changed from ecosystem-wide to island-specific); Playtime Surrounding Item Shop V-Bucks Spend; UA Rewards: New Players; UA Rewards: Returning Players — with creators receiving 75% of a new player's or a 6-month-lapsed player's pool contribution for six months, and only engagement from players who have ever made a real-money purchase counted (Epic, https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite-creative/engagement-payouts-in-fortnite-creative). Attribution caveats matter: direct links attribute on PC for new players and PC + mobile for returning players, are not supported in the EU, and Discover-sourced players split day-one credit across every island played.

**Skill implication.** Add a UGC-platform branch: benchmark by session-length band rather than against mobile medians; treat friend-invite and co-play design as a *distribution* lever because platform discovery amplifies it; and for Fortnite Creative, state that island-specific retention and returning-player acquisition are literally payout terms, so the retention plan is the revenue plan. Note the age-check engagement headwind as a documented safety-versus-engagement trade-off rather than a design failure.

## 10. Personalization and churn-risk targeting

**The strongest field evidence in this document is for difficulty relief, not for pricing.** A 12-week randomized rollout of dynamic difficulty adjustment against a holdout in a free-to-play mobile puzzle game (300,000+ players) found that easing puzzles for high-churn-risk users raised Retention-1/7/14 by +2.7 / +2.5 / +2.0 percentage points (p<0.001) and rounds played by +1.25, and — contrary to the firm's expectation — increased premium spending in both the short and longer run, for a net +$0.07 per average user of which only 18% came from ads. Purchases fell in the eased round itself, which is why the naive per-round read is misleading (Ascarza, Netzer & Runge, IJRM accepted 2024, https://www.columbia.edu/~on2110/Papers/Personalized_games.pdf). This directly contradicts the "pinch points are needed to monetize" logic that also underlies aggressive ad gating. King separately reports AI now pre-tests and retunes all 18,700+ Candy Crush levels (May 2025), which is the same lever at content scale.

**Trust is the binding constraint on everything else.** Braze's 2026 Customer Engagement Review (2,200 marketing execs, 4,000 US/UK consumers, 779 companies / 6B profiles, published 24 Feb 2026): 93% of marketing leaders believe AI helps them understand customers versus 53% of consumers who feel brands predict their wants; 43% of consumers would stop engaging entirely if their data were misused; 27% refuse to share any data with AI agents; consumers are 30% more likely to stay loyal when needs are accurately predicted (https://www.braze.com/press-releases/the-2026-braze-customer-engagement-review-ai-innovation-meets-the-trust-plateau).

**Targeting method matters more than model accuracy.** Target persuadables by uplift rather than ranking by churn probability, and model delayed response — the same literature that establishes this also shows push-frequency mis-targeting causing permanent opt-outs (Wei et al., arXiv 2408.12803, 2024; Zheng et al., AAAI 2026).

**Personalized *probability* is legally hazardous in Korea.** The amended 게임산업진흥에 관한 법률 took effect 1 Aug 2025: operators that breach probability-item disclosure duties owe damages up to three times the loss, and the burden of proving absence of intent or negligence shifts onto the company, with a 게임이용자 피해구제센터 established (뉴스포스트, 2025, https://www.newspost.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=216334). Any dynamic or personalized drop rate in a Korean-market event is exposed; fixed, disclosed rates plus audit logs are the safe configuration.

**Skill implication.** Frame AI LiveOps personalization as difficulty and content tuning (evidence-backed, with the Ascarza percentage-point deltas and population stated) rather than dynamic pricing or dynamic odds (legally and ethically exposed). Require that personalization be explainable to the user and opt-outable, and prohibit it entirely for price discrimination in sensitive domains. Replace churn-risk ranking with uplift targeting in the retention playbook. Add the Korean 3× damages rule as a hard gate on any personalized-probability idea.

## 11. Ad load versus retention

**The best-identified elasticity comes from outside games and must be labelled as an analogy.** A 21-month randomized ad-load experiment on Pandora (~35M listeners, nine arms crossing 3/4/6 breaks per hour with 1/1.5/2 ads per break, 2014–2016) found demand falls linearly in ads per hour: one additional ad per hour cut mean listening time by 2.08% (SE ~0.12–0.23%), and the highest-load arm ended with ~2% fewer weekly actives. The decomposition is the load-bearing part — of the final-month hours decline, ~40% came from users no longer active at all, ~42% from fewer active days, and only ~18% from shorter sessions. Treatment effects kept growing and stabilised only after 12–15 months, so **a one-month test understates ad-load churn by about 3×**. Higher ad load also raised paid ad-free subscriptions (Goli, Huang, Reiley & Riabov, QME 2025 / arXiv 2412.05516, https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05516). A companion paper found personalizing ad load across users raised subscription profit ~7% without lowering ad profit (Goli, Reiley & Zhang, Marketing Science 2024).

**A mobile-context replication of the shape.** A 66-day user-level RCT on an Android app store (>5M users, 26M searches; control = 1 sponsored slot, treatments 2–6) found revenue per user up to +43% at 6 slots, total conversions per user down up to 5%, and daily engagement down 2.2% with a significant downward trend in the 4–6 slot arms; a deployed adaptive per-context policy (22.3M users, 22 days) matched a fixed 5-ad policy's revenue with ~3% more conversions (Rashid & Yoganarasimhan, arXiv 2607.14418, 2026 — **working paper, not peer-reviewed**, https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14418).

**Store policy is a compliance floor beneath any A/B result.** Google Play's Better Ads Experiences policy (effective 30 Sept 2022, still current) prohibits full-screen interstitials that appear unexpectedly — at the start of a level or content segment, before the splash screen, or after the user chose another action — and requires interstitials to be closeable within 15 seconds; explicitly opted-in rewarded ads and post-score-screen interstitials are exempt, and child-directed apps must allow skipping interfering ads within 5 seconds (Google, https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en). Level-start, app-open-video and mid-action interstitials are violations regardless of what the test says; the compliant canonical placement is after the score/result screen.

**Rewarded video is a designable reward moment; its retention numbers are not causal.** Unity's own 2022 study across 8 high-DAU apps reports engagers were 4.5× more likely to make an IAP, spend rose 326% in the 7 days after first rewarded engagement, and D30 retention ≥50% for users completing one rewarded ad in week 1 versus a 13% all-app benchmark — **all self-selected, no randomization** (https://unity.com/blog/understanding-the-impact-of-rewarded-ads-on-iap-retention-and-engagement). The actionable number from Unity's 2024 report is the placement contrast: 38.1% engagement for context-sensitive placements when a player runs out of resources versus 23.8% between levels, with games running >15 placements reaching up to 46%; top reward types are gacha 31.1%, extra moves 30.5%, daily rewards 30.3% (via PocketGamer.biz and GameDev Reports; **primary report gated**).

**A causal, if small, study on ad framing.** In a between-subjects experiment with 95 children aged 9–11, forced static interstitials after each level lowered both fun and in-game performance (active play 343s versus 627s in control, because the interstitial occupied about half the session), while an optional rewarded video that let players avoid demotion scored the highest FunQ mean (66.9, SD 16.4) — above the no-ads control (Fitton, MacKenzie & Read, IDC 2024, https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/229351/). Small single-game lab study; the authors call the differences small.

**Heterogeneity breaks single-cap thinking [contested].** One mobile game's user-level data show a U-shaped relation between ad aversion and IAP — high- and low-aversion users both buy more than moderate ones (Bae, Yang & Ryu, ICIS 2024, https://aisel.aisnet.org/icis2024/user_behav/user_behav/25). Airbridge's 2025 interstitial-frequency experiments across three games moved total revenue up, up and down for the same intervention direction depending on which segment reacted: Game A more interstitials → total up (churning non-payers watched more ads); Game B fewer interstitials → IAP significantly up, total up (whales stayed); Game C more interstitials → IAP down, total down (whales left). No absolute numbers disclosed (**vendor**). A separate practitioner case reports introducing interstitials only for non-paying players active >1 month gave +23% ad ARPDAU with no retention harm, while the same publisher's earlier untargeted attempt cut retention with no revenue gain (GameBiz Consulting via PocketGamer.biz, 2025).

**Ad-load scheduling by progression, and the first-ad question.** Korean practitioner guidance recommends progression-indexed cooldowns — 180s+ early, 60–120s mid-game, 30–60s for committed players — with rewarded-first, post-success-only triggers for new players; one hypercasual studio reportedly raised new-user ad revenue 45.4% "while maintaining retention" by moving first interstitials earlier for selected segments (AB180/Airflux, 2025, https://airflux.ai/blog/korea-business-symposium-ad-optimization — **vendor case, low confidence; the same post's unsourced claim that "aggressive early ads reduce retention 40–60%" must never be quoted**). Observed first-interstitial timing across three hybrid-casual hits ranges 1 to 12 minutes (mean 5.6): Pizza Ready! (Supercent, Korea) introduces its first interstitial at minute 3 as a non-declinable "Get $30" reward pause, immediately followed by a No-Ads pack, then interstitials roughly every 90s each with a minor reward, alongside a reported D1 >35% and CPI ~$0.20; My Perfect Hotel's first forced interstitial is at minute 12 with an estimated D1 ~55% (Wehrmann, HAW Hamburg master's thesis, 2025 — **not peer-reviewed**, https://reposit.haw-hamburg.de/bitstream/20.500.12738/19148/1/MA_Design_and_development_of_a_hybrid_casual_game.pdf).

**Why "delay ads until hooked" is not free.** Ad revenue is front-loaded: hypercasual games earn 63% of their Day-60 ad revenue on Day 1, and across multi-stream accounts ad revenue's share fell from ~63% to ~56–57% while subscriptions doubled 4%→7% and IAP held ~35% (AppsFlyer, State of App Monetization 2026, $900M verified IAP / $800M subscription / $7.2B IAA, Jan 2025–Mar 2026, https://www.appsflyer.com/resources/reports/app-marketing-monetization-report). Meanwhile "remove ads" is a poor headline SKU — it contributed only 3.7% of Marble Sort's revenue against 85.5% from core bundles (AppMagic via gamey.kr, 2026) — while tiered and temporary ad-free (7/30-day passes, subscriptions bundling ad skips, 24–48h ad-free bought with earned currency) is the 2025 pattern; the "ad skips earn up to 10x per skip versus a rewarded impression" figure is an analyst estimate without disclosed data (Gamesforum × AdInMo, 2024, https://26655840.fs1.hubspotusercontent-eu1.net/hubfs/26655840/Gamesforum%20AdInMo%20Hybrid%20Monetisation%20Trends%20Report%20Updated.pdf).

Finally: perceived interruption and commercial effectiveness are rising together — 64% of players say ads interrupt their experience (+5pp YoY) while 46% often buy because of in-game ads (+6pp) (Bain, 5,243 respondents, six countries, 2025). "Players hate ads" is not evidence that ads reduce spend.

**Skill implication.** Rewrite ad guidance as: (1) ad load is a price on the free tier whose churn cost appears on the *sessions-initiated and active-days* margin, not session length; (2) short A/B tests systematically understate that cost, so require long-lived holdout cohorts and treat 1–2 week interstitial tests as directional only; (3) the compliance floor (post-result placement, 15s close, 5s for child-directed) precedes any optimisation; (4) ad-free IAP uptake is not evidence that ad load is healthy — ad pain drives it; (5) prefer segment-adaptive frequency (cap or gate forced ads for likely payers, allow more for predicted non-payers) over one studio-wide cap, and report total revenue decomposed into interstitial, rewarded and IAP plus retention by payer-propensity segment; (6) treat the rewarded ad at a resource-out pinch point as a designed reward moment, but note the CHI 2024 caveat that a non-declinable "reward" is not a choice.

## New reference module: LiveOps cadence

The plugin currently has no LiveOps cadence content — passes, quests, login, energy, streaks, notifications and win-back are mentioned as ethics risks but never specified. Proposed new reference file: `retention-strategy-designer/references/liveops-cadence.md`. Each spec below is what a designer must be able to fill in, plus acceptance bounds a reviewer can check.

**Pass spec.** Fields: season length; tier count; free-vs-premium split; price and earnable-currency return; owned-progress expiry; purchase-window expiry; re-release policy; track structure (linear vs pickable); catch-up mechanic. Acceptance bounds: owned progress never expires; purchase window may close; any re-release states its markup and delay up front; free track may be time-boxed for cohort synchrony; earnable return stated as a ratio of price (break-even is now normal, not a bug); tier count unchanged when track structure changes; no absence penalty anywhere. Reviewer flags: any pass-completion or repurchase target quoted as an industry number (none exist).

**Quest spec.** Fields: daily set size and reset time; weekly bucket; monthly non-resetting bucket; whether one action advances multiple layers; expiry of each layer. Acceptance bounds: dailies feed the weekly and monthly buckets from the same actions; the longest bucket does not reset weekly and spans ≥30 days inside a ≥35-day window; a player missing 2 consecutive days can still complete the monthly bucket; no cumulative-consecutive-day requirement anywhere in the stack.

**Login spec.** Fields: reward curve; reset rule; grace window; catch-up rule; minor-mode default. Acceptance bounds: non-resetting cumulative-count calendars (day 1..N by visits, not by dates) rather than consecutive-day calendars; a missed day costs zero previously-earned value; grace window ≥1 day before any progress pause; PEGI check — if absence removes content or reduces progress the design is PEGI 12 in Europe; default off for minors in the EU.

**Energy spec.** Fields: what consumes it; what refills it and how fast; free sessions per day at full refill; what it gates and what it never gates; paid bypass; unlimited tier. Acceptance bounds: state the job (session cap or economy control) before configuring; refill clock is predictable and published; at least one meaningful free session per day; never gates saving, reviewing, collecting or reading already-owned content; if the meter counts successful actions (volume gate) rather than failures, say so publicly and expect backlash.

**Streak spec.** Fields: unit definition (consecutive days vs N-of-7); minimum qualifying action; repair mechanism and its price; break-notification copy; milestone schedule; minor default. Acceptance bounds: rolling window preferred outside daily-cadence games; minimum action is small enough to survive a bad day; repair is free or earnable at least once per period; notification copy never references a broken streak; early milestones at day 3/7/14 (streak value is proportional — day 2→3 is +50%, day 200→201 is +0.5%); paid-only repair flagged as monetised loss aversion; default off for minors in the EU.

**Notification spec.** Fields: payload type; trigger (behavioural, not calendar); segment; delivery window; per-segment weekly budget; template pool and rotation rule; in-app payoff; opt-out path. Acceptance bounds: every push names its concrete in-app destination; behaviour-triggered rather than broadcast (OS filtering demotes the latter); templates rotate with a recency penalty; budget set per segment with an opt-out guardrail rather than a global ≤1/day; Korea — separate 21:00–08:00 consent, "(광고)" prefix, one-tap opt-out without login, expiring-reward alerts treated as advertising, ≥7-day snooze on any re-prompt; EU minors — off by default.

**Win-back spec.** Fields: dormancy definition; segment; channel; offer; return flow; holdout; success metric. Acceptance bounds: a randomized holdout of matched lapsed users is mandatory; primary metric is re-dormancy (share still active at D14 and D30 post-return), not reactivation rate; targeting by uplift, not churn probability; the return flow includes gap detection, a spoiler-safe recap restating the player's own prior choices, a controls refresher and an early expert-self moment; status restoration and friend gift/recall ranked above push and email; no benchmark reactivation figure is supplied anywhere in the module.

**Cross-cutting checks the module should carry.** A calendar block for time-zone and cultural cadence (Korean school breaks, 수능 season, 설날 and 추석, exam periods) since these move retention baselines and intervention windows; a measurement note that N-day and unbounded retention are different constructs and that Korean MMPs such as Airbridge count Day-1 by calendar date (an 8pm install and an 8am open next day counts as retained); and a standing "numbers that do not exist" list — battle-pass completion and repurchase rates, pass-fatigue percentages, win-back lift, login-calendar A/B results, 기다리면 무료 conversion rates, and Korea-specific D1/D7/D30 cohort benchmarks — so the skills refuse to generate them.
