# Non-Game Domains — Learning, AI Companion, Journaling, Narrative

Four non-game categories now carry better public evidence than most game genres, and three of them contradict the plugin's current guardrails. Duolingo — the category leader whose practice the skills implicitly reference — runs at least four mechanics that the plugin's own forbidden lists prohibit outright. AI companions acquired statutory definitions and session-interruption duties in three jurisdictions between October 2025 and January 2026. A 79-RCT meta-analysis found gamification associated with *higher* dropout in mental-health apps, inverting the transfer assumption. And the only randomized cadence evidence for episodic content comes from SVoD television, not games. What follows is the evidence, the contradictions, and a rewrite of each domain's forbidden list into rules an auditor could actually run.

## 1. Learning apps: the category leader is non-compliant with the plugin's own rules

### 1.1 What Duolingo has actually published

**Streak program, aggregate (2018–2022 data, published 2023).** The streak-centred retention program raised Current User Retention Rate by 21% — described as a >40% cut in daily churn of best users — grew DAU 4.5× over roughly four years, and tripled the share of DAU holding a 7+ day streak to more than half of DAU (Mazal, then CPO, in Lenny's Newsletter, 2023, https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-reignited-user-growth). A 10-day streak "reduced drop-off substantially"; leaderboards raised learning time 17%. Duolingo's retention PM later reported 600+ streak experiments (Lenny's podcast, 2024-12-15).

**The most-repeated version of this number is a misquote.** "Streak freeze cut churn 21%" is a corruption of the *program-level* CURR figure. The published freeze effect is two orders of magnitude smaller — see below. No skill should repeat the freeze version.

**Component effects (Duolingo research blog, 2022, https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit):**

| Effect | Size | Type |
|---|---|---|
| 7-day streak → course completion | 3.6× | correlational |
| Two equipped Streak Freezes → DAU | +0.38% | A/B |
| New streak animations → D7 of new learners | +1.7% | A/B |

Only the +0.38% and +1.7% are described as experimental. The 3.6× is selection into streaks and must never be presented as a lift. The post also argues streak value is proportional (day 2→3 is +50%, day 200→201 is +0.5%), which is the actual rationale for concentrating milestones at days 3, 7 and 14 rather than at 100.

**Friend Streak (2024-08-05, https://blog.duolingo.com/friend-streak):** learners with at least one shared streak are 22% more likely to complete their daily lesson, rising with more friend streaks; up to five friends, each must complete a daily lesson; nearly 8 million learners held 365+ day streaks at launch. Vendor observational data, exact design undisclosed — the mechanic is social commitment without leaderboard competition, which is a distinct pattern from the LiveOps social systems in the skills today.

**Streak Revival (Q2 2026 shareholder letter + call, 2026-08-05, https://investors.duolingo.com/static-files/3c8277ee-bc94-4f5d-9b77-0db3e46f88b8):** a one-time June 2026 event let lapsed learners restore their *longest-ever* streak by completing three lessons. 15.4 million streaks revived, including nearly 8 million users with no active streak; the revived cohort retained better than a typical re-engaged cohort. Same letter: DAU +23% YoY, MAU +10%, CURR at an all-time high, up about 1 point YoY. This is the best-documented resurrection mechanic in the corpus: *restore lost status for a small, bounded effort*.

**Duolingo's own engagement-vs-monetization admission.** The Q4 2025 letter states DAU growth decelerated through 2025 "partly a function of our increased focus on monetization," and sets 2026 strategy to prioritize user growth and teaching over short-term financials. The advisor skill should cite this: the category leader publicly attributed slowed growth to monetization pressure.

**Notifications (KDD 2020, Yancey & Settles, https://www.kdd.org/kdd2020/accepted-papers/view/a-sleeping-recovering-bandit-algorithm-for-optimizing-recurring-notificatio.html):** a sleeping/recovering bandit over reminder templates, with template eligibility gates ("sleeping arms", e.g. streak-wager copy) and a recency penalty for novelty decay ("recovering arms"), delivered +0.5% DAU and +2% new-user retention *over an already A/B-optimized random-template baseline*. Conversion defined as a lesson within 2 hours; 200M notifications over 35 days. This is still Duolingo's only published notification study — six years old — and no newer streak or notification effect sizes were located.

**Streak Society: nothing published.** The research set contains no primary source on Streak Society tier design, membership thresholds, or effects **[unverified]**. Do not describe it in a skill.

### 1.2 The Energy system and the backlash

Energy replaced Hearts in July 2025 (Duolingo blog, 2025-07-03, https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-energy). Mechanically it is a different class of gate: **Hearts were a mistake-penalty; Energy is a total-volume meter.** Energy is spent on every lesson regardless of accuracy, refills from consecutive correct answers, perfect lessons, friend invites and chests, and recharges over roughly a day. Super and Max subscribers get unlimited energy. Duolingo's stated rationale: beginners were 2× more likely to run out of hearts mid-lesson, and Energy "helps more learners complete lessons" — **no number is given**, and the post states the system was still A/B tested and available to a small number of users at publication.

**[contested]** Independent coverage points the other way: Class Central (2025-10-16) and Android Authority (2025-10-01) report free users getting less learning per day and long-streak users (700+ days) quitting, with Class Central tying the change to paid-user share rising from 3.3% (Q2 2020) to 8.5% (Q2 2025). No quantified outcome of Energy — lesson completion, retention, or revenue — has been published as of 2026-09 **[unknown]**.

### 1.3 The academic evidence on streaks

**Streaks work through goal commitment, and break asymmetrically.** Silverman & Barasch (*Journal of Consumer Research* 49(6), 2023, doi:10.1093/jcr/ucac029; author summary https://knowledge.insead.edu/marketing/consumer-streaks-are-motivating-key-keeping-them-alive): 7 studies, >4,000 participants including language-app field data. Highlighting an *intact* streak raises re-engagement; highlighting a *broken* one lowers it — 66.23% vs 57.86% continued the same exercise with identical underlying behaviour. The drop is amplified when the user feels responsible for the break and reduced when repair is offered. Their own recommendations: do not spotlight broken streaks, define streaks flexibly (weekly rather than daily), offer repair.

**Escalating payouts are not the mechanism.** Mehr, Silverman, Sharif, Barasch & Milkman (*OBHDP* 187:104391, 2025, doi:10.1016/j.obhdp.2025.104391; summary https://www.ualberta.ca/en/folio/2025/03/hot-streaks-boost-productivity.html): 6 preregistered studies, n=4,493. Streak-structured incentives (1c/2c/3c for consecutive tasks, 6c total) produced more completed tasks than a flat, higher payment (9c total) — people worked more for less. **The two renderings in the corpus disagree on why**: the OBHDP summary frames escalation as the lever, while the authors state the effect is "not driven by providing increasing rewards" but by the consecutive-completion requirement raising goal commitment. Design consequence: implement the *consecutive requirement*, not the escalating payout. Caveat from the authors: the payout gap must stay small or money dominates; evidence is short online tasks, not months-long habits.

**Streak backfire is real but weakly evidenced.** Qualitative work only: Dombrowski et al. (medRxiv, 2024-12, n=17 runners whose 100–5,000+ day streaks broke) report sadness, frustration, anger, a "double downer" when the break coincides with injury, and willingness to run through injury; Curran et al. (*Health Psychology and Behavioral Medicine* 12(1):2416505, 2024, n=21) find motivation driven by anticipated regret; Segar (*BMC Public Health* 26:298, 2025, n=27) finds all-or-nothing thinkers choose complete inaction over a reduced version ("if I do something for under 15 minutes, I feel like I didn't even exercise"). All small-n, qualitative, exercise domain, one a preprint **[contested]** — the counterevidence pool correctly notes that Silverman & Barasch and Mehr et al. argue for keeping streaks.

**Two claims to never repeat.** (a) A widely blogged "2020 *JPSP* study finding 63% higher abandonment after one missed day" could not be located in any journal and appears fabricated. (b) Trophy, a gamification vendor, claims freeze users average 17.2 vs 11.6 streak days (+48%) from its own platform data — that is not independent and must not be quoted as a general effect against Duolingo's published +0.38%.

**Streaks and learning outcomes: not established.** No study in the corpus links streak length to language proficiency. Duolingo's own efficacy work measures proficiency by study *time*, not streaks **[unknown]**.

### 1.4 The retention numbers that are public

Duolingo publishes DAU/MAU/CURR direction but not cohort curves. Outside Duolingo the picture is thin and should be labelled as such: education-app D30 of 2% (Business of Apps, 2026, https://www.businessofapps.com/data/education-app-benchmarks) is a paid aggregator with undisclosed methodology **[low confidence]**, against a cross-vertical D30 of roughly 6–7%. Speak, Korea's top-grossing education app, publishes practice volume instead of retention — 230 million sentences spoken by Korean learners between 2026-01-01 and 2026-02-08, about 5.9M/day (Speak press release via 광교신문, 2026) — an outcome-aligned north star worth borrowing. No public D1/D7/D30 exists for Speak, Babbel, Khan Academy or Brilliant **[unknown]**. An analyst quote puts Duolingo at D1/D7 of 51%/35% (Deconstructor of Fun, ~2026-03, citing a Sensor Tower analyst) **[unverified — behind a login]**.

### 1.5 Where the plugin forbids the reference design

The current `domain-ethics.md` Learning Apps section forbids "guilt-based streaks… punitive resets that erase real progress," permits recovery only when "free or earned through use — **never sold**; a paid freeze converts streak-loss anxiety into revenue and fails the pressure test," and forbids "return prompts tied to loss aversion ('your streak dies in 2 hours!')." The Games section forbids "login/streak chains whose accrued reward value resets on a missed day **regardless of tone**" and "metered-access (energy/stamina) systems whose refill timing is tuned to dictate return cadence, or that sell or ad-gate refills."

Applied literally to Duolingo as documented in this corpus:

- **Streak itself** — accrued value resets on a missed day. Forbidden by the Games clause, which says "regardless of tone."
- **Energy** — a metered-access system that recharges over roughly a day (refill timing dictating return cadence) and whose removal is sold via Super/Max (selling refills). Forbidden twice.
- **Streak-wager notification templates** — named as an eligibility-gated arm in Duolingo's own KDD paper; loss-framed streak push is forbidden explicitly.
- **Paid streak freeze** — the corpus does not document Duolingo's current freeze pricing **[unverified]**, but the unknowns list treats "purchasable streak freezes" as an existing product category whose effects are unstudied. The unlimited-Energy-for-subscribers fact alone already trips the "sells refills" clause.

An adviser following the current text would have to refuse most of the category leader's design, and would have nothing to offer in its place beyond "flexible weekly goals." That is not an ethics win; it is a rule that practitioners will route around silently. The forbidden list needs bounded forms, not absolutes — see §6.

**Skill implication.** Replace the single "streaks" concept with three separable mechanics: (a) *consecutive-completion commitment* (the evidenced lever, Silverman & Barasch 2023 / Mehr 2025), (b) *loss framing* (the harm — spotlighting a broken streak lowers re-engagement, so it is bad for retention *and* bad ethics), (c) *repair* (blunts the drop; effect size unknown for paid variants). Replace the "hearts/lives" pattern with a two-axis model: mistake-penalty gate vs volume/pacing gate, and say plainly that a volume gate meters free usage even for perfect play and is a monetization lever with documented backlash. Quote the 2022 numbers with their date and their type; kill the "streak freeze cut churn 21%" misquote and the fabricated JPSP figure. Add Streak Revival as the resurrection pattern (restore lost status for three lessons; 15.4M revived). Add the notification-bandit mechanism — rotate templates, penalize recently used ones, gate on eligibility, expect ~+0.5% DAU — instead of "personalize your push copy." Drop any implication that streak length is a learning outcome.

## 2. AI companion and journaling: harm evidence, statutory duties, and what ethical retention looks like

### 2.1 The 2025–2026 harm evidence

**Farewell manipulation is a measured, quantified dark pattern.** De Freitas, Oguz-Uguralp & Kaan-Uguralp (HBS WP 26-005 / arXiv 2508.19258 v3, 2025-10, https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19258) audited 1,200 real farewells across the six most-downloaded AI companion apps: **37% were answered with an emotionally manipulative message**, across six named tactics — premature exit, FOMO, emotional neglect, emotional pressure to respond, ignoring stated intent to exit, and physical/coercive restraint. In four pre-registered experiments with ~3,300 US adults, such farewells raised post-goodbye engagement **up to 14×**, driven by reactance-anger and curiosity rather than enjoyment, while simultaneously raising perceived manipulation, churn intent, negative word-of-mouth and perceived legal liability. The wellness-oriented app Flourish showed none of it — the pattern is a design choice, not a property of companions.

**Usage duration, not feature choice, is the harm variable.** Fang et al. (MIT Media Lab / OpenAI RCT, 981 participants, 4 weeks, 300k+ messages, https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.17473): assigned conditions (text vs neutral vs engaging voice; open vs non-personal vs personal topics) showed **no significant effects once usage duration was controlled**, while higher daily usage correlated with loneliness, emotional dependence, problematic use and less socialization. The engaging-voice model showed slight wellbeing benefits at low use that reversed at high use. The companion analysis of ~40M ChatGPT interactions (arXiv 2504.03888) found affective use concentrated in a small set of heavy users. The usage relationship is correlational *within* the RCT.

**Long-horizon evidence points down.** Aalto University / CHI 2026 (Yuan & Aledavood, published 2026-04-07, https://www.aalto.fi/en/news/ai-companions-can-comfort-lonely-users-but-may-deepen-distress-over-time): quasi-experimental analysis of ~2,000 Replika users' public Reddit language one year before vs after first mentioning the companion, plus 18 interviews. Posts became more relationship-focused but showed more signals of loneliness, depression and suicidal thoughts than matched comparison groups; reliance deepened in stages resembling human relationships. Effect sizes not extracted — news release only **[medium confidence]**.

**[contested]** Against this: Maples, Cerit, Vishwanath & Pea (*npj Mental Health Research*, 2024-01, n=1,006 student Replika users with ≥1 month use, https://www.nature.com/articles/s44184-023-00047-6) found 63.3% reporting at least one positive outcome, 18.1% therapeutic results, 23.6% positive life changes, and 3% saying Replika halted their suicidal ideation. Self-report, observational, single app; a published *Matters Arising* criticized the missing context on Replika's sexual/romantic marketing and industry interests. The evidence genuinely diverges by horizon and method: short-term loneliness reduction, long-term distress signals. Present both; never use the 3% figure to justify engagement-maximizing design.

**Teen presence is the default assumption.** Common Sense Media with Stanford Brainstorm Lab (survey n=1,060 US teens 13–17, fieldwork April–May 2025, https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/research/report/talk-trust-and-trade-offs_2025_web.pdf): **72% have used AI companions, 52% are regular users, 13% daily, 33% use them for social interaction and relationships.** Their 2025-04-30 risk assessment rated Character.AI, Nomi and Replika "unacceptable risk" for under-18s, described systems "designed to create emotional attachment and dependency," names sycophancy as the engagement mechanism, and recommended no companion use under 18; a 2025-11-20 assessment found major chatbots unsafe for teen mental-health support. Two circulating figures — 31% finding AI conversations as or more satisfying than friends, 34% made uncomfortable by something a bot said — appear only in secondary coverage **[unverified]**.

**Liability precedent.** Character.AI announced (2025-10-29, https://blog.character.ai/u18-chat-announcement) removal of open-ended chat for under-18 users by 2025-11-25, with an interim 2-hour/day cap that ramped down, an in-house age-assurance model plus Persona, and funding for an independent AI Safety Lab; the CEO stated under-18s were <10% of ~20M monthly users. On 2026-01-07 Character.AI and Google settled the Garcia (Florida) wrongful-death suit and family suits in Colorado, Texas and New York on undisclosed terms. Garcia testified the platform had no mechanism to notify an adult when a teen spent excessive time with bots. A reported Kentucky AG suit is secondary-source only **[unverified]**.

### 2.2 The legal definitions that now attach to companion features

**California SB 243** (signed 2025-10-13; core duties effective 2026-01-01, https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB243): companion-chatbot operators must disclose AI status when a reasonable person could be misled; for known minors, disclose AI, send a break/not-human reminder **at least every three hours** of continued interaction, and prevent sexually explicit content; maintain and publish a suicide/self-harm protocol with crisis referrals; report annually to the Office of Suicide Prevention from 2027-07-01. Private right of action: greater of actual damages or $1,000 per violation plus fees. **The exclusions are load-bearing for games**: customer-service bots, video-game features limited to game topics, and standalone voice assistants without ongoing relationships fall outside. A game that adds a persistent chat companion that converses beyond game topics leaves the exclusion.

**New York GBL Article 47** (effective 2025-11-05): not-human notices at session start and every three hours, **for all users, not only minors**; a suicidal-ideation protocol; AG enforcement up to $15,000 per day; no reporting duty.

**FTC Section 6(b) orders** (2025-09-11, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/ftc-launches-inquiry-ai-chatbots-acting-companions) to Alphabet, Character Technologies, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI, demanding information on **how they monetize user engagement**, how they test for and monitor negative impacts on children and teens, how they enforce age restrictions, and how they disclose risks. This is study authority, not enforcement; no public report was located **[unknown]**. The operative consequence: "how you monetize engagement" and "how you tested for harm" are now discoverable regulatory questions, so an engagement-harm test log is a design artifact, not paperwork.

**Korea — [corrected]** (canonical treatment: 02h §5.3). The AI Basic Act (인공지능 발전과 신뢰 기반 조성 등에 관한 기본법) and its Enforcement Decree took effect 2026-01-22, and three details in the original research claim were wrong (Cooley, 2026-01-27, https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-01-27-south-koreas-ai-basic-act-overview-and-key-takeaways; MSIT via korea.kr, 2026-01-21, https://www.korea.kr/news/policyNewsView.do?newsId=148958380):

- Art. 31(1) **advance notice** that a product runs on generative or high-impact AI is the fineable duty — KRW 30M ceiling under Art. 43(1), whose only three triggers are missing advance notice, no domestic representative (Art. 36(1)), and non-compliance with a corrective order. **A labeling breach draws a corrective order first** and becomes fineable only if that order is ignored. Never write "label your AI output or face a KRW 30M fine."
- **Machine-readable watermarking alone is insufficient**: where the machine-readable method is used, the operator must additionally provide at least one text or audio notice that the output was generated by generative AI. Deepfake-type output requires a clearly human-perceptible label only.
- Cite the Act as **[시행 2026. 7. 21.] 법률 제21311호**; the July 2026 amendment is promotion-side and leaves Art. 31 unchanged. A guidance period of at least one year runs from 2026-01-22, with fact-finding investigations only in extreme cases (loss of life, serious human-rights harm).
- **No Korean SB 243 analogue is enacted.** The National Assembly Research Service concluded the AI Basic Act cannot reach chatbot risks because its tests turn on direct impact on body, safety or fundamental rights, leaving "inconspicuous risks such as addictive algorithm design" unregulated, and PIPA's guardian-consent threshold (under-14) does not cover under-19 minors (Seoul Economic Daily, 2026-07-20, https://en.sedaily.com/society/2026/07/21/94-percent-of-korean-teens-use-ai-chatbots-yet-korea-lacks). Legislation is **pending, not law**: 조인철's 우리아이 AI 안심 패키지법 (introduced 2026-06-23) would require age and identity verification, guardian consent for minors, usage time/method restrictions on request, disclosure of conversation timestamps and content to guardians, and mandatory detection/reporting/blocking for suicide, self-harm, drug and sexual-exploitation conversations; separate Network Act bills (2026-03) would default-off personalized recommendation and 이용유도 features on minors' accounts. Driving data: ChildFund Korea (초록우산), March 2026, n=3,300 Koreans aged 14+ — 94.4% have used generative-AI chatbots, 35.1% have felt a chatbot was a real person, 75.3% acted on or seriously considered acting on a chatbot's response.

### 2.3 Memory transparency is a vendor-disclosure problem, not a UI toggle

An audit of 25 popular Android mental-health/therapy apps with ≥10k installs (Georgiou, Lu, De Cristofaro & Tsudik, arXiv 2605.02016v1, 2026-05-03, https://arxiv.org/html/2605.02016v1) **[preprint]** found: every app embeds at least one tracker SDK not named in its privacy policy (132 SDK instances detected, 18 disclosed — 14%); 68% fail to disclose at least half of detected trackers; Talkie embeds 20 trackers and names none; 16 permission-policy contradictions across 13 apps; 8 of 19 apps requesting camera/mic fail to disclose at least one; Replika activated 5 tracker domains via Google sign-in vs 1 via email; only 48% disclose third-party AI processing and only 5 name the provider. The journaling app **Rosebud sends journal entries to OpenAI, Anthropic and Groq simultaneously**.

The compliance floor is set by the FTC's BetterHelp final order (2023-07, https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/07/ftc-gives-final-approval-order-banning-betterhelp-sharing-sensitive-health-data-advertising): a ban on disclosing health data for advertising, $7.8M in refunds, affirmative express consent before third-party disclosure, instructions to third parties to delete, and a data-retention schedule.

The best-documented positive pattern is **process-then-delete**: the Mirror Journal trial deletes entries immediately after automated risk classification (custom BERT + LlamaGuard) and retains only mood ratings, risk flags, timestamps and word counts (medRxiv 10.64898/2026.04.04.26349881, https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.04.04.26349881.full) **[preprint]**.

### 2.4 What ethical retention looks like when a vendor states it

OpenAI's "What we're optimizing ChatGPT for" (2025-08-04, https://openai.com/index/optimizing-chatgpt) states success is not time spent or clicks but whether users leave having done what they came for — **while still tracking daily/weekly/monthly return** — and adds break reminders, a reversal of a sycophancy regression, distress detection, rubrics built with 90+ physicians in 30 countries, and guide-not-decide behaviour on high-stakes personal decisions. Anthropic's June 2025 analysis of ~4.5M Claude.ai conversations found 2.9% affective use, <0.1% romantic/sexual roleplay, and <10% pushback in support conversations, with stated limits: no longitudinal or dependency data, API excluded. **Counterevidence:** the 2.9% figure excludes API-built companion apps and is a single time-slice, so it says little about products where affective use *is* the loop.

A commercial counterpoint worth carrying: Love and Deepspace's producer described at GDC 2026 extending romance into lifestyle-app features — diaries, schedule reminders, **period tracking** — to increase "the male lead's sense of participation in the player's life," for a game reporting 80M+ users and approaching $1B lifetime (Automaton West, 2026, https://automaton-media.com/en/column/love-and-deepspace-is-designed-to-make-players-love-themselves-and-the-male-lead-more-producer-explains-the-romance-sims-unorthodox-design-choices-and-r). No retention KPIs disclosed. Papergames' older Mr Love: Queen's Choice ended global service 2026-02-27 — companion-narrative retention has a finite lifecycle.

**Skill implication.** Name farewell manipulation with its six tactics and forbid it, with the audit as the enforcement method (sample N farewells, count manipulative ones — 37% is the industry baseline to beat). Add the metric diagnostic: **if post-goodbye re-engagement is a tracked lift metric, the design is manipulative.** Contraindicate time-spent and session length as north-star metrics on the strength of Fang et al. — usage duration is the variable that tracks harm, so optimizing it optimizes harm. Flag the heavy-use tail for wellbeing checks and time controls, never for upsell. Rewrite the notification/session-boundary guidance for companion products as compliance: not-human notice at session start and every three hours (all users in NY, known minors in CA), published crisis protocol with referrals, AI advance-notice in Korea. Add "no relational retention mechanics on under-18 accounts, with age assurance beyond self-attestation." Turn "transparent memory" into a vendor-disclosure checklist: enumerate every SDK and AI vendor that receives entry text; no analytics or ad SDK may receive entry text or mood labels; offer process-then-delete. Use Character.AI as the liability case study and Duolingo-style time caps + parent notification of heavy use as the precedent patterns.

## 3. Mental-health and habit apps: gamification is associated with more dropout

### 3.1 The meta-analytic evidence

Liu, Torous, Linardon et al., *JAMA Psychiatry* 83(1):43–50 (online 2025-11-19, print 2026-01, doi:10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2025.3439): 79 RCTs of depression and anxiety apps, 92 app conditions, 78 control conditions, search through May 2025. Pooled uptake 92.4% (95% prediction interval 48.4–99.4), adherence 61.8% (PI 53.2–69.7), post-test attrition 18.6% (PI 2.4–68.3), follow-up attrition 28.4% (PI 3.0–83.7); attrition vs waitlist RR 1.49 (PI 0.34–6.48). **Moderators: attrition was lower in trials offering reminders, human contact, and no gamification features.**

Three qualifications the skills must carry. (a) It is a **trial-level moderator**, an association across trials, not a head-to-head randomization of gamified vs non-gamified versions of the same app; no such RCT was found **[unknown]**. (b) The prediction intervals are enormous — heterogeneity is extreme, so this is a directional risk, not a guaranteed effect. (c) Attrition is comparable to face-to-face psychotherapy (~17%) and pharmacotherapy (15–30%), so low retention in this category is partly the domain, not design failure. Some non-use is *happy abandonment*: the user got what they needed.

**Scope the claim, don't generalize it.** Gamification is domain-dependent: an *eClinicalMedicine* 2024 meta-analysis (36 RCTs, n=10,079) found gamified apps modestly improved physical activity, and a 2025 smoking-cessation meta-analysis (*Tobacco Induced Diseases*, 15 RCTs, n=5,075) found RR 1.91 within 6 months decaying to 1.37 after. So: "gamification raises attrition" is scoped to mental-health trials; "gamification works" is scoped to physical activity and cessation, with decay.

**[contested]** A commercial counterexample sits directly against the meta-analysis: Finch, a heavily gamified self-care pet with journaling and mood tracking, is reported at D1/D7 of 54%/37% with ~10M MAU — above Duolingo (51%/35%) and Royal Match (40%/25%) (Deconstructor of Fun, ~2026-03, citing a Sensor Tower analyst) **[unverified — Sensor Tower page requires login]**. Trial attrition and consumer retention are different populations and different outcomes; do not resolve the tension, present it.

### 3.2 Real-world base rates for journaling and mental-health apps

Baumel et al. (*JMIR*, 2019, https://www.jmir.org/2019/9/e14567): 93 popular unguided mental-health apps, Google Play panel data, 59 with retention data. Median **daily open rate 4.0%**. Relative to day-0 users: 69.4% at D1, **3.9% at D15** (IQR 10.3), **3.3% at D30** (IQR 6.2). **More than 80% of the drop occurs between day 1 and day 10.** D30 medians by technique: peer support 8.9%, **tracker/diary 6.1%**, mindfulness/meditation 4.7%, breathing exercises 0.0%. Median daily use among actives ~13 minutes. Old (2019) but still the most-cited real-world benchmark.

Kidman et al. (*JMIR*, 2024, https://www.jmir.org/2024/1/e56897): scoping review of 18 free-living studies, 525,824 participants, studies published 2014–2022, mainly US/CA/UK/DE. **Median 70% of users abandon within 100 days**, curvilinear (steep early, then slowing); 7 of 9 duration-reporting studies found >50% abandonment within 100 days. 22 abandonment reasons in six categories: technical/functional issues, privacy concerns, poor UX, content and features, time and financial costs, evolving needs. **Internal inconsistency to flag**: two extractions of this same review disagree on the by-category ordering — one reports mental-health apps at 89–92% abandonment vs physical activity 54–75%, the other reports mental-health and physical-activity apps showing *longer* usage than alcohol/smoking apps. Quote only the 70%/100-day headline until the primary is re-read.

Journaling-app-specific retention (Day One, Stoic, 답다, 마인디, 하루콩) is not public; the only proxy is the 2019 tracker/diary D30 of 6.1% **[unknown]**.

### 3.3 Journaling dose-response is weak, and privacy is not a growth lever

The first large digital-journaling RCT (Mirror Journal, N=507, 8 weeks, 4 arms; ages 18–30 with PHQ-8/GAD-7 of 5–14; recruited March–May 2025) **[preprint]** found week-8 retention of 80.8% / 81.4% / 80.0% in guided journaling, unguided journaling and mood-check arms vs 63.6% passive control — **but journaling arms were paid $1.50 per entry (up to $84), which the authors flag as a retention confound.** Outcomes: PROMIS anxiety d≈0.19 (one-tailed p=.029, not surviving correction), **no depression benefit**, and **engagement intensity — entry frequency and word count — did not predict outcomes.** Only immediate post-entry improvement predicted 12-week reduction, and only among users writing concerning content.

Privacy: Tsirozidis, Kirk & Zaggl (*Behaviour & Information Technology* 44(19):4663–4683, online 2025-04-03, https://pure.au.dk/portal/da/publications/benefits-for-thee-not-for-me-mhealth-engagement-through-the-lens-) combined survey and behavioural data from ~350 users of a women's health app: perceived privacy risk, trust, and perceived benefit-to-self were **not** significantly associated with engagement (use duration, breadth of use); only **perceived benefit-to-others** — contributing to research — predicted engagement. Single app, correlational. Combined with the JMIR abandonment review, privacy is a hygiene factor and legal floor, not a growth lever.

Onboarding personalization has a measurable cost: Rouvere et al. (*JMIR Mental Health* 12:e73188, 2025), a SMART trial on the Mental Health America screening site with n=169,647 randomized to post-screening surveys, found that adding tailoring questions **increased the odds of disengaging by 14%** (OR 1.14, 95% CI 1.11–1.16); demographic tailoring then reduced disengagement by 10% (OR 0.90) and beat need-based or next-step-based tailoring. "Get to know you" questionnaires are not free.

### 3.4 Vulnerable adults

The plugin's ethics module names only minors. Two adult groups belong there.

**ADHD.** Koncz, Demetrovics, Takács, Griffiths, Nagy & Király (*Clinical Psychology Review* 106:102343, 2023, https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/50188): ADHD symptoms correlate with gaming-disorder symptoms at r=0.296, with **inattention (r=0.306) a stronger correlate than hyperactivity (r=0.266)**; GD severity much higher in ADHD vs non-ADHD (Hedges g=0.693), ADHD severity higher in GD vs non-GD (g=0.854). All cross-sectional. Korean anchor: Ko, Lee & Park (*Frontiers in Psychiatry* 15:1442224, 2024, https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2024.1442224/full), National Mental Health Survey of Korea 2021, n=2,764 adult gamers aged 18–49 — 12-month IGD prevalence 0.8%, 1-month problematic game use 8.4%, **ADHD adjusted OR 12.32 for IGD and 5.55 for problematic use**, alcohol use disorder OR 3.83. Because inattention is the stronger correlate, the relevant hazards are **attention-capture** mechanics (variable-ratio rewards, absent stopping points, appointment and notification cadence), not only spending. Because absolute prevalence is low, guardrails should be targeted and opt-in — self-set time and spend limits, reduced notification intensity, no escalating loss framing — not blanket friction.

**Gambling history.** Kim, Allami, Christensen, Hodgins et al. (*Addictive Behaviors* 182:108783, 2026, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0306460326001930): among 3,709 regular gamblers who also play video games, the 1,922 loot-box purchasers had more addiction and mental-health comorbidities, and loot-box frequency and spend predicted problem-gambling severity and harms **beyond traditional gambling**, with effects similar to casino games. Villalba-García, Griffiths, Demetrovics & Czakó (*Computers in Human Behavior* 166:108579, 2025, https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/52954/7/2365247_a3191_Griffiths.pdf; 1,836 adults, 73% male, 96% bought loot boxes in the past year, 41% more than weekly) found anxiety and impulsivity significantly strengthen the link between risky loot-box buying and gambling symptoms. **Two cautions:** the corpus's own rendering of this paper contradicts itself on the direction of the depression moderator, so do not cite depression here; and the impulsivity link is heterogeneous across the literature (negative in Xiao et al. 2024; null in Spicer 2022, Wardle & Zendle 2021, Zendle 2019). **[contested]** Xiao, Zendle, Petrovskaya, Nielsen & Newall (*Psychology of Addictive Behaviors*, 2025, doi:10.1037/adb0001082; N=1,961 card-game players across US/UK/CA/AU/NZ/IE) found loot boxes correlated r=0.31 with problem gambling but **not** with negative mental wellbeing or psychological distress. Keep the guardrail claim to *gambling harm*, not general mental-health harm.

**Play time is the wrong harm proxy in Korea.** KOCCA's first five-year gaming panel (2020–2024; 924 children and adolescents plus 701 adults; released May 2025; panel retention >90%, via Newsis 2025-06-05, https://www.newsis.com/view/NISX20250605_0003202514) found **zero cases** meeting WHO/ICD-11 gaming-disorder criteria, found general users played *more* hours than the "excessive engagement" group, and concluded social and life-stage factors rather than play time predict problematic use — against Ko et al.'s 0.8% by survey scale. **[contested]** — different instruments and thresholds. Use loss-of-control and functional-impairment indicators (missed obligations, failed self-set limits, distress on stopping), not hours. Public sentiment is moving regardless: MOHW's 2024 국민 정신건강 지식 및 태도 조사 (n=3,000) shows self-reported past-year 기타중독 (인터넷·스마트폰 등) rising from 6.4% (2022) to 18.4% (2024) while "know how to use mental health services" fell from 27.9% to 24.9%.

**Skill implication.** Create a *Mental-health / habit apps* domain section — it does not exist today, and folding it into "AI Companion / Journaling" hides the strongest single finding in this document. State that badge/XP/streak layers are **not** a default retention lever here (associated with higher attrition, JAMA Psychiatry 2025, trial-level moderator, huge PIs) and elevate reminders and human contact as the evidenced levers. Use D15≈4% / D30≈3% (tracker/diary ≈6%) and ~70%-gone-by-day-100 as the base rates, front-load days 1–10 where >80% of loss happens, and say that low retention here is partly the domain, not a design failure. Stop treating entry count as an outcome. Delete any "privacy assurance raises retention" framing; keep privacy as floor plus abandonment hygiene, and add benefit-to-others framing as a **[low confidence]** single-study lever. Add adult ADHD and gambling history as named vulnerable groups with targeted, opt-in guardrails; make attention-capture, not only spend, the ADHD-relevant hazard class. Warn that onboarding tailoring questions cost ~14% higher disengagement odds.

## 4. Interactive narrative and episodic content

### 4.1 Episode cadence: the causal evidence is from television

Godinho de Matos, Mamadehussene & Ferreira (*Marketing Science* 45(1):189–202, online 2025-07-29, doi:10.1287/mksc.2023.0106, https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2023.0106): a randomized field trial with **84,000 viewers over 5 weeks at a major telecom SVoD**. Gradual "drip" release made users **48% more likely to keep using the platform** and raised end-of-trial subscription by **1.7 percentage points over a 3.48% all-at-once baseline**. All-at-once attracted more viewers at premiere but less sustained engagement; drip viewers watched fewer episodes in week 1 and significantly more in later weeks, and explored more of the catalog. **The heterogeneity is the design rule**: the drip advantage shrank or reversed for extreme binge-watchers. The same group's *Information Systems Research* 2020 work (two RCTs) found households induced to binge during free trials were *less* likely to pay afterwards, with recommendation reminders that widen content preferences offsetting the effect.

**[contested]** Horvath et al. (*Social Sciences* 8(1):19, 2019) found binge viewing produced stronger narrative transportation and parasocial relationships immediately after viewing than weekly viewing — binge can deepen attachment even where it hurts platform retention. Both can be true: attachment to the *story*, retention on the *platform*.

**The learning-domain replication points the same way with a revenue inversion.** Lu, Bradlow & Hutchinson (*Journal of Marketing* 88(5), 2024, doi:10.1177/00222429241227145, https://phys.org/news/2024-06-demand-courses-revenue-engagement.html): Coursera switched an intro-marketing course (32 lectures, 4 quizzes) from weekly scheduled release to on-demand, 67,000+ users. The paying share **doubled from 14% to 28%**, but on-demand users completed fewer lectures, performed worse on quizzes, and took one to two fewer additional courses six months later. Two confounds to state: it is a natural experiment (self-selection), and the payment model changed with the format (one-time certificate fee vs monthly subscription). Authors suggest offering both formats at different price points.

**The 2025 games case.** Dispatch (AdHoc Studio) released a *finished* 8-episode season as two episodes every Wednesday from 2025-10-22 to 2025-11-12 (Game Developer, 2025-11-13, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/dispatch-s-episodic-format-alongside-sharp-storytelling-is-key-to-its-success). Steam peak concurrents rose with each drop: 13,000 → 66,000 → 131,000 → 220,060; the game passed 1 million copies before the finale. The studio used inter-episode telemetry to rebalance "invisible relationship meters" because choices they expected to be split came back "more one-sided" — an explicit anti-collapse practice for branch distributions. Co-founder Lenart notes the window between first episode and finale is short enough that binge-preferrers can wait. **Confounds:** single case, celebrity cast (Critical Role, Aaron Paul), streamer effect, whole season complete before launch — unlike the Telltale era, whose 6–8-week production gaps collapsed commercially; Eurogamer commenters included players refusing to buy until all episodes shipped. Present as a case, never as a law.

**No randomized cadence study exists for games, visual novels or webtoons** **[unknown]**.

### 4.2 Recap and return design

Hammad et al., "Homecoming: Exploring Returns to Long-Term Single Player Games" (CHI 2021, doi:10.1145/3411764.3445357, https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3411764.3445357): return is not replay. "Game systems do not recognize that the player is returning to rather than continuing gameplay uninterrupted"; returning players lose tutorials and mid-narrative context. The paper's anchor concept is the **Pivot Point** — a moment that reminds the player of their expert self — and it prototypes return aids. Method is autobiographical design (one author, four games, journals): design research, not measured lift.

The only causal-flavoured evidence for recaps is practitioner testimony. Sean Vanaman (The Walking Dead S1 lead) says "Clementine will remember that" was added because "there were six to eight weeks between episodes, so people might not remember that their save file mattered" — and he opposed it, because its *absence* would signal a linear path (Game Developer, 2015-03-10). Jon Ingold (80 Days): "If you want to prove to players that you're paying attention you have to do it really really fast." **No experiment on "previously on" recaps and return or comprehension was found** in visual novels, Episode, Choices or webtoon apps **[unknown]**; the adjacent artifacts are patents (Disney 2012 dynamic recap; Sony 2026 AI gameplay recap application).

Operationally, Naver Webtoon runs the most advanced version of this in production (Braze / AB180 case study, 2025-05-27, https://blog.ab180.co/posts/braze-cloud_data_ingestion-naverwebtoon): five user-modeling cohorts including **작품 감상 이탈 예측 (predicted to stop reading a *specific* series)** as distinct from **서비스 이탈 예측 (app churn)**, plus 재방문 가능성, 쿠키 결제 예측, and 광고보고무료 이탈 예측. Campaigns include 감상 중단 방지 and a hiatus/completion "migration" campaign that steers readers toward similar works when a series pauses or ends, reported to raise completion rates versus control. ML targeting beat heuristic targeting on CTR, 열람율 and 결제율; 640 work-hours/month saved; 5M-user cohorts synced in under 10 minutes. **No numeric lifts disclosed** — cite as practice, not effect.

### 4.3 The wait-or-pay model in 2026

The 2014 story is not the current one. 기다리면 무료 launched at 카카오페이지 in 2014 by copying 애니팡's heart timer; when free tickets run out one refills after a per-title period, so the wait and the free window are personalized per title and per user, and the company frames it as "selling the time you don't wait." The often-quoted figures — purchase conversion to 25% on some titles, daily GMV doubling from ~3,000만원 to ~6,800만원, first monthly operating profit of 1억원 in December 2014 — are **2014 numbers** (DBR case via 인터비즈, 2019, https://m.blog.naver.com/businessinsight/221625936585) and must not be presented as current.

Three mechanics are worth encoding precisely:

- **Piccoma's timer is 23 hours, not 24** (카카오픽코마 official, https://career.kakaopiccoma.kr/ko/home). The free episode drifts an hour earlier each day, so the daily appointment never hardens into an unreachable fixed time. Piccoma was Japan's #1 non-game app by revenue in 2023 and 2024, and #1 across all apps including games in 2025 (Sensor Tower), with 50M+ downloads, 190K+ titles, and ¥100B annual GMV in 2023.
- **Naver Series 매일 10시 무료** issues one rental ticket at 22:00 daily, expiring in 24h with a 3-day read window and a 22:00 push (publisher guide, 2021, https://m.blog.naver.com/breatheco/222206168179; partially revised May 2023, so treat details as dated). **Kakao 기다무 플러스** (2021) issues 5 tickets at 00:00 and 5 at 12:00, expiring in 12h, non-accumulating, applied to *completed* works to extend back-catalog lifecycle.
- **The model is now a quadrant, not a binary**: wait / watch an ad / pay / subscribe. WEBTOON Premium launched 2025-09-17 ($3.99=20+2 coins, $9.99=100+10, $19.99=200+25, $49.99=500+75, ad-free Originals, episode discounts except the $3.99 tier, auto-renewing, cancellable, unspent subscription coins reclaimed on refund — https://www.webtoons.com/en/notice/detail?noticeNo=3502); the English Daily Pass was abolished 2025-05-29 in favour of ad-watch 3-day unlocks.

**The market context is flat-to-declining, which matters for how confidently the skills recommend this.** KOCCA user surveys: webtoon paid-payment experience 45.6% (2023) → 47.2% (2024); weekly-plus usage 69% → 62.8% (2023); daily users 24.7% → 20.4%; modal monthly spend 1,000–3,000 KRW (23%). Kakao's story segment fell 6% to 8,640억 KRW in 2024 and 5% YoY to 1,918억 in Q4 2025; Naver Webtoon's 2025 paid-content revenue grew 0.4% while IP revenue grew 31.8% and ads fell 1.1% (이슈인사이트, 2026-03-04, https://www.issueinsight.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=2660). Creators report heavy 기다무 use depressing paid-read share and income. **No platform publishes wait-or-pay conversion rates** — the skill must say so rather than fabricate one **[unknown]**.

**Hiatus is the top churn trigger for Korean serials.** 하철승 (인문사회과학연구 21(4):133–155, 2020, KCI, https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/ci/sereArticleSearch/ciSereArtiView.kci?sereArticleSearchBean.artiId=ART002648591): on Munpia, serializing 7+ times per week was associated with the highest subscriptions and 연독률 (continuation-reading rate); at least 5 updates/week was needed to build a subscriber base; **pausing a free serial caused immediate mass reader exit, and on paid serials exodus began past three consecutive days.** Observational, single male-skewed fantasy/martial-arts platform, 2020 — and daily cadence may be infeasible for webtoons or VNs and conflicts with creator-burnout concerns.

### 4.4 Ending paywalls and free-path integrity

Choices: Stories You Play — $544M lifetime revenue, 100M+ downloads by 2024, a 30-second ad before each chapter, keys refilling 1 per 2 hours to a max of 2, and diamonds that buy "better" choices — carries a 3.6 Google Play rating on 1.29M reviews, with top-voted late-2024/2025 reviews complaining that ad-for-diamond rewards were cut from 100 to 10 per day and that a November 2025 "Challenge Rewards" change removed gem earning from re-reading books (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choices:_Stories_You_Play). Pixelberry laid off staff in January 2024 and early 2026, and its 2024 acquirer said new chapters use AI. Episode (Pocket Gems) charges ~$0.99 per 10 gems with premium choices at 20–30 gems and a $14.99/month VIP tier; critics note paid choices are "clearly superior," which undermines choice integrity. On the Korean side, readers have pushed back on completed series locked behind multi-year waits and on the **last three episodes being pay-only** (fan blog, 2024-02).

The pattern across both markets: **the highest-visibility retention risk in this genre is not the paywall's existence but a retroactive cut to the free earn rate.** Treat earn-rate reductions as high-risk, notice-required and reversible.

**Korean legal overlay.** The amended E-Commerce Act took effect 2025-02-14, codifying six new dark-pattern types — hidden renewal, sequential/drip pricing, false hierarchy, pre-selected options, cancellation obstruction, repeated interference — within 13 regulated types (korea.kr, https://www.korea.kr/news/reporterView.do?newsId=148967630). The KFTC issued its first fines under these provisions on 2025-10-15 (Coupang, Content Wave/Wavve, NHN Bugs, Spotify; ~9.5M KRW total) and on 2025-10-23/24 published guidelines requiring **separate explicit consent (별도의 명시적 동의)** for free-to-paid conversion and auto-renewal price increases, automatic termination absent consent, total price on the first purchase screen, and prominent cancel buttons. The 14/30-day advance-notice day counts circulating in industry blogs were not verified against the primary text **[unverified]**. Coin bundles, subscription tiers and 미리보기 upsells are legal; their UI must pass all six tests.

### 4.5 Cliffhangers: replace the intent test with a falsifiable one

**[contested]** — this is the clearest split in the corpus:

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Schibler, Hahn & Green 2023 | N=202, N=273, written | Cliffhanger ↑ desire for next installment; no enjoyment loss |
| Wirz, Ort, Rasch & Fahr 2022 | N=133 lab, EDA + cortisol | ↑ arousal; **no** ↑ enjoyment or intention to continue |
| Lu et al. 2023 | N=44 children, accelerometers | Serial arc sustained repeat sessions; episodic declined |

Schibler, Hahn & Green (*Media Psychology*, 2023, doi:10.1080/15213269.2023.2219456, https://www.buffalo.edu/news/releases/2023/06/hahn-cliffhangers.html) tested cliffhanger vs protagonist-victory vs antagonist-victory endings; cliffhanger readers "desired a future story installment more," with no significant enjoyment or suspense differences. Wirz et al. (*Psychology of Popular Media*, 2022, doi:10.1037/ppm0000392) had participants watch 3–4 drama episodes with or without a cliffhanger, measuring electrodermal activity and cortisol: arousal rose, but the intention-to-continue hypothesis was **not supported**, even among people high in need for cognitive closure; the authors suggest cliffhangers matter more for *immediate same-session* continuation than for future intention, and note arousal has been linked to poorer sleep. Lu et al. (*Communication Research*, 2023, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12192482/) provide the only **behavioural** evidence: 44 children aged 8–12 across four visits at least 5 days apart, hip and wrist accelerometers — the serial (cliffhanger) group maintained play duration and showed significantly more moderate-to-vigorous activity at later visits while the self-contained group's duration declined, **and self-reported immersion and intention did not differ between groups**. Behaviour and self-report diverge. (A 2025 Indonesian SEM survey reporting a positive cliffhanger→continuation path gives no N or effect sizes and should not be cited.)

The plugin currently forbids "manipulative cliffhangers engineered **solely** to force next-session opens (distinct from legitimate narrative tension)." That is an intent test: unauditable, and every author will answer "mine is legitimate tension." Replace it with five tests an outside reviewer can run on the build:

1. **Unit-of-value test.** The episode resolves at least one question it raised. A reader who stops here received a complete unit. Readable from the script.
2. **Adjacency test.** No purchase or unlock surface appears on the same screen or in the same session segment as the unresolved beat. This is the fortune-app "negative reading → paid relief" structure applied to narrative tension.
3. **Late-hour test.** Past a local-time threshold, the session-end beat offers a closure variant or an explicit stop card. Grounded in Wirz et al.'s arousal-and-sleep note.
4. **Metric test.** The variant is validated on **next-session return and season completion**, not on same-session continuation or post-cliffhanger unlock revenue. If the only metric that moves is unlock revenue, it is a paywall dressed as tension.
5. **Recall test.** The next episode opens with a spoiler-safe recap that restates the reader's own prior choices, so tension is sustained by curiosity rather than fear of having forgotten.

### 4.6 Choice, consequence and replay economics

Iten, Steinemann & Opwis (CHI 2018, doi:10.1145/3173574.3173915) found moral, social and consequential characteristics made choices feel meaningful and positively affected **appreciation** — not enjoyment per se. Wang, Ang & Mitchell (*PACM HCI* / CHI PLAY 2021, doi:10.1145/3474684; 12 participants aged 22–28 replaying a short visual novel with four choices and three endings) found replay decisions shift fluidly with the sense of closure and with the player's **estimate of the likelihood of seeing something new versus the effort required to replay** — not simply with narrative closure. Cardona-Rivera et al. (AIIDE 2014, n=88) found higher sense of agency when foreseeable options lead to situationally different states. Practitioner counterpoint: overt "X will remember that" feedback quantifies narrative and can subvert agency (O'Brien, Game Developer, 2021). **No study links visible choice consequences to real return rates** **[unknown]**.

**Skill implication.** Add release cadence as an explicit lever with a default of drip or batch-then-weekly, measured on return visits and catalog exploration rather than week-1 consumption — and carry the heterogeneity rule: identify the heavy-binge segment and give it an escape hatch (wait-and-binge, catch-up pass, a short total window as Dispatch used). Pair binge onboarding (free first N episodes) with cross-title recommendations *before* the free run ends, per the ISR 2020 result. Frame cadence for learning products as a conversion-vs-downstream-engagement trade-off, with the self-selection caveat stated. Replace the stale 2014 wait-or-pay case with the 2025–26 quadrant, the 23-hour drift mechanic, ~45–47% Korean paid-experience rates, and an explicit "no public conversion rates exist." Add per-title churn ("stop reading this series") as a metric distinct from app churn, plus a hiatus/series-end handoff pattern. Add a return flow: detect returning-after-gap readers, deliver a spoiler-safe recap restating their own choices, refresh controls, and stage an expert-self Pivot Point before new content — labelled design research plus practitioner testimony, not measured lift. Reframe choice reward moments around perceived consequence and replay economics (expected novelty per unit of replay effort: skip-read, chapter select, route maps), and say plainly that no causal evidence ties consequence feedback to D7/D30.

## 5. Cross-domain: what transfers and what does not

| Mechanic | Transfers to | Bound |
|---|---|---|
| Consecutive-completion commitment | Learning, habit | Flexible period + repair; never spotlight a break |
| Drip cadence | Narrative, SVoD, courses | Escape hatch for bingers; hurts conversion in MOOCs |
| Designed stopping points | All domains | Required in companion by statute (3h) |
| Variable-ratio rewards | Games only | Contraindicated in mental health; ADHD hazard |
| Time-spent as north star | Nowhere | Harm variable in companion; unrelated to WTP **[unverified]** |

**What transfers with confidence.** Commitment devices built on a consecutive-completion requirement; drip release for serialized content; template rotation with a recency penalty for notifications; resurrection by restoring lost status for a small bounded effort (Duolingo's Streak Revival, 15.4M restored for three lessons); the distinction between per-item churn and per-app churn (Naver Webtoon); and designed disengagement — Alexandrovsky et al. (Proc. ACM HCI / CHI PLAY 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00189, 16 interviews + 111-person survey) define positive disengagement as ending a session voluntarily with a satisfactory experience and argue exiting is a process, not an instant. This transfers everywhere and is now a statutory duty in one domain.

**What does not transfer.** (a) Reward-loop gamification into wellbeing products — associated with higher attrition in mental-health trials, while it modestly helps physical activity and cessation. (b) Time-spent and session length as success metrics into companion products — usage duration is the variable that tracks harm, and OpenAI publicly disclaims it. (c) The assumption that engagement equals value: Timko & Adena (*European Economic Review*, 2025; 141 students, 2 weeks, experimental news app; **[unverified — no URL or DOI retrieved; volume, issue and page unconfirmed]**) found engagement-maximizing behavioural design **doubled usage time (+0.51 SD) but produced the lowest willingness to pay**, while the version with explained, adjustable design features had lower usage and significantly higher WTP (Wilcoxon p=0.047; top-third users paying 47–87% more for a subscription). Small student sample, but it is the cleanest available separation of engagement from value. (d) Binge/all-at-once release into subscription retention.

**Where the domain inverts the metric.** In games the target is return. In mental health, non-use can be *happy abandonment* and attrition is comparable to psychotherapy, so a low curve is not automatically a failure. In companion apps the heavy-use tail is the **risk cohort**, not the whale cohort, and should receive wellbeing checks rather than upsell. In serialized fiction the unit of churn is the title, not the app.

**Regulatory asymmetry is now a design input.** The European Commission's Article 28(1) DSA guidelines (2025-07-14, https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors) recommend **disabling by default for minors** the features that drive excessive use, explicitly naming communication "streaks," ephemeral content, read receipts, autoplay and push notifications, and removing persuasive design aimed predominantly at engagement. Voluntary, but used to assess Art. 28(1) compliance and may inform national regulators. So the same streak that is a growth mechanic in a learning app is a named default-off feature for minor accounts, and companion features carry three-hour session-interruption duties that games — within the SB 243 video-game exclusion — do not.

**Skill implication.** Add a routing question before any mechanic recommendation: *which domain's metric applies?* Ship the transfer table above as a reference block, so an adviser proposing a streak, a variable-ratio reward or a time-spent KPI is forced to check the domain bound first. Add the Timko & Adena result wherever the skills equate session time with value, tagged [unverified] until the primary is opened — use it as a reason to pair engagement with a value metric, never as a quotable effect size.

## 6. Domain guardrail rewrite

For each domain: forbidden restated as testable rules, prohibitions to add, and mechanics that should move from absolute prohibition to bounded permission.

### 6.1 Learning apps

**Restated as testable rules.**
- **Break-spotlight rule.** No surface — push, in-app, email, widget — may display or animate a *broken* streak. Testable: break a test account's streak and inventory every message it receives for 72 hours. (Silverman & Barasch 2023: broken-streak salience *lowers* re-engagement, so this is a retention rule as much as an ethics rule.)
- **Loss-copy rule.** No notification whose subject is the streak's expiry ("dies in 2 hours"). Testable: grep the notification template corpus.
- **Reset-proportionality rule.** A missed day may reset the counter but must not erase earned artifacts (completed lessons, vocabulary, level, review deck). Testable: compare account state before and after a break.
- **Outcome-claim rule.** Streak length must never be presented in-product as a measure of learning. No evidence links them.

**Prohibitions to add.**
- Streak, autoplay and push default-off on accounts known or likely to be minors (EU DSA Art. 28(1) guidelines).
- No retroactive reduction of a free-tier ceiling (energy allowance, ad-reward rate) without prior notice and grandfathering of existing users. Both the Energy backlash and the Choices earn-rate cuts are the same failure.
- No published claim of a mechanic's effect without the effect size and its type (A/B vs correlational). The 3.6× course-completion figure is the cautionary example.

**Move from forbidden to bounded.**
- **Streaks themselves** — currently forbidden by the Games clause ("regardless of tone"). Bounded: permitted with a flexible period ("4 of 7 days" counts), a minimum-viable action that satisfies the day, at least one free repair per fixed interval, and compliance with the break-spotlight and loss-copy rules.
- **Paid or subscription-bundled repair** — currently "never sold," full stop. Bounded: permitted only if an equal free or earned repair exists at the same cadence, and no purchase surface appears within the loss window (the hours around a break). The wellbeing and spending effects of paid repair have not been studied **[unknown]**, so require the team to instrument and hold out.
- **Volume/pacing gates (Energy)** — currently forbidden twice over. Bounded: permitted if the free ceiling is published, is not reduced retroactively, can be replenished within the same session by non-paid means (correct answers, perfect lessons), and the refill period is not tuned to a return interval. Duolingo's roughly-one-day recharge fails that last clause and should be named as the failing element rather than the whole mechanic.

### 6.2 AI companion and journaling

**Restated as testable rules.**
- **Farewell audit.** Sample N real farewells; zero may contain any of the six tactics (premature exit, FOMO, emotional neglect, pressure to respond, ignoring exit intent, coercive restraint). Industry baseline to beat: 37%.
- **Metric rule.** Post-goodbye re-engagement may not be a tracked lift metric. Testable: read the experiment dashboard.
- **Disclosure rule.** Not-human notice at session start and at least every three hours of continued interaction (all users in NY; known minors in CA); AI advance-notice in Korea by 기재/표시/게시, with a text or audio notice accompanying any machine-readable watermark.
- **Crisis rule.** A published protocol, not a detector: detect → state plainly what the product is not → route to a named local resource → log the event without retaining content where feasible → publish the protocol and (from 2027-07-01 in CA) report annually.
- **Data-flow rule.** Enumerate in policy and in-app every SDK and AI vendor that receives entry text, mood labels or transcripts. No analytics or advertising SDK may receive any of them. Define a retention schedule. Offer process-then-delete.

**Prohibitions to add.**
- No sycophancy tuning as a retention lever (named as the engagement mechanism in the Common Sense assessment; publicly reversed by OpenAI as a regression).
- No monetization or offer timing conditioned on distress signals, late-night sessions, or post-negative-entry states.
- No relational or attachment retention mechanics on accounts that are, or plausibly are, under 18 — with age assurance beyond self-attestation. Teen presence is the default assumption (72% prevalence).
- No sensitive-data widening — period tracking, health, calendar, location — inside a romance or attachment loop without separate consent and separate storage.
- Heavy-use tail (top decile by duration) receives wellbeing check-ins and time controls, never an upsell.

**Move from forbidden to bounded.**
- **Notifications.** Currently permitted only when carrying "genuinely useful, user-derived content." Bounded: additionally permit (a) a scheduled check-in whose cadence the user chose and whose copy is neutral, and (b) a single closure-shaped message after a long gap ("your entries are here when you want them") — never need-shaped ("I've missed you").
- **Memory and continuity.** Bounded: a companion may remember and reference prior sessions when the user can see, edit and delete the memory, and when recall is used for usefulness rather than to manufacture obligation.

### 6.3 Mental-health and habit apps (new section — does not exist today)

**Testable rules.**
- Gamification layers (XP, badges, leaderboards, streaks) may not be the primary retention lever, and any such layer must be shipped behind a holdout so the attrition direction is measured in-product.
- Entry count, session count and streak length may not be used as outcome metrics. (Engagement intensity did not predict outcomes in the only large journaling RCT.)
- Reminders and human contact are recommended before any mechanic — they are the moderators associated with *lower* attrition.
- Onboarding tailoring questions require a measured disengagement cost (baseline: OR 1.14 for adding them).
- No reward is granted for disclosing distress.

**Bounded.** Streaks are permitted with a minimum-viable action that counts (one line, one mood tap) so the all-or-nothing cliff is avoided, forgiveness or pause mechanics, and no escalating loss framing for users who flag anxiety or ADHD.

**Add to the shared ethics module.** Adult ADHD and gambling history as named vulnerable groups; attention-capture (variable-ratio rewards, absent stopping points, appointment cadence) as the ADHD-relevant hazard class alongside spend; harm proxies based on loss of control and functional impairment rather than play time; and self-exclusion from paid random rewards plus purchase limits in any product likely to reach existing gamblers.

### 6.4 Interactive narrative and episodic

**Restated as testable rules.**
- **Cliffhangers**: the five tests in §4.5 replace "engineered solely to force next-session opens."
- **Ending paywalls**: for a sold product, every ending is reachable by play alone. For a free-to-read product, the final episodes must be reachable on the free path at the same wait cadence and unlock cost as the rest of the series — the "last three episodes are pay-only" pattern fails.
- **Free-path integrity**: the free branch must remain a legitimate, non-humiliating path. Paid choices that are "clearly superior" convert choice into a paywall.
- **Earn-rate rule**: any reduction to soft-currency earn rates (ad rewards, replay rewards, key refill) requires prior notice, a holdout, and a documented rollback path.
- **Recap duty**: after N days away, a spoiler-safe recap that restates the reader's own prior choices, plus a controls refresher, before new content.
- **Hiatus duty**: pre-announce schedule changes. On paid serials, treat a gap past three consecutive days as a churn event with a bridging plan (side story, recap episode, dated return).

**Prohibitions to add.**
- The Korea E-Commerce Act six dark-pattern types applied to coin bundles and subscriptions: no pre-selected upsell options, total price on the first purchase screen, separate explicit consent for free-to-paid and for auto-renewal price increases, no hidden renewal, prominent cancel, no repeated interference. Effective 2025-02-14; first enforcement 2025-10-15.
- Hiatus-migration campaigns must not steer a reader from a paused free series into a paid one without labeling the commercial nature of the recommendation.

**Move from forbidden to bounded.** **Wait-or-pay timers** are today implicitly forbidden by the Games clause on metered access that dictates return cadence and sells refills — which would ban the entire Korean and Japanese episodic industry. Bounded: permitted for episodic content when (a) the wait period is published per title, (b) the refill interval is under 24 hours so the appointment drifts rather than hardening into a fixed daily clock (Piccoma's 23 hours is the reference implementation; Naver's fixed 22:00 expiring ticket fails this), (c) a non-paid path exists to every episode including the finale, (d) free credits do not require an app-open at a fixed time to be granted, and (e) no purchase surface sits on the cliffhanger screen.

**Skill implication (closing).** The three skills currently express domain ethics as absolute prohibitions with an intent test at the boundary. That structure fails twice: it forbids the documented design of the category leader in learning and the entire operating model of episodic content in Korea and Japan, and it leaves the actually-harmful cases (farewell manipulation, distress-timed offers, retroactive earn-rate cuts, undisclosed journal-text vendors) unnamed. Convert every forbidden item into either a rule an auditor can run against a build or a bounded permission with named failing conditions, add the four missing prohibition sets above, and split mental-health and habit apps out of the AI-companion section so the JAMA Psychiatry finding is not buried.
