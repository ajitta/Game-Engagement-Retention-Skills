# Genre and Title Lessons (2024–2026)

Genre is not a flavour label on a retention curve; it decides which curve shape is healthy, which metric is diagnostic, and which reward moments can carry the return. The 2024–2026 evidence set makes that concrete: the last mobile benchmark edition with genre cuts spreads median D7 from 6.83% (Card) to 1.87% (Multiplayer), PC medians sit at roughly a third of mobile D1, and the two best-documented extraction shooters of 2026 diverged by nearly 100 points of five-month player retention with similar review scores. Everything below is tagged with population and definition, because the single most common error in the current skills is quoting a top-quartile 2022 aggregator table as if it were a market median. Where a title's figure is an analyst panel estimate rather than first-party disclosure, that is stated inline — for several of the most-cited titles (Balatro, Hades II, Peak, Schedule I, Blue Prince) no retention data exists at all.

## 2. Genre retention models

Standing definition note for everything in this section. GameAnalytics figures are UTC **calendar-day, strict return-on-day-N**, medians across *games* (not players), on a self-serve SDK population skewed to indie and early-stage titles. Sensor Tower per-title figures are **panel estimates on an install cohort**, for top-grossing titles — the extreme right tail. The two families are not comparable and must never be averaged (GameAnalytics 2026 vs Sensor Tower, both below).

### Puzzle (match-3, block, merge)

**Curve:** the flattest mobile curve in the last benchmark edition with genre cuts — Puzzle P50 D1 20.53% / D7 4.51% / D28 1.19% (GameAnalytics, 2025 edition, 11,600 games ≥ platform floor, iOS+Android, 9 regions, Jan–Dec 2024 calendar data, https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-mobile-gaming-benchmarks). Card sits above it (19.05 / 6.83 / 2.85) and Board close behind (19.81 / 5.96 / 2.23). The top of the market is a completely different curve: Royal Match D1 60.9% / D7 31.5% / D30 17.8% / D180 6.8%, Block Blast! D7 33.4% / D180 7.93%, Candy Crush Saga D1 39.9% / D7 22.6% / D30 12.5% (Sensor Tower panel estimates, January 2026 install cohort, https://sensortower.com/blog/h1-2026-digital-gaming-market-index).

**Return driver:** an unfinished level position you personally own, gated by a lives/energy timer. The level number is a compounding asset a returning player cannot re-derive, which is why puzzle D28 outlasts genres with far higher D1.

**Failure mode:** a difficulty *slope* instead of a *rhythm*, and mode dilution. Royal Match's first 100 levels run normal levels at ~1.2 attempts (23–30 moves), hard levels roughly every 10 at ~1.6 attempts, super-hard peaks at 59/69/79/89/99 up to 2.5 attempts, with move budgets tightening from 30–38 (lv 1–20) to 23–27 (lv 61–100) (Playliner/Slashcev breakdown via Naavik, 2026, https://naavik.co/digest/what-leading-match-3-and-merge-games-do-differently/) — **[unverified]** in its primary form: the original Playliner study now redirects to Sensor Tower and survives only in secondary posts. The mode-dilution evidence is older and cleaner: King's Nightmare cut downloads 21% when placed prominently and added only ~3% D14 once embedded invisibly in progression (Naavik, 2022-era data).

**Moment types:** near-miss at move exhaustion; the escalating extra-move offer ($2 for the first five extra moves, then $4.35 / $6.65 / $8.95); hard-level celebration; "victory lap" bonus levels. The near-miss and the price ladder are the same moment — that coupling is the genre's ethics flag, not an incidental.

**Audience contract:** 84% of puzzle players seek relaxation and 81% are women (Mistplay survey via Naavik, 2026 — **[unverified]**, no primary URL retrieved), so intense live-ops must still read as calm. Genre revenue is large and growing: puzzle IAP >$10B in 2025, +14% YoY, second only to strategy (Naavik, 2026); Sensor Tower's differently-drawn taxonomy puts Puzzle at $14.4B and Strategy at $20.2B for 2025 (via GameDev Reports, 2026, https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026). The two figures differ because the genre boundaries differ — do not reconcile them.

### 4X / strategy hybrid

**Curve:** a steep median with an enormous top tail. Strategy P50 D1 19.85% / D7 3.18% / D28 0.68% (GameAnalytics 2025 edition, 2024 data). Top titles, January 2026 cohort panel estimates: Whiteout Survival D1 42.7% / D7 14.1%; Last War D1 39.8% / D7 11.1% (Sensor Tower, https://sensortower.com/blog/h1-2026-digital-gaming-market-index). **[contested]** Naavik's earlier US-iOS estimates for the same pair were 42/17/8 and 34/11/4 (2025, https://naavik.co/digest/how-last-war-is-winning-the-4x-game/); the ordering is stable across both, the magnitudes are not.

**Return driver:** timed build/research queues *plus* alliance obligation. The distinguishing feature is that the appointment is **server-set** — a weekly Alliance Duel VS happens whether or not you set a timer — rather than player-set like a puzzle energy refill.

**Failure mode:** creative-to-loop mismatch. Last War's mini-game creative was >50% of ad impressions and its onboarding runs 4–5 minutes of mini-game before the 4X appears; Naavik attributes its retention deficit to that expectation mismatch, and concludes the model depends on continuous UA rather than retention strength. It still reached ~$1.1B in 2024 (~$30M in January rising to ~$138M in December) — **retention is not a sufficient predictor of commercial success when UA scale and monetization dominate.**

**Moment types:** queue-completion collection; alliance scoreboard placement; limited-time currency and store windows; "co-opetition" events that make your absence visible to allies.

**Evidence gap that the skills must not paper over:** the claim "alliance obligation drives retention" is analyst assertion. Naavik gives no data tying alliance mechanics to retention, and neither does the DoF/Katkoff framing. The nearest controlled evidence is from a different genre: a stratified Cox PH model with propensity-score matching on 1,204 MMORPG users found guild systems lengthen retention more than friend systems (Hwang & Han, SSRN 4606486, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4606486) — **[unverified]** in detail, since the SSRN page returned 403/429 and hazard ratios could not be read.

**Korea:** 4X strategy revenue grew +25% YoY in Jan–Sep 2025, and Last War drew 18–23% of lifetime revenue from Korea at the highest RPD of any market ($53), ranking #2 grossing in Korea for 2024 behind 리니지M (Sensor Tower / AppMagic figures, https://sensortower.com/ko/blog/state-of-gaming-in-korea-2025-report-KR). A 2025 systematic analysis of the top 40 Korean mobile games classifies "feeling compelled to keep up with guild members" as a retention/monetization strategy and flags it as exploitation of community — the ethics module should treat alliance obligation as a named pattern, not a neutral one.

### Gacha collection RPG

**Curve:** the worst median in the genre table — Role Playing P50 D1 15.27% / D7 2.05% / D28 0.42% — but D7 is the wrong instrument here. The genre runs on a **42-day version cadence split into two 21-day banner phases** (Genshin v7.0 ran Aug 12 – Sep 22 2026; Game8 records only versions 1.0, 2.6 and 2.7 exceeding 42 days). Spend follows that clock: Zenless Zone Zero's v2.0 update day (June 6 2025) lifted daily spend +740% to $8.5M and held above pre-update levels for **10 days**; v1.4 day (Dec 18 2024) was +189% to $9M (AppMagic estimates via PocketGamer.biz, 2025, https://www.pocketgamer.biz/zenless-zone-zero-celebrates-first-anniversary-with-442m/).

**Return driver:** banner windows, character attachment, and a narrative season. HoYoverse's own framing at GDC 2025 is "playable fiction" on three principles — immersion, simplicity, expandability — with versions released "like new seasons of a TV series" and, critically, newcomers able to enter the current season without finishing earlier chapters (4Gamer report of the Honkai: Star Rail session, 2025, http://www.4gamer.net/games/599/G059954/20250321048/).

**Failure mode:** the inter-version trough, and unrealistic launch-year expectations. ZZZ's first-year mobile revenue was $442.1M — only 23% of Genshin's year one — which is normal, not a failure signal.

**Moment types:** the pull reveal; proximity to the pity counter; the pre-patch livestream as scheduled anticipation; anniversary "generosity events" (free S-rank selectors) used as resurrection triggers.

**Regulatory constraint (non-negotiable in Korea):** 게임산업진흥에 관한 법률 §33-2 was promulgated Jan 31 2025 and took effect Aug 1 2025 — operators breaching 확률형 아이템 disclosure duties are liable for damages unless they prove the absence of **both** intent and negligence, and where a violation is found intentional the court **may** award up to 3x the amount it estimates under paragraph 2 (canonical treatment: 02h §5.1) (Hwawoo newsletter, 2025, https://www.hwawoo.com/kor/insights/newsletter/13145). This builds on the mandatory disclosure regime in force since Mar 22 2024. Any "hidden pity" or undisclosed compound-probability design is a damages exposure carrying a discretionary up-to-3x uplift, not merely a dark pattern. No adjudicated case under §33-2 was located as of 2026-09.

### Collectible / TCG (adjacent to gacha, different failure curve)

Pokémon TCG Pocket is the corpus's most instructive collectible case precisely because it looks like a success and behaved like a retention problem. First-anniversary first-party figures: 150M+ downloads, 2B+ battles, 490M+ showcases; AppMagic estimates ~$1.3B first-year revenue (Sensor Tower, 2026, https://sensortower.com/blog/pokemon-tcg-pocket-100-million-downloads). **[contested] provenance:** Sensor Tower's own store-download estimate reached 100M only around early 2026 versus the first-party 150M+ in Oct 2025 — a ~50% divergence, so any install figure must carry its source.

Then the retention data. DeNA disclosed average MAU falling 51M (Jan–Mar 2025) → 39M (Apr–Jun) → 30M (Jul–Sep) → 28M (Oct–Dec 2025), and stated that **login frequency and existing-user retention** were the problem: 「配信当初に比べては、既存ユーザの継続率が低下しており、MAUの維持が課題」 (gamebiz reporting DeNA's Q3 FY3/2026 briefing, 2026, https://gamebiz.jp/news/420505). The remedy was collection-experience updates, in-game events and new expansions — **not more pack-opening**. The pack-stamina loop (one pack per 12 hours, two free reveals a day, plus Wonder Pick on the same clock) was unchanged throughout the decline. Gated reveal is a fine appointment mechanic and a poor retention asset on its own.

### Hybrid-casual

**Curve:** the only mobile segment with meaningful IAP growth. Mobile IAP overall grew just 1.3% to $81.75B in 2025 while hybrid-casual grew ~20% YoY; in H1 2026 hybridcasual IAP rose +23% to $2.4B while total downloads fell 12% to 24B (Sensor Tower via GameDev Reports, 2026, https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/sensor-tower-state-of-gaming-2026; Sensor Tower H1 2026 index, https://sensortower.com/blog/h1-2026-digital-gaming-market-index). Leading hybrid-casual titles now out-retain top-25 casual at D7 — **Tasty Travels** (Century Games) at ~22% D7 against a top-25-by-revenue casual average of 14.9% in December 2025 **[corrected]**: the title is plural, the 14.9% and the "declining since early 2022" trend come from two different Sensor Tower reports and must not be merged, and "top 25" means top 25 *by revenue*.

**Return driver:** a hypercasual on-ramp carrying a midcore meta (battle pass, live-ops, collection) financed by a hybrid ad+IAP economy.

**Failure mode:** importing hypercasual benchmarks (D30 under 2%) or flat ad cadence. First-session norms are the opposite of hypercasual: Color Block Jam runs a 20–30 minute first session and only intensifies difficulty from level 20–25; Screwdom uses 5–15 minute levels to make failure costly (AppMagic, 2025, https://appmagic.rocks/blog/hybridcasual-q1-2025/). Observed first-interstitial timing across three hits ranges 1–12 minutes, mean 5.6 — Pizza Ready! at minute 3 framed as a "Get $30" reward pause with a No-Ads pack immediately after and interstitials roughly every 90 seconds thereafter, reportedly co-existing with D1 >35% and CPI ~$0.20 (Wehrmann, HAW Hamburg MA thesis, 2025, non-peer-reviewed, https://reposit.haw-hamburg.de/bitstream/20.500.12738/19148/1/MA_Design_and_development_of_a_hybrid_casual_game.pdf).

**Moment types:** the long uninterrupted first session; the rewarded pause framed as a gift; the level 20–25 difficulty step that opens booster demand.

**Prototype gates are not market norms:** Voodoo lowered its hypercasual gates from 55% D1 / 22% D7 to 45% D1 / 13% D7 / $0.20 CPI (GameAnalytics, 2026, https://www.gameanalytics.com/blog/how-to-think-about-retention-in-games). Those are pass/fail thresholds for a prototype at a publisher, not targets for a live product. The figures themselves are sourced; what is unverified is any claim that they represent market norms rather than one publisher's greenlight threshold, which sits above P90 of the live-game population (02b §4).

**Korea:** hybrid-casual +37% and casual +8% YoY (Jan–Sep 2025), against midcore still holding 79% of Korean mobile revenue (Sensor Tower Korea, https://sensortower.com/ko/blog/state-of-gaming-in-korea-2025-report-KR).

### Roguelite

**Curve:** unknown at the data level. No first-party retention curves exist for Balatro, Hades II, Blue Prince, Peak or Schedule I; only sales and Steam concurrency proxies are public. Treat any roguelite retention number in the wild as an inference.

**Return driver:** one of two meta-progression archetypes, and the choice is the design decision.
- *Power/unlock meta* (Hades II): softens the sting of a lost run, risks trivializing later runs. Supergiant ran 18 months of Early Access, iterated on "our impressions… feedback from players, and data", shortened EA by four months, and rewrote the ending — "It's important not to be too precious about these things" (GamesRadar, 2025, https://www.gamesradar.com/games/hades/weve-learned-to-love-and-trust-the-process-how-hades-2-built-on-supergiants-early-access-legacy-to-deliver-the-best-roguelike-of-2025/).
- *Knowledge meta* (Blue Prince, Slay the Spire 2): nothing permanent carries over except what the player learned. Within days of StS2's Early Access (Mar 5 2026), Steam threads complained of "zero feeling of progression between runs" — **[contested]**, since PC Gamer simultaneously describes the game as commercially strong. Knowledge-only progression alienates a segment without being commercially fatal.

**Failure mode:** balance changes without expectation-setting. Mega Crit's Mar 20 2026 statement followed a review bomb over a card nerf, framing changes as "a mix of player feedback, collected metrics, and our own design philosophies" and stating "no change is necessarily permanent."

**Moment types:** the build/synergy discovery; deliberate information withholding — LocalThunk keeps Balatro's score preview hidden and calls it a "fundamental design flaw" he refuses to fix, because "if I add an option to have this score preview, people are just going to click on it, and they're not going to experience the game that I wanted to create" (GMTK, 2025, https://gmtk.substack.com/p/balatros-cursed-design-problem); the self-set difficulty ladder (stakes/ascension).

### Extraction shooter / PvPvE

**Curve:** launch spike, then a fork. Arc Raiders (Embark, Oct 30 2025, ~$40) reached ~16M copies by June 2026. Bungie's Marathon (Mar 5 2026) fell 59% on Steam within a month and over 96% by five months despite 88% positive reviews and 1.5M+ sales (Forbes, 2026 — no URL captured in the research set; **[unverified]** at the figure level, though the direction is corroborated by the same GamesBeat-anchored finding). This pair is the genre's cleanest evidence that **accessibility beats hardcore purity for retention** and that quality does not equal retention.

**Return driver:** the risk/reward run plus stored gear value plus a visible progression cap.

**Failure modes, in the order they bite:** (1) the cap-hit churn cliff — after a level-75 cap, Embark's Grøndal describes a content drought: "The players wanted more… the team just couldn't keep pace"; (2) forced wipes, which Embark's Watkins argues have "the potential outcome of disincentivizing using your gear" when the reset is only about money; (3) cadence outrunning capacity — a ~120-person studio "cannot compete on content quantity", so Embark moved from frequent small updates to fewer, larger ones (GamesBeat, 2026, https://gamesbeat.com/how-arc-raiders-devs-dealt-with-the-rollercoaster-of-hyper-growth-player-abandonment-and-then-slow-cooked-a-major-update-aleksander-grondal-interview/).

**The fixes worth copying:** aggression-based matchmaking that separates peaceful and PvP cohorts — segmentation by *play intent*, not by skill; and opt-in resets ("Expeditions") with objective-based rewards, of which over 1M players completed the first, 35–40% of those having already maxed skill points.

**Moment types:** extraction-or-die tension; the post-raid inventory reveal; the voluntary reset as a ceremony rather than a punishment.

### Hero shooter / live PvP

**Curve:** an enormous launch peak decaying ~70–80% to a stable band that cadence maintains but does not reverse. Marvel Rivals Steam monthly average concurrents: 279,402 (Dec 2024), 306,066 (Jan 2025), 82,825 (Jul 2025), 63,716 (Oct 2025), 88,790 (Jan 2026), 65,150 (Mar 2026), 84,281 (Jul 2026, peak 196,974) — Steam only, consoles unavailable (https://steamcharts.com/app/2767030).

**Return driver:** the season clock — pass tiers, rank reset, and a new hero. Marvel Rivals cut seasons from ~3 months to 2 from Season 3 (July 11 2025) so a hero ships roughly monthly, against Overwatch 2's roughly one hero per ~6 months (GameSpot, 2025, https://www.gamespot.com/articles/marvel-rivals-shortening-seasons-releasing-a-new-hero-every-month/1100-6530612/); the wiki records Season 3+ as 9 weeks (4-week and 5-week halves) with a balance pass each half.

**Failure mode:** attributing the band to the cadence. **[contested]** — whether the two-month season measurably improved retention is unverified; only aggregator commentary claims it, and the Steam trajectory shows a ~75% decline through the change. Pair this with the Arc Raiders counter-rule: compression is a retention tool only while quality holds.

**Also worth correcting in the skills:** "the battle pass never expires" is scarcity-*preserving*, not FOMO-free. Owned Luxury passes stay redeemable, but Chrono Tokens must be re-earned in later seasons, unowned past passes cannot be bought, and the single re-release (S0, May 2025) was time-limited, +20% priced, with no free track (NetEase Dev Talk Vol.14, 2025, https://www.marvelrivals.com/devdiaries/20250513/40954_1233900.html).

**Moment types:** hero debut; pass tier unlock; half-season balance patch as a scheduled re-evaluation of your main.

### Cozy

**Curve:** no benchmark exists; the genre spans premium finite PC titles and F2P mobile dress-up gacha, which have opposite healthy curves.

**Return driver:** agency, abundance, prosocial narrative and eudaimonic emotion. A survey of 277 cozy players found motives centred on moral self-reflection, agency, escapism, eudaimonic emotions and narrative, with defining elements of easy mechanics, prosocial narratives, no violence, and feelings of comfort, safety, abundance and softness; cozy players score high on Light Triad traits (Eludamos, 2025, peer-reviewed, https://septentrio.uit.no/index.php/eludamos/article/view/7938).

**Failure mode:** tightening. Infinity Nikki's v1.5 (Apr 29 2025) added more outfit pieces per set, made pity less generous and shipped bugs; players organized a "girlcott", review-bombed the Steam launch, and forced multiple apologies, compensation and schedule changes. Inverse notes the game had put relatively low pressure on players to spend at launch, which is exactly why the tightening read as betrayal. **In a cozy product, a monetization tightening is a brand-risk event, not an economy tune.**

**Moment types:** eudaimonic "being moved" beats; abundance reveal (a complete set, a full harvest); low-stakes daily tending — Grow a Garden players describe the appeal as "simple", "chill", "switch off". Loss-framed streaks and FOMO timers are off-contract for this audience.

### Idle / incremental

**Curve:** high session frequency at moderate minutes, with retention resting on offline accrual that creates a return debt. In Korea, idle RPG's share of RPG revenue rose from 1.7% (2020) to 16% (2024) while MMORPG's fell from 78.8% to 56.2%. The engagement shapes are entirely different: MMORPG players ~135 min/day over 5.4 sessions versus idle RPG ~50 min/day over 5.2 sessions (Sensor Tower, Aug 2024, via https://sensortower.com/ko/blog/state-of-gaming-in-korea-2025-report-KR) — *same session count, one-third the time*.

**Return driver:** offline accumulation plus a storage cap you must return to empty.

**Failure mode:** either accrual so generous that returning is optional, or caps so tight that the appointment is experienced as an obligation. Idle is the genre where "increase session count" advice is most often structurally wrong — the session count is already near ceiling.

**Moment types:** the offline-earnings collection screen; the prestige/reset reveal that converts a plateau into a new curve.

### UGC platform (experience-level and platform-level)

**Curve:** portfolio, not per-title. Roblox users engaged with over 24 unique experiences per month, and experiences outside the top 10 supplied more than half of Q4 Robux-spend growth; Q4 2025 DAU 144M (+69%), hours 35B (+88%), FY2025 revenue $4.9B (+36%) and bookings $6.8B (+55%) (Roblox Q4 2025 Shareholder Letter, https://s27.q4cdn.com/984876518/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/Q4-2025-Shareholder-Letter.pdf).

**Per-experience retention scales with session-length band**, which is the single most actionable UGC number in the corpus: 0–3 minute experiences show median D1 4.3% / D7 0.41%, while 19–24 minute experiences show D1 11.46% / D7 1.61%; the platform median session is 6.62 min at 2.02 sessions/day (GameAnalytics, *2025 Roblox Benchmark Report*, covering 47% of platform engagement, https://www.gameanalytics.com/reports/2025-roblox-report). The 2025 discovery algorithm amplifies experiences that drive friend invites and intentional co-play.

**Return driver:** the platform's social graph and its algorithm — not your D1. Creator incentives now make this explicit: Roblox launched a Creator Rewards program in 2025 that pays creators specifically for retention and engagement, and DevEx rose 8.5% on Sept 5 2025.

**Fortnite/UEFN payout formula** **[corrected]** — the earlier hedge ("Epic primary not retrievable") is wrong and should be dropped; Epic's live documentation carries all of it (https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/fortnite-creative/engagement-payouts-in-fortnite-creative). Effective Nov 1 2025 the formula has five metrics: Active Playtime; Island Retention; Playtime Surrounding Item Shop V-Bucks Spend; User Acquisition Rewards: New Players; User Acquisition Rewards: Returning Players. Retention is now **island-specific rather than ecosystem-wide**. Only engagement from players who have ever made a real-money Fortnite purchase counts ("This doesn't reduce the total pool; it simply removes non-payer engagement from the calculation"). For a new player or one absent over six months, the creator receives 75% of that player's engagement-pool contributions for six months. Three details the skills must carry: (a) Epic's Sept 18 2025 announcement uses different labels for the same five metrics (Minutes Played, New User Acquisition, Lapsed User Acquisition, Playtime Surrounding V-Bucks Spend, Island Retention), so name-matching must accept both sets; (b) link attribution is **not supported for players in the European Union**, works for new players on PC only and returning players on PC + mobile, and Discover-sourced players have day-one credit split pro-rata across every island played — so UA-reward advice is inapplicable to EU audiences; (c) the pool is 40% of eligible net Item Shop/real-money revenue plus 50% of Sponsored Row revenue (100% through end of 2026).

**Failure mode:** applying standalone-app benchmarks to an experience, and designing for cohorts the platform does not pay for.

**Ethics context:** Roblox's age-checked DAU mix as of Jan 31 2026 was 35% under 13, 38% aged 13–17, 27% over 18. Mandatory age checks produced a mid-single-digit engagement headwind — **safety measures can cost engagement in the short term**, and that tradeoff should be stated rather than hidden.

### Premium PC / console

**Curve:** mobile day-N metrics are the wrong instrument, and the report that publishes both says so. GameAnalytics 2026 (3,582 PC games, ≥100 MAU, live 4+ months, 2025 calendar data): median D1 ~7% (P75 12–13%, P99 40–50%), D7 median 1.1–1.8% **[contested]** (1.7–1.8% in the PDF text), D30 ~0.2–0.25% with P25 at 0%, median DAU/MAU 4–5%, median playtime 32–33 min/day across ~18-minute sessions at 1.65–1.70 sessions/day. The report's own framing: "PC engagement is depth-driven, so mobile-style retention reads differently… success on PC shows up in session length, playtime and DAU/MAU." **[corrected]** two caveats: the HTML page rendered PC D7 median as 1.1–1.3% against the PDF's 1.7–1.8% (unresolved), and PC medians were revised downward between the January PDF (D1 ~9%, D30 ~0.3%) and the August 2026 page — date any PC figure to the version you read (https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-01-27-2026-mobile-pc-benchmarks_compressed.pdf).

**Return driver:** a content completion arc, then either a finished experience (where churn is the correct outcome) or a mastery/endgame ladder.

**Failure modes:**
1. *Treating finite completion as churn.* The same report notes that "games with lower DAU vs MAU but long sessions and high playtime often reflect binge-style or finite experiences."
2. *Smoothing early difficulty without shipping a veteran endgame.* Monster Hunter Wilds sold 10M in its first month but only 10.745M by Sept 30 2025, with just 160k units in Jul–Sep 2025 against 477k the prior quarter — while Monster Hunter World shipped over 1M per quarter for years and doubled its sales in the two years after an equivalent ~10.7M point. Director Tokuda's own letter concedes tempered-monster difficulty "may have felt lacking" for veterans and that endgame was "slightly skewed around Arkveld", pulling 9-star content forward to Ver. 1.021 (Capcom, first-party, 2025, https://www.monsterhunter.com/wilds/en-us/topics/letter-2508/). This is the **two-audience difficulty problem**: the newcomer fix and the veteran ladder are separate deliverables, and the veteran ladder must exist at launch.
3. *Missing the refund deadline.* On Steam a purchase is refundable within 14 days if played under 2 hours, and Early Access playtime counts (https://store.steampowered.com/steam_refunds). The first two hours are a commercial deadline, not just a design opportunity. **[unverified]** but directionally relevant: per-title PlayTracker medians for visual novels cluster near that 2-hour line with heavy mean/median skew (cited in the corpus counterevidence pool; no URL retrievable).

**Premium co-op ("friendslop") is a distinct sub-model.** Peak (Aggro Crab + Landfall) cost under $200,000 across roughly four months, launched June 16 2025 under $5 with a 38% launch discount, sold 1M in six days and 11M on Steam by October 2025; replay runs on a daily map rotation (10 AM PDT reset) that is **identical for every player**, so friends can compare runs (Game Developer, 2025, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/how-co-op-climbing-hit-peak-achieved-2-million-sales-for-less-than-200-000-). And the studio rejects the growth frame outright: "We're not going to continually have a graph go up… having an expectation of games continuously having to go up on that graph is what adds to your pressure." The measured cost of that model is real: friend co-op hits without meta-progression average ~3% D30 (Content Warning 3%, Ball x Pit 4.7%, Phasmophobia 5.3%) against 11.3% for Dead by Daylight — "fast fun, and fast churn" (AppMagic via GameDev Reports, 2026, https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/appmagic-friendslop-games-in-2025).

**Moment types:** timed input inside a slow genre — Clair Obscur's real-time dodge/parry, where creative director Guillaume Broche says "the timing is pretty important, but the payoff is always really rewarding", built to make turn-based "more engaging for people who don't necessarily like turn-based combat, while keeping the strategic core" (PlayStation Blog, 2024, https://blog.playstation.com/2024/07/29/clair-obscur-expedition-33-devs-discuss-classic-turn-based-rpg-inspiration-and-real-time-mechanics/); the shared daily seed; the clean, upsell-free first impression that LocalThunk treats as a design asset.

**Skill implication.** retention-strategy-designer must stop presenting a single genre-blind D1/D7/D30 target. Replace it with (a) a genre × quantile table that names its data year and edition, (b) per-genre session-shape expectations so that "increase session count" is never proposed to an idle or multiplayer product where the count is structurally capped, and (c) a per-genre *failure mode* line, because the failure modes above are more transferable than the successes. interaction-reward-moments gains five named patterns from this section that it currently lacks: deliberate information withholding, gated reveal (fixed-interval free reveal plus optional acceleration), timed input inside a slow genre, shared daily seed, and eudaimonic "being moved" beats. Two patterns must be routed through the ethics module rather than listed neutrally: near-miss framing coupled to escalating extra-move pricing, and alliance co-opetition as social pressure to spend.

## 3. Platform differences, and why mobile benchmarks must not cross over

| Axis | Mobile (F2P) | PC (Steam-shaped) | UGC experience |
|---|---|---|---|
| Median D1 | ~22% | ~7% | 4.3–11.46% by session band |
| Median D7 | just under 4% | 1.1–1.8% **[contested]** | 0.41–1.61% by band |
| Diagnostic metric | D1/D7/D30 | DAU/MAU, playtime, session len | Session band + platform algo |
| Commercial deadline | Store rating / first session | 2 hours / 14 days (refund) | Discovery placement |

Populations: mobile = GameAnalytics 2026, 16,262 live games with a 1,000-MAU floor, iOS+Android, a stated 9-region scope of which only 8 appear in the published regional table, 2025 calendar data **[corrected]** (the sample is 16,262, and the report's prose "P99 exceeds 45% in every region" is contradicted by its own table — Africa P99 D1 is 43.30%). PC = the same report, 3,582 games ≥100 MAU and 4+ months live; PC D7 is **[contested]** between the HTML page (1.1–1.3%) and the PDF text (1.7–1.8%), so date any PC figure to the version read (02b §3.2). UGC = GameAnalytics 2025 Roblox report, 47% of platform engagement.

Three structural reasons the numbers cannot be transported.

1. **Different denominators of intent.** A mobile install is often a browse; a PC purchase is a decision with money attached and a 2-hour refund clock. A Roblox "install" is a click inside a session the platform already owns. The same player behaviour produces different day-N values on each.
2. **Different success definitions.** GameAnalytics states outright that on PC, success shows up in session length, playtime and DAU/MAU, and that "high new-user inflow alone doesn't convert into lasting engagement". A finite premium game with low DAU/MAU and long sessions is healthy; the same shape on mobile F2P is a leaky bucket.
3. **Different clocks.** GameAnalytics uses UTC calendar days with strict return-on-day-N; Adjust uses rolling 24 hours from install. Adjust's marketing-attributed gaming figures are D1 27% / D7 13% / D30 5% — roughly 7× the GameAnalytics D30 median, on a completely different population (UA-attributed installs on Adjust-measured apps) (https://www.adjust.com/resources/guides/user-retention/).

Regional and national platform mixes then move again. Japan's game apps ran D1 26% / D7 11% / D30 4% in H1 2026 (Jan–May 2026), up from 24/9/3 in H1 2025, on Adjust's top-5,000-app aggregate (Adjust × Sensor Tower, https://sensortower.com/ja/blog/adjust-sensor-tower-2026). Korea shows the opposite of the mobile-first assumption: past-year game participation fell to 50.2% (−9.7pp YoY; 74.4% in 2022), while among players PC weekday playtime hit a five-year high of 117.9 minutes against mobile's 90.9 — a core consolidation, not a mobile expansion (KOCCA 2025 게임이용자 실태조사, https://welcon.kocca.kr/ko/info/business/1956858). **[corrected]** three sourcing points: the KOCCA press release's own sample wording ("최근 1년간 게임이용자 10,000명") should not be quoted literally — it is a general-population sample of 10,000 aged 10–69 with a user/non-user screen; the 14 Jul – 29 Aug 2025 fieldwork dates and the 74.4% 2022 base come from secondary coverage, not the press release; and non-user sub-figures have different bases (the "lack of time 44.0%" is among 3,828 lapsed players).

**Skill implication.** SKILL.md needs a hard branch before any number is quoted: *what platform, and which analytics clock?* If the answer is PC/console, the skill must report DAU/MAU, playtime and session length instead of D1/D7/D30, and must treat a finite game's low DAU/MAU as expected rather than as churn. If the answer is a UGC experience, the skill must use session-length bands and platform payout rules rather than app benchmarks. If the answer is Korea, the skill must state that **no public Korea-specific D1/D7/D30 benchmark exists** and instruct the team to build internal cohort baselines.

## 4. Meta-progression across sessions: what makes a run feel meaningful later

The corpus supports three classes of stored value, in ascending order of return inertia.

**1. Power carried forward.** Permanent upgrades (Hades II's aspects, hybrid-casual boosters). Softens the sting of a lost run and gives a reason to start the next one. Cost: it can trivialize later runs, and every nerf becomes a perceived confiscation — the Mega Crit review bomb is the failure case, and its remedy is expectation-setting ("no change is necessarily permanent"), not restraint from balancing.

**2. Knowledge carried forward.** Blue Prince and Slay the Spire 2 keep nothing but what the player learned. Design consequence: knowledge is invisible, so a segment reports "zero feeling of progression between runs" within days. The mitigation is to give mastery an *artifact* — a logbook, a discovered-recipe list, an unlocked map annotation — so the player can see the thing they now own. Blue Prince's own framing supports this: each day is a blank slate for drafting rooms under a step quota, "walking through your own boardgame".

**3. Position and collection carried forward.** A puzzle level number, a TCG collection, a gacha roster. This is the highest-inertia class because it cannot be re-derived by a returning player and it compounds — which is exactly why Puzzle and Card show the strongest D28 in the genre table despite unremarkable D1.

The strongest available comparison across these classes is the friendslop split: co-op hits without meta-progression at ~3–5.3% D30 versus Dead by Daylight at 11.3% (AppMagic estimates, 2026). **[contested]** — this is correlational and confounded (Dead by Daylight is far older, licensed, and continuously operated), so read it as an order-of-magnitude signal, not an effect size.

Then the counter-evidence that stops "add meta-progression" from becoming a reflex. Pokémon TCG Pocket had all three classes and still lost 45% of its MAU across three quarters; DeNA's diagnosis was login frequency and existing-user retention, and its remedy was to enrich the **collection experience** rather than the reveal loop. Meta-progression retains only while the player believes the accumulation is going somewhere.

Two more findings belong here.

*Social position is stored value too, and it is asymmetric.* A Cox survival analysis of 295,008 League of Legends players found champion (play-variety) diversity decreased churn while favouring preferred positions increased it, and that social connectedness generally reduced churn — but with a real caveat: "playing with teammates was associated with lower win rates, which may negatively influence player retention." Churn probability rose 8% after 60 days and 20% after 90 days of tenure (Kim, Kim, Kim & Li, *Sport, Business and Management* 16(4), 2026, https://www.emerald.com/sbm/article/16/4/453/1333130/Analyzing-player-churn-in-esports-games-a-survival). So "social features always raise retention" is not a claim the skills may make.

*Returning is not resuming, and almost no game handles it.* A CHI 2021 autobiographical-design study of returns to long-term single-player games found that "game systems do not recognize that the player is returning to rather than continuing gameplay uninterrupted", and identifies a "Pivot Point" — a moment that reminds the player of their expert self — as the anchor for return flows (Hammad et al., https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3411764.3445357). The only causal-style practitioner evidence for recaps is Telltale's: "Clementine will remember that" was added because six-to-eight-week gaps between episodes made the team fear players would forget their choices carried. HoYoverse's catch-up entry — enter the current season without completing prior chapters — is the productized version of the same idea.

**Validation warning.** Supercell's own Squad Busters postmortem is the clearest statement in the corpus that meta-progression cannot be validated by an FTUE-stage test: beta D7 of 29% then 38% (D1 61%, D3 44%) with 140,000+ testers, 40M pre-registrations and 75M installs, and post-launch retention still "didn't match what we saw in closed betas". Paananen's lessons: "Even large beta samples don't predict global behavior. You cannot draw reliable conclusions even from 100,000+ players", and "Don't skip testing meta and long term retention" — the one-month soft launch "only validated D7" (Supercell, first-party, 2026, https://supercell.com/en/news/the-best-games-havent-been-made-yet).

**Skill implication.** retention-strategy-designer should ask "what is stored between sessions?" as a first-class diagnostic and classify the answer as power / knowledge / position-collection / social position / nothing. For knowledge-only designs it should recommend an artifact that makes mastery visible. For every design it should require that meta and long-term retention be validated on their own timescale, and explicitly warn that a large beta validates onboarding only. It should drop any blanket claim that social features raise retention, and add a return-flow checklist (recognize the returning player, restore context, deliver a Pivot Point, offer catch-up entry).

## 5. Content cadence and the content cliff

The content cliff is not a calendar date. It is the point at which the cumulative content consumed by your **fastest** cohort exceeds the cumulative content you have shipped. Three of this document's titles hit it and documented the crash.

- **Arc Raiders** hit it at the level-75 cap: "The players wanted more… the team just couldn't keep pace." The response was structural — fewer, larger updates, because a ~120-person studio "cannot compete on content quantity" and "what we want to do is spend more time building quality stuff."
- **Monster Hunter Wilds** hit it because the endgame ladder was thin and skewed to one monster; the fix (9-star tempered monsters at HR100+, random-bonus talismans, per-monster reward balancing, finer parameter customization in TU2) arrived months after the sales legs were already gone.
- **Zenless Zone Zero** shows the shape of the trough in miniature: the v2.0 spend spike held above pre-update levels for **10 days**, on a 42-day version cycle. The other ~32 days are what the cadence design exists to cover.

Observed cadence parameters, by genre:

| Genre | Cadence unit | Sub-beat |
|---|---|---|
| Gacha RPG | 42-day version | Two 21-day banner phases |
| Hero shooter | 8–9 week season | Hero ~monthly; balance each half |
| Extraction | Larger, rarer updates | Sized to team capacity |
| Episodic narrative | 2 episodes/week × 4 weeks | Season complete before launch |
| Roguelite | 18-month Early Access | Then 1.0 |

The episodic row deserves its numbers: Dispatch released a finished 8-episode season two episodes every Wednesday from Oct 22 to Nov 12 2025, and Steam peak concurrents rose with each drop — ~13,000 → 66,000 → 131,000 → 220,060 — passing 1M copies before the finale, with the studio tuning "invisible relationship meters" between drops after finding that choices they expected to split were "more one-sided" (Game Developer, 2025, https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/dispatch-s-episodic-format-alongside-sharp-storytelling-is-key-to-its-success). **[contested]** as a general rule: the case is confounded by a celebrity cast and streamer effect, and separately, a 2025 Marketing Science field trial found drip release's benefit "diminished for extreme binge watchers". Cadence is graduated by audience and lifecycle stage, not binary.

**Cheap cliff mitigations that shipped and worked:**

- *Rotation instead of production.* Peak's daily map rotation gives every player the same seed at the same reset — new content every day, zero new content produced.
- *Bank the missed days.* Marvel Snap's 30-day non-resetting Bonus Challenge (50 missions, fed by the same daily missions as the weekly challenge) exists so players who "miss a couple of days… still have the opportunity to earn everything" inside a 35-day window (https://marvelsnap.com/patch-notes-september-16-2025/).
- *Roadmap co-ownership.* Schedule I's solo developer let the community vote on the first major update while stating openly that 1.0 was "two to three years away" — retention through honesty rather than through a scarcity calendar the team cannot sustain.
- *Catch-up entry.* Let new and returning players enter the current season without clearing the backlog (Honkai: Star Rail).

**And a target worth borrowing because it is published.** Tencent's audited 2025 annual results define an evergreen game (长青游戏) as a mobile game with quarterly-average DAU over 5M — or a PC game over 2M — **and** annual gross receipts over RMB 4B (https://static.www.tencent.com/uploads/2026/03/18/559e5d480a4411165e6c7367d61fefbd.pdf). It is one of the few explicit, dated, first-party definitions of "sustained" in the corpus.

**Skill implication.** The lifecycle skill should compute a content-cliff estimate rather than recommending a cadence: consumption rate of the top decile × content shipped per cycle = the date the cliff arrives, and the end-cap loop must exist at launch, not in a title update. Cadence recommendations must be paired with a capacity check ("can this team hold quality at this interval?"), and the skill must state that cadence compression has no verified retention effect — Marvel Rivals compressed and still decayed ~75%. Rotation, banking, co-ownership and catch-up entry should be offered as the low-cost tier before any new-content proposal.

## 6. What to ask about genre before any retention diagnosis

Ask these before quoting a single benchmark. Each one changes which numbers are admissible.

1. **Platform and business model?** F2P mobile, premium PC/console, or a UGC experience. This selects the benchmark family and the diagnostic metric (§3).
2. **Which analytics clock?** Calendar day with strict return-on-day-N, or rolling 24 hours; any-session or a critical return event. Two teams with identical products can report D1 43% and D1 32%.
3. **What is the intended return cadence?** Twice daily (Pokémon TCG Pocket's 12-hour stamina), daily, weekly, per-content-drop, or finite. Stickiness targets follow the intended cadence; they do not define it.
4. **What is stored between sessions?** Power, knowledge, position/collection, social position, or nothing (§4).
5. **Who sets the appointment — the player or the server?** An energy timer is player-set and forgiving; an alliance duel is server-set and coercive. The ethics review differs accordingly.
6. **Where does content come from?** Production, rotation, procedural generation, or players. This determines whether the content cliff is a scheduling problem or a structural one.
7. **Does the acquisition creative match the loop?** A mismatch is a known D1 penalty and, at Last War's scale, a permanent dependence on UA.
8. **Does the endgame exist at launch, and for whom?** The two-audience difficulty problem is a launch-scope question, not a live-ops one.
9. **What is the audience contract?** Cozy/relaxing (tightening is a brand-risk event), competitive (rank reset is the clock), completionist (finishing is success). 84% of puzzle players want relaxation even inside heavy live-ops **[unverified]** (§2).
10. **Which probabilistic systems ship where?** Any 확률형 아이템, pack or box shipping to Korea inherits §33-2's burden shift and a discretionary award of up to 3x the court-estimated amount (02h §5.1).
11. **Is decay the correct outcome?** For premium, finite and co-op titles, yes — and Peak's team says so explicitly. Do not apply an F2P D30/LTV frame to a product whose success is a completed experience.
12. **Which cohort does the platform actually pay you for?** On UEFN, only players who have ever made a real-money Fortnite purchase count toward engagement payouts, and EU players cannot carry link attribution at all.

**Skill implication.** These twelve questions are the genre selector. They belong in SKILL.md verbatim, because they are stable; the numbers they route to are not. The advisor should refuse to give a retention target until questions 1, 2 and 3 are answered, and should say why rather than guessing.
