# Relief, Recovery and Re-entry Patterns

Read when the scene is a failure, a near-loss, a comeback, a broken streak, or a 복귀 유저 opening the app after a gap.

## Family bound

Relief may never be the thing sold. The one forbidden configuration is **engineered depletion sold back as relief** (T3 metered-access, T3 paid streak freeze). Test per beat: *if the relief were free and always available, would this moment still be worth designing?* If no, it is a purchase prompt in a recovery costume. Compliant floor for every pattern below: an equal free or earned repair at the same cadence, no purchase surface inside the loss window, no refill clock tuned to a return-cadence KPI, never sold to store-signalled minor accounts, never loss-framed copy.

## 1. Clutch recovery

The save must be *caused*, not granted: success-dependent amplified feedback raised competence, curiosity and effectance, while amplification not tied to success **lowered** effectance (−.19) and competence (−.43) [Kao et al., CHI 2024 | pre-registered, n=1,699, purpose-built action RPG].
- **Bound** — a paid revive at the instant of loss is this family failing.
- **Measure** — recovery rate from the near-failure state against a pre-shipped baseline. Never optimise "urge to keep playing"; its own authors frame it as a risk marker [Larche & Dixon, 2020 | n=60, Candy Crush | flow added 21.8% of variance in urge].

## 2. Comeback beat (at-risk difficulty relief)

Lowering difficulty for at-risk players raises day-N return at a measured cost to in-round purchases [Ascarza, Netzer & Runge, IJRM 2025 | ~330k users, 12-week randomized rollout, F2P mobile puzzle | day-N return] — effect sizes, the net-revenue read and the rollout design live in `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../retention-strategy-designer/references/experiments.md`. It is churn-risk relief for a named cohort, not flow tuning.
- **Bound** — silent and unpriced; announced at the failure screen as a purchasable easy path, it inverts.
- **Measure** — holdout on the at-risk segment only; the mandatory guardrail is in-round purchase rate.

## 3. Failure-state readability

Every loss, before any retry offer: one line naming a cause the player could have acted on, plus the gradation (how close, on what axis) instead of a binary fail. Legible success gradations, exposed learning progress and expectations set *before* the attempt replace difficulty tuning as the lever ["Leveling up fun", Scientific Reports 2025 | game datasets + 2 experiments] **[unverified — full text not opened]**.
- **Bound** — never manufacture a near-miss on a loss that was not near: the effect is **[contested]** and what it reliably produces is a faster, larger next attempt rather than durable retention — evidence, populations and the Palmer-vs-Pisklak disagreement in `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../interaction-reward-moments/references/research-basis.md`.

## 4. Forgiveness mechanics

Decide what a miss destroys before the miss: accrual pauses and earned artifacts are never erased; a **minimum-viable action** satisfies the period (one line, one mood tap, one round) so the all-or-nothing cliff never forms; the period is flexible ("4 of 7 days", a cumulative visit count), never a consecutive chain. All-or-nothing thinkers choose complete inaction over a reduced version [Segar, 2025 | n=27, qualitative, exercise] **[contested]** — small-n qualitative only, and Silverman & Barasch argue for keeping streaks.
- **Rating price (T2b)** — rewarding return is PEGI 7; punishing non-return by "losing content or reducing progress" is **PEGI 12 from June 2026**; EU DSA Art. 28 guidelines (2025-07-14) put streaks default-off for minors.

## 5. Streak repair

Highlighting an *intact* streak raises re-engagement while surfacing a *broken* one lowers it at identical underlying behaviour, and offering repair blunts the effect [Silverman & Barasch, JCR 2023 | 7 studies, >4,000 participants incl. language-app field data] — the effect sizes and the responsibility moderator are in `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../retention-strategy-designer/references/retention-playbook.md`. Published freeze effects are small [Duolingo research blog, 2022 data | vendor A/B]; the figures and the tier row they sit under are in `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engagement-retention-advisor/references/domain-ethics.md` (learning section).
- **Two testable rules** — *break-spotlight*: no surface displays or animates a broken streak (break a test account, inventory 72h of its messages). *Loss-copy*: no notification whose subject is expiry.
- **Never quote** — "7-day streak → 3.6× completion" (selection, not lift), "streak freeze cut churn 21%" (a corrupted program-level CURR figure), Trophy's 17.2-vs-11.6 freeze days (vendor self-data).

## 6. Re-entry after a lapse

Returning is not resuming: "game systems do not recognize that the player is returning to rather than continuing gameplay uninterrupted" [Hammad et al., CHI 2021 | autobiographical design study]. Beat order: recognise the return → spoiler-safe recap restating the player's *own* prior choices → **Pivot Point**, a scene that reminds them of their expert self → catch-up entry into the current season without clearing the backlog. The one documented resurrection form is restoring lost *status* for a small bounded effort: a June 2026 one-time event restored longest-ever streaks for three lessons, at a vendor-reported scale of millions [Duolingo Q2 2026 shareholder letter | 2026 data | Duolingo learners | vendor-reported, no holdout described] — the counts and the retention claim attached to them are in `${CLAUDE_SKILL_DIR}/../engagement-retention-advisor/references/domain-ethics.md` (learning section).
- **Bound** — recap and catch-up entry are free; a re-entry flow whose first interactive element is an offer makes the lapse itself the product.
